# Magic House (`magic-house/`)

A house of rooms nobody has seen before. One to four children at one screen walk into a room, work out what it wants and do it, in three to five minutes. Then come fireworks, a present to wear for each of them, a dressing room and the next door. Opus designs each room as code while the children play the one before. Every room made is kept, so the house grows and old rooms can be played again. The brief is Sami's [`design.md`](design.md); how to run it is in [`README.md`](README.md).

Every step of the first plan is built, and the rework "every room a room" that Sami asked for after playing the first rooms (2026-11-22) with it. This is what stands: the decisions, the contract between the game and the model, and what is open. Numbers are measured unless they say otherwise. Any decision here can be overturned with a reason; whoever does it writes the new one in its place. It lies beside the brief because Sami asked for that (2026-11-21); `.cursor/rules/plans.md` otherwise puts plans in `plans/`. Code comments name the steps that built things: 1 the contracts, 2 the sim, the scene, the seats and the own rooms, 3 the bots and the trial, 4 the server and the line, 5 the house round the rooms, 6 sounds, patterns and the view from high up, 7a every room a room, 7 the starting library.

**Whoever works on the game next reads the log first** (`library/log.jsonl` and `line.jsonl`, git-ignored): every room asked for in play is logged with its tries, failures, seconds, tokens and cost, and every play with how it went.

  - Followed as patterns, not shared: its store (`server/store.ts`), the library the build ships (`brain/shipped.ts`), and the worker that tries new code and is killed when it hangs (`brain/validate.ts`; `brain/workerWindow.ts` says why a worker's imports must stay clear of `.tsx`).

It is another repo, so its ideas and wording are taken, not its files.

## A visit

1. **Who plays.** The game opens as the picked player. The others join from the device's players, shown by their gallery faces, up to four. Each takes a seat by pressing on it.
2. **The house.** A door for every kept room made for a party of this size, and one glowing door for a new room. While the new room is late, maths and reading quizzes fill the wait.
3. **A room.** One screen, the whole room in sight, the camera still. Nothing is told at the door. The children look, try and find out. Dots at the top fill as the steps are done.
4. **The end.** The way out opens, fireworks go up, and each player opens a present.
5. **The dressing room.** Each player has a column of the screen: their own person, what they own, and the colours and patterns to wear it in. When all are ready they are back in the house.

## Where the plan reads the brief its own way

- **"figure out the task"**: nothing is told at the door. The room shows what it wants, and a helper comes when the children are stuck or ask.
- **"collaboration would be best"**: a room is made for exactly the party in front of it, and a room played together needs every one of them. Fewer could not clear it.
- **"competitions, or just parallel work"**: the game rolls which kind a room is, mostly together. Everyone gets a present whoever wins.
- **"a random (designed by opus) apparel ... from a predefined set"**: Opus models the thing for this room. The colours and patterns it is worn in are the game's own set.
- **"keyboard and mouse, and ... a tablet"**: a seat is a stick and two buttons, from keys, a pad on the touch screen or a gamepad. Pointing with a finger or the mouse belongs to the room and comes from anyone. A screen with only touch seats two.
- **"some rooms may be 3d"**: one world seen two ways, from the side or from high up.
- **"a free to use library of sounds"**: synthesised sounds, and recordings from CC0 packs that `npm install` fetches.
- **"tune the difficulty"**: from the first room on, how the children played goes into the next room Opus designs. Nothing waits for a batch. Every room also has three levels of its own, which move at once.
- **"3-5 minutes"**: the bots' clean pass takes at least a minute, and Opus is told how long these children really took.

## Decisions

### Where things run

- **The dev server asks and keeps; the page plays and proves.** A Vite plugin like Silly Billy's serves `./api/`: it asks Opus through the Claude Code CLI (no tools, an empty folder, structured output), guards the answer as text and keeps the library on disk. It never runs made code. The page compiles guarded code behind the sandbox and plays it, and tries a new room and its look first in a Web Worker it can kill. The hosted build, and a laptop with no CLI, invent nothing: the new door there opens a kept room of their size that these players have not played.
- **Tests do run kept rooms in Node**, behind the guard (Sami, 2026-11-21). A kept room is a whole game, and an engine change that breaks one must turn a test red, not ship a dead room. `trial/library.test.ts` holds every kept room to its file's bot seconds and checks every present.

### A room is code

- **Code, not parts to combine**, as in both earlier games. The room is the body of `function (r, w)`: it runs once, builds the place for its party at the level asked, and hands over what runs each step. It keeps what it remembers in its own variables; what the engine hands it is frozen.
- **A room is made for one party size** (Sami, 2026-11-21): the size of the party that asked for it. A room played together needs every one of them. The room's file holds the size, and the house offers the room only to a party of that size.
- **The building blocks are general**: a solid, a mover, a zone, a loose thing, a player's two buttons. Never a ready-made puzzle part: a ready-made button and door would make every room a button and a door. The one fixture is the way out.
- **The engine owns the feel and is the referee.** Walking, jumping, carrying, bonks, coming back after a fall, the camera, the helper and the end are the game's own, tuned by hand once, with their own sounds and puffs. Whatever the code says: nobody is hurt or out, nobody is kept from playing for more than a couple of seconds, a loose thing that leaves the room comes home, and a room can only be finished or left, never lost.
- **One seed, and weight that is counted.** Room code gets a `Math` whose `random` is the world's seeded one, and no `Date`. Each room has a seed of its own and always plays on it. A room's weight is counted (loop turns, calls into the engine, things built), never timed, so a room passes or fails the same way on the laptop, on the tablet and in the image build. Real time is only the backstop that stops a hung worker. Made code's `Math` is the engine's for every transcendental function (fdlibm's, within 2 ulps of V8, and the rest built from them), so V8 and JavaScriptCore agree to the bit; `random` throws inside a mover's place, which the search asks about times to come.
- **Three ways to move a thing.** A mover's place is a function of time alone, and the trial checks that the same time gives the same place. Anything may be sent somewhere by an engine call (slide there in two seconds, open, close), which the bots can see coming. Or the room's code moves it step by step, which they cannot foresee.
- **Each answer also says what it lacked** (`wish`): what the engine could not do for this room. That log, not guesses, decides what the engine learns next.

### Every room a room

Sami played the first rooms and said (2026-11-22): the room fills the screen and is no bigger, so everything is visible all the time; the rooms should be rooms, with a theme, and look like rooms; more things to interact with and nicer decor; more light, and a space room could be darker with star lights.

- **One size, which is the engine's** (`ROOM` in `engine/api.ts`). From the side the room is x -8 to 8 and y 0 to 8, the back wall at z -3 and the open front at z 1.5. From high up the floor is x -7 to 7 and z -4 to 4, and players are held under y 3. A room that calls the old `r.bounds` or `r.sky` fails at build (`MISTAKES.houseOwns`); something built outside the room fails (`MISTAKES.outside`).
- **The camera shows the whole room and never moves** except for `w.shake`: `SIDE_FRAME` (16.6 by 8.8 m) and `HIGH_FRAME` (14.6 by 8.6 m of floor). A kid is 59 px tall on a 1024 by 768 tablet with its pads, 89 px at 1280 by 800 and 100 px at 1440 by 900; from high up 32 to 37 px on the tablet with its pads. THE TRIAL says the seconds come from what there is to do, never from distance ("the room's 16 m in 4 s walking").
- **The house draws the room** round every room before any code runs, in the room's `shell` ({ walls, floor, ceiling, trim, light }, each paint a colour with a pattern and a second colour), so a room whose look failed is still a themed room. From the side: back wall, floor, side walls in perspective, a ceiling with a beam, skirting and cornice, an open front, an upper storey in timber, and the house's frame 6 m past the room so no background shows. From high up: a patterned floor with walls 2.5 m high at the back and both sides, no ceiling. All of it merges into the room's still mesh at no draw call. The way out is a door in the back wall (from high up, the nearest wall). A solid the room gives no colour takes the floor's colour and is dressable by the look when it has no model, handler, glow, surface or mover of its own.
- **Light.** `shell.light` runs from 0.6 to 1; below 0.8 is dim. The key light is 2.1 x light from the front above left, the fill 0.6 + 0.6 x light: 3.3 at light 1, 2.22 at 0.6 (67%). The 4 strongest lamps are real lights, at most 12 lamps with the look's, the rest halos. A dim room is helped: a soft halo in each seat's colour, play solids' tops 10% lighter, a faint halo on anything with `use`. `r.lights` gives up to 400 small twinkling lights in one draw call, stronger the dimmer the room. A wall pattern's second colour more than `SHELL_CALM.most` (0.12 in Oklab) from the first is drawn toward it, so walls stay a calm background.
- **No trap.** `trapIn` in `trial/trial.ts` builds the bots' graph over the room at each level and finds, for each player whose stick walks, where they can get by walking, jumping, dropping or riding. A place is a trap when from it the player can get neither back to their start, nor to anything that helps (a solid, thing or zone that answers, a thing, a moving or hidden solid, within a jump), nor to a place the play needs that the start gets to (a step's place, or a zone). `MISTAKES.trapped` names the place, the start and the first needed place lost. It costs a few milliseconds a level. False passes are preferred to false traps.
- **What was cut, and why.** Rooms bigger than a screen and the die for them; the sky, the bounds and the outdoors (the first seven kept rooms were outdoor places, so they were deleted; their presents stay). Look toys, bodies only the scene would move: the trial would never see them, and the game's own loose things give things to kick and carry. A consolidating call on the critical path, Sami's shape (an idea call, game and look side by side, a consolidator that rewrites the whole room): a room would be ready after about 160 to 210 s for $0.33 to $0.71 more a first pass, and its fixes would work on code a third hand rewrote. It is offered as FIT instead (Open).

### Proof before play

A room that cannot be finished is the worst thing this game can do to a child, and code that merely runs proves nothing about that. So bots play every room, only to show that it can be cleared and how long a clean pass takes (Sami, 2026-11-21). How hard a room is for children is learned from the children.

- **Every room comes with its own walkthrough**: the body of `function* (bots, w)`, which plays the room to its end with one bot a player. It is written in what the bots do, not in key presses: go there, take that, press this, wait until. The designer knows its room and plans the route; the engine finds the presses. The walkthrough also times the room, and `?autoplay` shows any room being played. A press holds the button 0.1 s; goTo arrives standing still; carry picks a thing up only where grab would choose it.
- **`goTo` is a search over the bot's own body** (`trial/search.ts`, `trial/graph.ts`; from high up `trial/highSearch.ts`, `trial/highGraph.ts`). A room's memory lives in its own code and cannot be copied, so the search copies only the bot (plain numbers) and holds the room as it stands: solids, movers by their clocks, engine moves under way, zones' pulls, loose things and other players. A beam tried narrow first (4 wide, then 12, then 32), deciding every 0.15 s, scored on a floor graph of spots every 0.25 m. It plans again when the room changes under it, and when it gives up it says where and why in metres ("no way from 8, 2 to the ledge at 12, 4: the gap is 4.2 m and a jump reaches 3.1"). What it cannot do: foresee the room's code (the walkthrough waits with `w.until` or sets the stick itself), follow a body the code moves every frame, plan more than 30 s of moving at once, or plan bots together (pushers in `w.together` add their weights).
- **The trial** (`trial/trial.ts`) is a pure function, run in the worker for a new room and in vitest for kept ones. The scan, the compile, the build within its counted weight at every level, every player started on a floor, no trap, 30 s of standing still that harms nobody and ends nothing, 60 s of random pressing and tapping that breaks nothing, then the walkthrough, which must finish the room with each step turning done in order in 60 to 120 s (`PASS`; a room that ends by its own clock, 3 to 5 min). It plays only the room's own party size (Sami, 2026-11-21), at each level, and the party's own level on a second seed (the room's seed plus 2) to catch a pass by luck. In a room played together it plays the walkthrough once with each bot standing still, and the room must stay unfinished: every player has a part in the designer's own way through, though it cannot show that no other way exists. Levels that build alike and finish alike fail (`levelsAlike`). A pass is cut at 150 s. A room is kept when it passes at all three levels.
- **The trial runs on the game's clock, not the wall's** (Sami, 2026-11-21). Minutes of play are tried in seconds, a contest's clock with them. The whole trial has a real-time budget (`TRIAL.realSecs`, 60 s; most rooms take 1 to 11 s), and the page kills the worker at 90 s, a presents' check at 20 s. A page keeps its frame time while its worker tries a room.
- **Made code's walls** (`trial/compile.ts`). Its own Math, `Date` undefined, a quiet console, and every other built-in behind a proxy that refuses every change, so one trial cannot spoil the next. Budgets count turns: the walkthrough 20,000 between two yields, a present 100,000 a build.
- **What fails goes back as plain sentences**, with the numbers, the designer's own line, its answer, and a survey of the room as it was built (where its floors, gaps and things really are), because the designer wrote the walkthrough without seeing the room. A fix answers only the part that changes, the room or the walkthrough, and hears every earlier try's failure, so it does not undo the last mend. Three tries in all, as Dissertation's arenas get; then the room is dropped and logged. A pass too short or too long says when each step turned done and asks for about `PASS.aimSecs` (90) s of play; too short, for about so many times as much for the players to do.
- **Standing is not play.** The trial cannot tell a padding wait from one the room asks for, so it does not fail one; the reference says instead that the seconds are the room's own play and that a minute made of standing, holding or waiting is not play: a room with less to do needs more for the players to do, never longer to stand, hold or wait, whether its code or its walkthrough asks it. It speaks of the minute, not of holding as such: one player holding or standing on something while another goes is a part to play in a room played together. The trial's report and the log give the seconds of a pass in which every bot stood idle (`waitSecs`: stick at rest, no button down, no tap, in `w.wait`, `w.until` or a `goTo` alike), so standing that the room's own code asks for shows as well as a walkthrough's wait; the own rooms stand idle under a tenth of their pass (`rooms.test.ts`).
- **The twins** (`trial/twins/`) prove that the trial names what is wrong. `base.ts` is Pirate Sails written again as made code, held equal to the original at every level; 31 side twins (a ledge too far, a step that never turns done, a lock with no key, an endless loop, a throw, a build too heavy, a trap and more) and 4 from high up (on Egg Islands, `highBase.ts`) each fail with exactly their sentences. The look has its own twins (`looks.ts`).

### How Opus is asked

- **Dissertation's rule**: be general, never list what a room may or may not be, and give no example, because an example pulls every answer toward itself. The API reference is the only list, and it names building blocks. Examples in this plan, in the brief and in chat stay out of every prompt, and the game's own rooms and looks are never shown to the model.
- **The reference is the prompt, and it is true.** Each call's line of reference text lives beside the call. A test fails when the engine does not do what a line says, and when the builder has a call no line names. From Dissertation: say that handles are frozen, say what comes back empty at a limit, give every axis and sign with the thing itself and ask for no convention the model must guess, give a build one budget for all it builds and say "build lighter" when it overruns. Every read of a handle is a call, and a misread comes back as a sentence that names the right way.
- **New by being unlike the old.** Each answer starts with a few words for its theme and a line for what the players do. The request lists up to 60 earlier rooms of the library and the line, newest first, dropped ones too (asked again after a drop, the same request got the same idea back), as `- Name: theme; task (walls #c8e6ff stars, floor #8a5a3a stripes, light 0.7)`, and asks for a room unlike all of them in its theme, its colours and what the players do. The line logs a `drift` when 3 of the last 10 share a theme's head word or wall hues within 20 degrees, for whoever reads the log, never told to the model.
- **A model cannot roll dice, so the game does**, and says the result in a plain sentence: together, against each other or side by side (3, 1 and 1 in 5; alone, always together); from the side or from high up (4 in 5 from the side). The server checks the roll against the same odds.
- **The idea before the code.** The fields come in this order: the theme, what the players do, the name in both languages, the shell, the room, the walkthrough, the wish. The schema is built from the types, as in Silly Billy's `prompts.ts`.
- **About children, as principles**: a child who cannot read sees what to do from the room itself; one clear idea with a start, a middle and an end; in a room played together everyone is needed; each button does one thing all room long; nothing scary; one theme a child names at a glance; the whole room in sight; the things of the play answer a child who tries them early; bright rooms, and a dim theme shines with lights of its own. Nothing about the children themselves goes to the model: no names, only the count, an age of about seven, what they play on and how the last rooms went.
- **Principles, not cases** (Silly Billy's rule): when a room fails or bores, change what the engine can do in general or what the reference says in general. Never add a rule for one room. When a natural reading of the reference is a trap, the engine is made to answer it rather than the line made to forbid it: a mover's build is handed the builder, `build(r)`, because a model wrote it the way a kit model takes `(m)`.
- **The kit's lines are Magic House's own**, typed against Silly Billy's `Kit` and not shared: Silly Billy's kit text has examples and anchors this game does not want, and changing it would change Silly Billy's prompts.
- **Opus at `--effort low`**, as both earlier games run it.
- **The ask by hand** (2026-11-22, before any room could run) taught three things that stand. Short pulled the room small, so the prompt asks for plain code that works the first time and leaves the room's size to its play. A walkthrough padded fifteen seconds of play to a minute with `w.wait`, so the request's last line says what the minute is: "there is a minute or two of play in it even for players who know the way", never a minute a walkthrough could fill by waiting. Colours come as the model writes them (`'#6b3'`) and go through the kit's `parseColor`.
- **The prompts.** The room's system prompt is the reference in order (the children's rules, the room's preamble and reference with the kit's lines once, the walkthrough's, the answer's fields), 44,595 characters, opening on a room of the house, indoors, round a theme, which another designer then dresses. The look's is 10,427, the presents' 4,937. A request is about 400 characters with no earlier rooms and 1,400 with seven; a fix 5,500 to 12,600 (the request, the answer with its lines numbered, every earlier try's sentences, the last try's survey). A test holds that no text a model reads names the sky, a bigger room, zoom or follow.

```
Build a new room, unlike every one of these in its theme, its colours and what the players do: - <name>: <theme>; <what they do> (walls ..., floor ..., light ...)
Three players of about seven play it together, on a touch screen and a gamepad, and it needs all three. It is seen from the side.
How they played before: <name> (the bots took 70 s): finished in 6 min 10 s with 2 restarts and 3 hints. They loved <name>.
They should need three to five minutes: there is a minute or two of play in it even for players who know the way.
```

- **When rooms fail**, the levers in order, each by principle and with its test. For a room: `medium` effort on the same asks; a plainer reference; the walkthrough written in a second call shown the survey of the room as built; a simpler `goTo`. For "too short": the fix's "about N times as much for the players to do", then the line that the seconds come from doing, then Sami is asked about `PASS.minSecs`, which is never changed silently. For the look: if more than 1 in 5 rooms lose their look, an idle re-dress of rooms kept without one; if the look call's median passes 70 s, a shorter `LOOK_SYSTEM` or not waiting for it at the door. Three drops in a row of a party size stop the asking for that size in a visit (`DROPS_STOP`), and three in a row in a library round stop the round, with a word to Sami.

### One room ahead

- **The server holds the line of rooms** (`server/line.ts`, kept in `line.jsonl`): asked, being written, waiting for its trial, being fixed, kept or dropped, with its look beside it. Any open page takes the next room that waits for a trial or a look check, tries it and reports, so a reload loses nothing. One CLI process an answer, called at once, no pool; one room in the making per party size. A fix is a fresh call on the same system prompt, so the cache serves it. A CLI that gives no answer is called again, at most twice in a try, and spends no try. Timeouts: the room 8 min, a fix 6, the look 6, its fix 3, the presents 4. With no CLI a failed room waits in fixing. One dev server holds a library at a time (`server.lock`); another reads it and refuses the line. Seeds are drawn by the server; an id is the name's first four words and a number. Each ask logs a hash of its system prompt (kept once in `prompts.jsonl`) and its tokens and cost (`server/metered.ts`).
- **One unplayed room is kept ready** for the party's size. When the party walks into it, the next is asked for, with how they played every room before it. So Opus designs while the children play and dress. One design is spent for each new room played, and none while old rooms are replayed. More rooms ahead would only make Opus hear later how the children did.
- **Getting a room ready in time comes first** (Sami, 2026-11-21). Opus's answer is the long part: the prompt asks for plain code that works the first time, a fix answers only what changes, the look is a call of its own beside the trial, and the presents wait until the room is kept. The trial adds only seconds.
- **The door.** It opens the newest kept room of the party's size not played this visit (played means walked into; `?room` counts). The next room is asked for whenever none of their size is ready or in the making. A room kept while its look is still being made waits for it at most `LOOK_WAIT_MS` (60 s), then opens in its shell. The bar and the drops go by the server's clock (`Line.now`): the tablet's is not the laptop's. A room the bots play (`?autoplay`) is neither logged as play nor told to Opus. A kept room's file is fetched and compiled only when its door opens.
- **A late room is waited for with quizzes** (Sami, 2026-11-21). When a design with its fixes still takes longer than the children's play and dressing, or a party of a new size has no room yet, the new door shows a bar against the time a build nearly always takes (the 95th percentile of the last builds kept, three minutes until there are five, as in Dissertation); a room asked again after a drop fills the bar on from where it stood. Meanwhile each player gets a column of the screen with maths and reading questions at their own level and in their own language, answered with their seat or a tap. A right answer is a star; a wrong one shows the right one, and nothing is lost. When the room is ready, the door opens after the question in hand (every column answered, or 12 s). The kept rooms stay one tap away; when the wait gives up, the house says for 5 s that no new room is coming.
- **Sums by code, words by objects.** Maths questions are made by code, so they are always right: 10 rungs from counting 1 to 3 to whole tens within 100, the answer and two near neighbours. Reading matches words to objects (Sami, 2026-11-21) in 5 rungs by the letters of the player's own language: an object shown and its word picked from three, or a word and its object. The objects are Silly Billy's built-in things by import (`silly-billy/src/content/items/`) and the presents this house has made. Each subject moves alone: 3 right in a row up a rung, 2 wrong down, kept in the player's saves. Nothing is said aloud, since four columns answer at once.
- **Presents are a second, smaller call** made after the room has passed: exactly four, no fix round, a refused one dropped. Nobody needs them until the room is finished, so they never slow a room down.

### The engine and the seats

- **Its own pure sim, no physics library**: plain numbers, no three.js, a fixed step, as the repo's rule asks. Boxes, ramps, balls, movers that may turn, loose things that stack a little, zones. The search needs bodies it can copy, a trial must play again exactly, and arcade rules are what the brief's own games are made of. What is given up is real joints, ropes and tall stacks; Rapier, which Dissertation uses, is the way back if the wishes ask for them often. A clone of the World is the whole state.
- **One world in three dimensions.** A room seen from the side keeps every body in one plane (a body's z is set to 0, never a failure), with decor in front and behind. A room seen from high up frees the floor: x and z, y up, a turn round y counterclockwise from above; pushers count whole within 45 degrees of the push's way. The same bodies, zones and movers serve both.
- **The feel**, tuned by hand once (`FEEL`). A child is 1.15 m tall. In the air the stick moves a player 3.4 m/s, so the best running jump clears 3.1 m and not 3.2 (from high up 3 m any way across the floor, given room to run up). Coyote time 0.06 s and a kept press 0.15 s (Pia Jump's 0.12 s coyote would break the jump across); the short hop is exact. Feet that stand are 0.24 m wide; a player steps up onto solids but only 5 cm onto loose things, so crates are pushed, not climbed. A body overlapping something at the start of its move ignores it for that move, so nobody is ever stuck. Riders form a tree and ride as one. A carried thing sits on the head. A thing thinner than 0.4 m is grown to it.
- **Players walk through each other and can stand on each other's heads.** Nobody is shoved off a ledge by a sibling, and a boost up is teamwork. A contest may switch pushing on.
- **The freedom rule**: a bonked player cannot be bonked again for 2 s, and a player held still by bonks, speed 0 or `putAt` for 2 s in a row is then free of them for 2 s.
- **A room sees a stick and two buttons for each player.** It says how the stick moves them (walk and jump in the plane, walk the floor, float free, or not at all, so the room's code reads the stick itself) and gives each button one job for the whole room: the engine's jump or grab, or code of its own, with a picture and a word for the HUD. That is how controls change from room to room.
- **Pointing is the room's, not a player's.** A room may also take a tap or a click on a spot or a thing, and where a finger or the mouse is held. It comes from anyone and the room is not told who, so it suits what is done together. Every device has it, and the bots can do it too.
- **A seat is a device**: one of four zones of keys (by `KeyboardEvent.code`: W A S D with V B; I J K L with N M; the arrows with Enter and right Shift; the number pad's 8 4 5 6 with its Enter and +; no Ctrl, Alt or system key), one of two pads on the touch screen (a floating stick on the left, a big a and a smaller b on the right, in the bottom 30% of the screen), or a gamepad (buttons 0 and 1, Start pauses, Select or Y calls the helper). A player claims one by pressing on it, and their saves remember it, and it is offered first: a press on it seats the player who sat there last. A laptop has no zone 3, and three or four on one keyboard meet its limit on keys held at once, so gamepads are the way to a full party. Each seat has a colour, shown over its player and free for a room to use. A seat's buttons count 1 s after it sat down.
- **The page** steps the sim at a fixed 60 Hz from a requestAnimationFrame accumulator, at most 6 sim frames a screen frame; only the HUD re-renders. It goes back to the house 3.5 s of game clock after the end.

### The look, people and presents

- **Silly Billy's flat-shaded low-poly look by import.** From the side the camera is this game's own: a long lens straight into the room's open front, its lens shifted (not tipped) so floors show their tops and a tap maps to the room by a scale and a shift. From high up it has Castle Builder's pitch (52 degrees) and lens, never turned. Both stand still. No shadows; tops are painted lighter. A room's still solids and decor are merged into one lit and one glowing mesh; a piece that changes is folded to a point, never rebuilt.
- **The look call** (`engine/look.ts`, `engine/lookBuilder.ts`): a second Opus call writes the room's look as code `function (r, room)`, asked on the first trial report that has a survey, so it sees the room as built, with its dressable solids marked. It may use only `r.wallpaper`, `r.dress`, `r.decor`, `r.prop`, `r.lights` and `r.lamp`, and frozen reads of the built room. It gets no `w`, no handlers and no frame function, and a Math of its own (the room's seed + 6): it cannot change the play, and a vitest invariant holds that 60 s of random play with and without a look give the same state frame by frame. `r.dress` never stretches: a model is built to the solid's size and drawn only when its top is within 0.1 m of the solid's, it reaches within 0.1 m of its sides, and it goes at most 0.3 m past them (`DRESS_FIT`).
- **Props** live in the scene only, never in the sim, the trial or the bots. A prop reacts (wobble, hop, spin, blink or swing, at most once a second, with its sound 6 dB under the room's and a puff) when a player passes in front of it or anyone taps it, and idles all the time. A tap goes to the play as before; the prop never takes it. All props share one mesh, one draw call. A look needs at least 8 props that react, in at least 3 of the room's 4 quarters (`LOOK_LEAST`).
- **The look's budget** (`LOOK_BUDGET`): 3,000 pieces, 500,000 build turns, 48 props, 400 small lights, a wallpaper motif of at most 12 shapes, and at most a third of its pieces left out for breaking a placement rule. From the side, pieces behind the play stand within z -3 to -1, in front within z 1 to 1.5 and at most 0.5 m tall; from high up nothing tall stands where it would hide the floor. The check (`trial/lookCheck.ts`) is a page job of about 0.05 s; its failures go to the look's fix only, a room's to the game's fix only. Two tries for a look. A look that fails twice or times out is given up and the room is kept without one; a room that passed is never dropped for its look.
- **A player is a Silly Billy kid**, posed each frame by this game's own animator from what the sim says. A new player starts as their gallery face, in the house's own skin and hair colours, a little lighter and warmer, because the lit side view shades faces. Five of the fourteen pieces come from Silly Billy's `content/items/people.ts`, the other nine are written in the kit; a test checks no piece covers the eyes or brows. A kid costs about 24 draw calls.
- **A present is model code** `function (m, c)` in one of Silly Billy's slots, built round its model kid in two marker colours the scene swaps for the wearer's: `c.main` and `c.second` are the wearer's two colours, and what is painted `c.main` carries the wearer's pattern. Its real form is written in a sentence before its code, as Dissertation does. The check (`trial/giftCheck.ts`): at most 120 shapes, inside the slot's frame, the face left free, at least one shape `c.main`, and in the hand at least 0.4 m. A room has four.
- **The end** is the house's own hall, built by the sim, the kids each beside a box in their seat's colour. A present opens by the seat's a or b or a tap; this room's presents first, then other rooms' newest first; never one the player already owns, so a player who owns every present gets none; siblings get different ones while there are enough; one that will not build is never given. The fireworks cost two draw calls.
- **Colours and patterns are the game's own set**: sixteen colours and six patterns (plain, stripes, dots, squares, zigzag, stars) in the second colour over the main, drawn in the shader from the model's own frame at no draw call. Hair is always plain. A present is first worn in the bright colour nearest its seat's colour, made 40% lighter, with the seat's own as its second.
- **The scene's limits** (`scene/limits.ts`): 160 draw calls (four kids wearing all five slots add 20, and the heaviest rooms reach the 150s) and 3 ms a frame. Measured in a headless Chromium: the own rooms peak at 141 calls and 1.2 to 2.2 ms. A room's first frame with twenty worn models takes 140 to 180 ms.

### Help, and how hard

- **Steps are the room's**: one list in order for everyone, whatever the kind of room, each with a hint in both languages, the spot it happens at and when it is done. What each player has done on their own, the room shows by their badge. A contest has one step and may say its rule at the start.
- **The helper** is the house's own, a small lantern with a face (invented here; Sami agreed, 2026-11-21). It waits on the ceiling's beam at the right, outside the room, so it hides no play. After 40 s with no step done it flies to the next step's spot; after 40 s more it says the hint, shown and aloud with `pickVoice`. A `?` button (or a gamepad's Select or Y) calls it at once. Hints say what to try, never the whole answer. Speech wakes on the visit's first touch or key, so a tablet says the helper's hints aloud.
- **Never trapped.** The pause card (Esc, a gamepad's Start, or the button) has keep going, start again, another room, sound, full screen and more games. Leaving is always possible and is counted.
- **Opus hears how it went, from the first room on** (Sami, 2026-11-21). Each request gives the last five plays (`PLAYED_MOST`), newest first: their time against the bots', restarts, hints, whether they left, their thumbs, without the bots' plays or rooms missing from the library. A clock room that ran out is told as "the time ran out with N of M steps done", never as finished. WHAT THE ROOM IS TOLD says what it is for: easier where they took long, needed hints or left, harder where they were quick without hints. Because Opus designs one room ahead, what happens in a room shapes the room after next.
- **Three levels in every room**, for the step in between. Room code reads the level and scales how much there is to do by it (counts, speeds, gaps), never longer to stand, hold or wait: the `w.level()` line and `levelsAlike` both say so, held by `reference.test.ts`. The party plays at the lowest level among its players, and starting again a second time drops it by one. After a room each player's own level moves (`LEVEL_MOVE`), against the bots' seconds at the level played: up when finished with every step done, no hint and no start-again, within 2.5 times the bots; down when left after 30 s or more, ended by its clock with steps undone, at 6 times the bots or more, or after two start-agains. Left within 30 s moves nothing. Up is to at least one over the door's level, so the lowest player climbs; down applies only to a player the room was played at. A `?` pressed again for a hint already said counts no new hint. This needs no Opus, so it works on kept rooms and on the hosted build.
- **At least a minute for the bots** (Sami, 2026-11-21). A walkthrough takes at least a minute and at most two; children take several times longer, which is the brief's three to five minutes, and with the dressing room it is the time Opus needs for the next room.
- **Each child gives a thumb** in the dressing room. One module, `saves.ts`, folds the seated players' saves into the party's level and who has played what, and writes a room's result back to each, in one key, `house`, read, changed and written at once so another tab's write is kept. Nothing else reads or writes saves. Under `?autoplay` a result is marked `bots` and moves no level; under `?level` it moves no level.

## The contract, in outline

The contract is types and reference text in `src/engine/api.ts` (the room), `src/trial/api.ts` (the walkthrough), `src/engine/look.ts` (the look), `src/engine/gift.ts` (the kit and a present), `src/engine/world.ts`, `src/seats/types.ts`, `src/protocol.ts` and `src/saves.ts`. Each call's line sits beside it in a const the compiler holds to the interface.

```
room   the body of function (r, w)       runs once; builds the room for its party (always the same number of players) at w.level() (1 to 3)
walk   the body of function* (bots, w)   plays the room to its end, one bot a player
look   the body of function (r, room)    dresses the room as built; cannot touch the play
gift   the body of function (m, c)       a present's model, in the wearer's colours
```

| | What the builder gives |
|---|---|
| The place | Solids in colours: a box, a ramp, a ball, a floor one can jump up through, a surface that is slippery, bouncy or carries along. Movers, built as a group and placed by a function of time, which carry and push. Loose things with a kit model, to carry, push, throw or stand on. Decor that is only seen. Lamps. The way out. The shell (the room's colours, patterns and light) comes in the answer; the room's size is the house's. |
| Noticing | Zones (entered, stayed in, left), which may also change how bodies move inside them: pull, drag, lift, float. A thing touched, landed on or used. A tap or a click from anyone. Something run every step. |
| The players | Where each starts. How the stick moves them and what the two buttons do. Putting a player somewhere, changing their speed, jump, size or weight for a while, a bonk, their score, their colour. |
| Telling | The steps. A count, a clock, a number or a word floating in the world. |
| Feeling | Puffs, a sound by name, a shake, a thing's parts posed, a glow. |
| The end | Finish, with the order they came in when it was a contest. |

The bots have: go to a place or a thing, press or hold a button, carry or push a thing to a place, tap a thing, set the stick, wait, wait until, and several of these side by side.

- Everything is built before play; a thing appears by being built hidden and shown. No builder call gives back null. One budget of pieces, where every shape counts each time its model is placed.
- The order inside a frame is fixed (`FRAME_ORDER`), so a room plays the same in the worker, in vitest and on the tablet.
- Movers have their own clock. The search sees where an engine move under way goes; a move the room's code starts it sees only from then.
- The room ends only by `w.finish()` or `r.clock` (3 to 5 minutes). `w.finish()` takes effect at the end of its frame, after the steps are asked, and every step must be done by then. A step whose `done()` is already true when its turn comes fails.
- The request says the kind. Together needs every player. Against each other has one step, a rule and an order. Side by side gives each player a part of their own, and a step is done when all have done it.
- The page reads the library through one `LibrarySource`: `./api/` on the dev server, the shipped files on the build.

**Known limits**:
- `goTo` from high up cannot make a corner-to-corner jump near 3 m from a floor too small to run up on (about 2 m square).

- A seat that falls asleep (a child walks away) stalls a room that needs everyone. The way out is another room from the pause card, seated again as a smaller party; a way to tap the sleeper out is not planned.

- The zigzag swatch follows the scene's screenshot, not a reading of the shader: if the model frame's y is ever flipped, check both.

## Not in the first version

Music. Quiz questions written by a model. A second model that judges a room for fun before it is tried. Play over a network. Voice. The mouse as a player's stick. A split screen.
