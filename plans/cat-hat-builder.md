# Cat Hat Builder (`cat-hat-builder/`)

The brief is [`cat-hat-builder/design.md`](../cat-hat-builder/design.md), in Finnish: a builder in a hard hat with cat ears drives a road, picks up blocks and materials, stops at rocks to break them for stone, and builds a house on an empty building site out of materials they choose; the child picks and dresses a human builder (hair, skin, shirt, trousers, shoes, glasses, backpack, tool); machines turn the raw goods into better materials; the house is a kerrostalo or a rivitalo of rooms in different shapes, each with a theme the child picks (disco, play, ball pit, slide, gym, sleep, music, crafts, cat, birthday and more) and contents the child chooses. Side view, 2D like pia-jump. "Keksi lisää" applies.

## Decisions

- **One loop, five steps a child can retell.** Drive (pick up goods, break rocks) → the truck tips the goods at the yard → machines turn each raw good into a building material → build rooms on a plot → pick a theme and fill the room. Every step answers a tap at once; the builder dashes over as a picture (at most 1 s), never a walk to wait for. Home from the road, the truck drives in from the street's end, tips its load at the yard and backs into the garage; while it drives in, the street takes no taps.
- **The look is its own: "sunny site".** Saturated toy primaries, one navy ink, flat fills, made things as boxes with a lit top face and a darker right face receding up-right, a stepped flat sky. Nothing of pia-jump's pastel, plum ink or bottom crescent; pia-jump lends only the side view on the canvas. The rules and the palette are the header of `src/draw/look.ts`.
- **The builder is one side-view rig** (3/4 view facing right, mirrored to face left) on every screen including the mirror; no front view. 1.2 m tall, about 3.2 heads to the ear tips, boxy and sturdy, bean eyes, mitten hands. The cat-ear hard hat is the brightest thing on the figure; its ears grow relative to the head as the builder gets small on screen, and flick on every success. Only the player's builder wears it; the friends in the rooms are the same rig in eight fixed outfits without a hat.
- **Wardrobe without reading.** Picture tabs head to toe (hat, hair, skin, glasses, shirt, bag, trousers, shoes, tool), each tab icon the child's current pick drawn by the real draw code; tapping a part of the big builder opens its tab. At most 8 options per tab and one colour row (hat, hair, shirt, trousers). Every option differs by silhouette, with big, few pattern repeats, so it reads at street size. A dice makes a random builder and a back arrow undoes the last five rolls. Hair styles are all designed from the hat brim down. Choices show in play: the tool sets the rock's sound and burst, light-up sneakers blink, the jetpack puffs on hops.
- **Five raw goods, five machines, five materials, each one to one.** Log → sawmill → planks; clay → brick oven → bricks; rock → cement mixer → concrete (pale element slabs, never mistaken for the blue-grey rock); sand → glass oven → glass; gem → rainbow machine → rainbow blocks. Raw goods are drawn as lumps and made goods as blocks, so a child sees what a machine did. One good is one shape and one colour everywhere: road, bed, heap, stack, HUD.
- **Machines are made, then used** ("tekee tai käyttää"). The yard starts with five empty pads. The first time a raw good comes home without its machine, the crane drops a crate with that good's picture on its pad; the child taps it, the builder hits it three times with their own tool, and it unfolds into the machine. A built machine runs its whole heap on one tap, one batch every 0.8 s; every extra tap makes the current batch pop out at once. A batch spends and credits at its start; everything moving is a picture.
- **Economy: two blocks per square.** A room costs 2 of its material per cell (small 2, wide 4, tall 4, big 8), shown as two little blocks drawn in each square of the shape card. Machines are 1 to 1. A trip brings about 16 raw goods (18 when the truck flies through the ramp's gems), about five rooms, so driving and building take turns every 5 to 8 minutes and the six plots fill around trip 10 or 11. A sim test drives the real road layouts and holds 4 to 7 rooms a trip, never zero.
- **The road is the child's to drive.** Hold the big pedal (or Space, the right arrow, D); let go and the truck rolls to a stop; a tap is a push of about a second. The truck stops by itself at every boulder and every crossing, so holding can never crash or skip anything. At a boulder the pedal becomes the tool button; each tap (on the button or the rock) is a hit with the chosen tool, the cracks grow, and after 3 (small) or 5 (big) hits the rock bursts into 2 or 3 stones that fly into the bed, and a pedal still held drives on after a short pause; with no hit for 6 s the builder starts hitting on their own. One boulder in three hides a gem. Crossers (ducks, cats, hedgehogs, cows) cross in at most 3 s, never on a bridge or in a tunnel, and answer the horn. One ramp per trip flies the truck through an arc of three gems at full speed and leaves one gem at its foot for a slow truck. Each trip has one surprise: a train at a level crossing (the truck waits clear of the rails and the camera frames the crossing) or a hot-air balloon that drops a gem into the bed. Every trip has one present box. Nothing falls off for good.
- **Trip 1 is a 200 m training road** (4 logs, one small boulder, ducks, a present) so the first themed room comes within about three minutes. From trip 2, tapping the truck opens a signpost with three roads: Forest (logs, clay at the river), Beach (sand), Hills (rocks, gems in the tunnel). Each road carries its own goods most and every raw good at least twice. Every road is laid out from a seed and linted over a thousand seeds.
- **Presents keep trip 10 fresh.** Each road's present rides home in the bed, pops out as the truck tips and lands in the yard in view, where it opens with a fanfare and a "New!" card; the box shrinks away when the card goes. It holds the next unlock in a fixed order: the Bath, Space, Kitchen and Sauna rooms and the wardrobe specials (jetpack, magic wand, light-up sneakers, star glasses, heart glasses, cowboy boots, cat bag). After the last, a present holds three gems. Locked things are simply not shown.
- **Two house kinds, one 18 m plot.** "Tall house" (kerrostalo): 3 columns by 5 floors, with balconies and an outside glass lift. "Row house" (rivitalo): 6 columns by 2 floors, visibly three homes of 2 columns each with their own door and front garden. Cells are 2.4 m square. The plot shows its whole grid as faint blueprint lines from the start.
- **Four room shapes**: small 1x1, wide 2x1, tall 1x2, big 2x2. A room's bottom cells rest on the ground or on rooms. The charm of other shapes moves to the roof, which follows the top room of every column, stops at the wall of a taller neighbour, and cycles through pointed, round, flat with a garden, and a tower with a flag on the build tray's roof button (or a tap on a roof with no room to build above it). Pick a material and a shape (both sticky); valid spots show as blueprint ghosts; touching shows the ghost that covers the finger (nearest centre among overlaps) and lifting places it. The crane lowers the finished room, it lands with a squash and a puff, and the builder hammers it.
- **Taking back takes two taps.** With the hand tool, the first tap lifts a room with nothing on top a hand's width on the crane hook and it wobbles; a second tap on it carries it away and every good flies back. A tap elsewhere sets it down; a room with rooms on top makes them wiggle, with a soft wooden rattle.
- **Rooms are slots, not free placement.** Per cell column: one ceiling slot, one wall slot per storey, two floor slots on the bottom storey (small 4, wide 8, tall 5, big 10). Picking a theme fills the room with that theme's default items. In the room view a tap on a tray item puts it in the first free slot of its kind, never under the visiting builder while another is free (when full, it replaces the oldest of its kind); an item already in the room wears a tick in the tray, and a tap there takes it back out. A tap on anything in the room makes it play. No drag, no small x. Two paint pots cycle the wallpaper and the floor through four colourings of the theme.
- **Fourteen themes, five items each**: one ceiling, one wall, two floor items and a friend or pet, every item with an idle loop, a tap reaction and a sound. The brief's ten are open from the start (Disco, Play, Ball pit, Slide, Gym, Sleep, Music, Crafts, Cat, Birthday); Bath, Space, Kitchen and Sauna come in presents. The "Which room?" picker is a grid of big picture cards with one word each; tapping outside leaves the room empty, and its tray then offers "Which room?" as one big button. "Another room" changes a furnished room's theme: the new theme's things replace the old theme's, and things put in from other themes stay. Idle motion and tap reactions come from a shared vocabulary applied by the renderer; an item's own drawing adds its detail (candles out, a fresh painting).
- **The builder lives in the house.** Zooming into a furnished room sends the builder in to do its thing (dance, dive, slide, bounce, sleep, drum), standing where the visit started; a tap on the builder does it again. A tap on a front door, or the build tray's bell, rings a doorbell party: every item in the house plays in turn from the bottom floor up, and the friends wave.
- **One thing bounces.** Exactly one next step bounces at a time, picked in order: in the yard, what is in sight first (a station counts once half of it shows), a crate before a machine with goods in its heap; then what is out of sight, the garage's side of the camera first and each time the nearest, so the camera walks the yard once and its last glide heads to the houses; then the plot nearest the camera that can take a room if one is affordable; then the truck. When the next thing is out of sight, the camera glides to it a beat after the street is quiet (no finger down, no machine running in view, no present card up). After 8 s with no touch a hand taps it twice, on the thing itself (a plot's sign, a house's rooms); it too waits while a machine in view runs. In the build view the material or the smaller shape that pays bounces; when nothing pays, the missing material bounces under a bubble with its machine or the truck, and the bubble goes there. At night the nearest lit house bounces.
- **One back button** in the same place on every screen (top left); on the street that place holds the builder's face, which opens the mirror. On the road it is a house: the truck goes home at once and keeps its load. Back from a house lands on the next thing to do; from a house wider than the screen, on its rooms.
- **Plots: always exactly one empty plot waiting.** The street starts with one plot; when the empty plot gets its first room, a bulldozer levels the next one, up to six.
- **Good night ends a session.** From the pause menu or the moon on the street: evening falls, every built room's windows glow, the camera goes to the nearest lit house and after each lights-out to the next, the child taps each house to turn its lights off room by room while the friends yawn, and when the street is dark the builder waves from the garage and a still "Good night!" card stops the frame loop. A tap plays again.
- **Nothing is lost and nothing startles.** Take-back returns every good, Home on the road keeps the load, lights change at most three times a second, the horn is a soft "meep meep", machines chug and puff, the crane never drops or swings.
- **The save forgives.** One JSON object under the key `cat-hat-builder` in the player's saves (`profiles/`, in localStorage): the look, goods, machines, plots, trips, unlocks, switches. It is read field by field: an unknown item becomes an empty slot, an unknown theme none, a room that no longer fits is removed and refunded, a bad count becomes 0, a bad look part the default. This file is exempt from the repo's no-backward-compatibility rule because "nothing is lost" is the documented requirement: never rename an id, only add. Written after any change to it (at most once a second) and on pagehide; `navigator.storage.persist()` is asked on the first tap. URL switches read the save and never write it.
- **Finnish or English, by the player** (plans/finnish-and-english.md). Every word is in `src/words.ts`; the logic keeps ids, and the catalog keeps only the themes' visits (`THEME_VISIT`). The house cards are Kerrostalo and Rivitalo, the brief's own words. Finnish words are longer: the house cards' word shrinks with a card on an upright phone, and Finnish Go! ("Mennään!") and the longest theme words ("Liukumäki") shrink to fit their button or card where the English would not need it (`:lang(fi)` in styles.css).
- **Screens.** The iPad comes first (1180x820, 1024x768 and 820x1180 upright), then a desktop. A window under 520 px tall and wide (a phone on its side) gets its own camera insets and a compact HUD, the build view fitted to the floors in use; an upright phone gets a theme strip that scrolls. The camera's insets and the HUD's CSS read the same size rules.
- **Engineering.** The whole session is a pure state (`game/rules.ts`: `createState`, `act`, `step` at 60 Hz) driven by one `Action` union; the browser glue (`game.ts`) turns keys, pointers and HUD buttons into actions. Two worlds: the street (garage, yard, plots, houses and every room) and the road. The mirror, plot and room views are camera shots of the street world, and the camera is a pure function of state and time, so the picture and the hit test read the same geometry (`game/layout.ts`, `game/camera.ts`). A scripted child plays whole sessions through the real hit test, and a fuzz checks the invariants. Houses are baked per plot and zoom bucket; only the room in view, the builder's room and a room that just played are drawn live over the bake, and Good night's lights are washed over it.

## Left to build

- Try it on the real iPad: the sound levels against each other and the device volume, the frame rate in the tall house's room view and at Good night, how the pedal and the ghosts feel under a finger.
- Notched phones on their side: the camera's insets ignore the safe areas.
- A phone on its side: a tall house's top floors build at 43 px cells and the wardrobe's colours are 51 to 53 px, under the 56 px the rest keeps; on the oldest small phones (568x320) the wardrobe's options still scroll.
- With every floor slot full, the visiting builder stands at the room's middle and hides a sliver of the two things beside it, the sleeping pose most.
- Small phones held upright (375x667, 360x740): the pedal covers the level crossing and the passing train.
- Good night on an upright phone: the row house is wider than the screen (a tap anywhere on it works).
- Move-in day: a family moves into a house at its third room.
- A wrecking ball that takes a whole house back.
- A photo button that shares a picture of the house.
- Dragging items between slots.
- The truck cat following the builder into rooms.
- A name sign on finished houses, typed once by a parent.
- More themes as presents: pool, aquarium, jungle, dinosaur, snow, candy, library, cinema, garden, train.
- A web app manifest so the directory can live on the iPad's Home Screen, where Safari's storage cap does not apply (repo-wide).
