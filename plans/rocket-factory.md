# Rocket Factory (`rocket-factory/`)

The brief is [`rocket-factory/design.md`](../rocket-factory/design.md), in Sami's words: a kids' version of Factorio with less tech tree and no upgrades, keeping resource gathering, factory building, conveyor belts, science, and monsters or opponents that cause havoc and must be planned around. Four concepts were written from four lenses (a five-year-old's play, Factorio's systems, the monsters as the drama, the art and feel), three judges scored them, and the winner with its grafts became a draft. A seven-lens panel then reviewed the draft (child development, the co-playing parent, a Factorio veteran, touch on an iPad, art direction, engineering, and an economist who simulated the numbers belt by belt). Everything below is the result; where a lens lost, the reason is given.

## Decisions

### The game

- **Beep's rocket.** Beep, a little tin robot, lives by a launch pad where a rocket waits as a dotted outline in four parts. The child taps rocks so Beep digs them, presses machines onto a mint-green board like stickers, and draws belts with one finger; things ride a belt the way she drew it. Ovens bake rocks into bars, the workshop turns blue bars into gears, two bottle makers mix bars or a gear and a bubble into science bottles, and the lab drinks the bottles to find new things. Every working machine plays its part of the factory's song, and the song wakes the Munchers in their burrows: round raspberry critters who come out for the fizzy bottles, sit on belts stuffing their cheeks and nap on the bottle makers. Tickles, bubble towers fed by belt, hedges and bowls of gold send them home. Fill the rocket and it flies.
- **The rules a child retells**, one fact each, arriving one or two at a time over an hour: tap the rocks and Beep digs one; a digger digs by itself; things ride a belt the way you drew it; a belt into a machine feeds it, a belt out of a machine takes the new things away; drop a machine on a belt and it works on everything riding by; ovens bake rocks into bars; two blue bars make a gear; a blue bar and a gold bar make a green bottle; a gear and a bubble make an orange bottle; the lab drinks bottles and finds new things; machines near the munchers' flowers wake them sooner; munchers love the fizzy bottles; tickle a muncher and it sneezes everything back; a bubble tower on a bubble belt sends munchers home; hedges make munchers walk round; fill their bowl and they have a picnic; fill the rocket and it flies.

### The board and the view

- **One designed board of 60 x 36 cells, all of it open from the first minute.** Today's first board, 20 x 12, is its north-west corner (x 0..19, y 0..11), unchanged but for burrow C's ring of flowers, which the old east edge cut and which now closes on column 20: the pad, the start oven, the first blue and gold patches, the pond at the west edge and burrows A, B and C stay where every rule, blueprint cell, demo and test knows them, so a coordinate in code is the same as before and a cell's index changes only through COLS. The new land lies east and south of it. This reverses the first design's "one designed board, always entirely on screen" (every judge but the Factorio veteran's first instinct agreed, and at five out of sight is out of mind): after playing, Sami found the screen too small and picked a map about three times wider and taller, with more patches of everything and more burrows spread round, which no screen shows whole at a size a finger can draw on. There are no lands, no flaps and no quarter turn.
- **The new land** (`board.ts`): burrows D, E and F far out (D east at x 47, y 5; E south-east at x 51, y 27; F south at x 27, y 29: 35 to 55 cells from the pad, a 40 to 65 cell walk from their doors to the factory's core), each in its whole ring of purple flowers; seven more blue patches and five more gold ones (2x2 to 4x3, each taking a digger), the richest touching D's, E's and F's flowers and a few small safe ones just south of the start block; two more ponds, each a rectangle of 2x2 or more, so the pump fits with a free cell beside it for its belt; groves of trees and single trees. The walkable land is one piece (every door reaches the core by walking, and a belt reaches every free cell from the pad); `board.test.ts` is the lint.
- **The view is a camera** (`camera.ts`): `{ x, y, cell }`, the board point at the middle of what the HUD leaves free and CSS px per cell, the view's and never the sim's. `startCamera` fits the start block the way the whole board used to be fitted, in whole px cells, so the start reads exactly as before; `viewOf(camera, ...)` makes the View (the canvas, the cell, the origin, the HUD's frame). The board beyond the start block shows where it falls, in the free area's margins east and south, and the table only past the board's own edge. The renderer bakes only the cells in view (a bake of the whole board at the build zoom is 6240 x 3744 device px, past iOS Safari's 16.7 Mpx canvas, which paints nothing and raises no error), and the hit test lists only what the free area shows.
- **Cells** are as big as the camera makes them. At the start view in full screen that is 52 px on 1180x820 (54 without the home indicator's inset), 49 px on 1024x768 and 39 px upright (an upright window shows the same corner unturned, with rows of the board south of it); Safari's bars would take them to about 43 px, so on a touch screen the start card's Play asks for full screen, Keep going asks again, and a parent who turns full screen off in the pause menu is remembered. Side gutters are 20 px on touch screens (Safari's back swipe), and the tray stays 16 px plus the bottom inset above the screen's edge (the Dock swipe). Cells are paths for strokes, not tap targets: every tap target keeps 56 px or more through the slop and the hit order.
- **A phone on its side** shows the start block fitted (about 25 px cells), the tray as columns inside the safe areas and the cards in a left column; an upright phone shows a turn-me picture and takes no board input. The phone gets the camera too.
- **The camera's motion is still to build** (Left to build): two fingers pan and a pinch zooms, from an overview (the whole map) to about 80 px cells; the wheel zooms about the pointer and a middle drag or the arrow keys pan; one finger still only draws, places, taps and tickles, a second finger landing drops a stroke or a ghost with nothing placed, and a still resting thumb is not a pan; a belt drawn or a sticker carried near the edge pans the view along; out of sight is out of mind at five, so what bounces off screen, a burrow's warning and munchers out show as their pictures at the edge, a tap on one glides the view there, and the view glides to the step when the hint's hand is due and never moves while a finger is down.

### Beep and the child's hands

- **Beep** is a round coral tin-toy robot (the rocket's fin colour, so the rocket is plainly Beep's) with a cream face plate, two painted dot eyes and a smile, short springy arms with mitten hands, a propeller on top that spins while it flies, and an antenna ball that glows the colour of what it carries. No face screen: a hovering egg with glowing eyes is somebody else's robot. Beep is never hurt, tired or chased.
- **Every job is credited at the tap**; Beep flies over in at most 0.35 s as a picture. Dig: a touch on a bare patch digs a rock for the nearest oven with room (it arcs in); held, it digs again each time an oven takes one; with no oven wanting one, Beep taps the patch and points at the full oven (the basket never fills from digging, so the digger visibly beats the hand). Carry: a tap on an item on a belt, a machine's stack, a heap or the basket sends the things where they are wanted. Tickle: section Munchers. Place and peel: the sticker slaps down and Beep pats it.
- **Where wanted** has one fixed order: the rocket's open part, then the lab's need, then the nearest machine with room for it (Manhattan distance from the thing's cell, ties by id), else Beep's basket on its own cell beside the pad. A tap on the basket sends each thing on the same way. Beep's carry off a full roof stack keeps to the stack's own line, the buildings its belts lead to, else the basket: a stack stands full because its line is backed up, and its things in another line's machine choke that line (the gold digger's rocks in the gear oven bake gold bars the workshop's belt fills with). Flights of more than 8 things fly 8 pictures and credit the rest at once.
- **A touch is read in one pure pointer router** (`touch.ts`) that the page and the scripted child both use:
  - A touch within 72 px of a muncher (where it is now or was 0.25 s ago; where cells are small at most 1.5 cells, never under 28 px round its body, so three munchers on a phone's 25 px cells leave her finger most of the board), or anywhere on a machine a muncher naps on, tickles at once, from any finger at any time. It never draws, places or peels, never takes the board, and leaves a picked tool picked. Dragging a muncher off a belt is therefore a tickle, which is what a five-year-old tries first.
  - On a bare patch (rock cells with no digger) or the pond, the touch digs at once and never becomes a stroke, however far a held finger drifts.
  - A touch that lifts before it became a stroke, a dig or a ghost drag is a tap on what it went down on, however long it rested (up to 2 s) and however far it slid while still within 28 px of that thing; a press on a thing riding a belt follows the thing hop by hop, so the lift carries the thing she pressed. When her other hand took that thing away meanwhile (Oops, the peel), the tap finds nothing there: a building gone answers nothing, a hedge or a belt gone puffs the ground.
  - On bare ground a stroke begins after 24 px of travel with the finger in a second cell. From anything a tap answers (a machine, the pad, a belt, a thing, a heap) it begins only once the finger is more than 28 px off it and in another cell (from a footprint, from the cell it left by), so a sloppy tap never edits her factory: a tap that slides a little off a machine's edge or back along a belt stays a tap. A lift followed within 150 ms by a touch within one cell continues the same stroke or drag, even within a muncher's reach, and so does one in the very next frame however long it was (under `?fast` a frame is longer than 150 ms of the game). At the lift the cell under the finger, next to the stroke's end, is its last turn when the stroke then ends in a mouth, a join or a feed, or on open ground once the finger is 0.15 cell into it: the row lock keeps a wobbly finger straight, and must never eat the turn into what she draws to.
  - The board belongs to the finger that starts a stroke or a ghost drag. A finger that has started neither (a press, or a dig) hands the board at once to the next finger that lands, and one digging stops then: the heel of her hand lands just before her fingertip as the hand comes down to draw, and a thumb holding the iPad rests on the board's west edge (on the pond). A contact the browser reports wider than 100 CSS px (a heel, a palm) never takes it at all, and nor does a touch in a gutter or on the table round the board. A resting thumb or heel never freezes the game or draws.
  - A window rotation, a page hide, a pause or a pointercancel drops a stroke or a ghost with nothing placed, and so does her other hand picking a tool (the rules drop the drag; the finger lets go, so the peel picked mid-stroke never rolls up her line). A card found while a finger is down comes at its lift (at most 3 s after that touch began), never over a stroke under way; a card and the launch wait for a finger down to lift (at most 3 s), so a lift never closes a card she did not see. During the launch a press is only ever a tap and nothing edits: a tap on the rocket while it counts pops the number, a tree shakes, flowers sway, the ground puffs.
- **Feedback floats above the fingertip**: her hand comes from below and covers 40 to 55 px. The stroke's end sign rides 0.8 cell (at least 44 px) above the finger on a thin stem, where the finger is the pen. A held sticker is held by its bottom edge: its footprint's middle rides half its size plus 0.15 cell up the screen from the fingertip (its dashed rim just touches the fingertip), and its picture is drawn over its own snapped footprint, so the sticker lands where she sees it and the snap and the hint's spot work where the sticker is. The mouse covers nothing: its ghost is at the pointer.
- **Every tap answers at once**: a tree shakes and drops a leaf, flowers sway, bare ground puffs and Beep looks over, an empty belt's chevrons hop, a sleeping burrow's muncher peeks out and waves sleepily, a grey sticker wiggles the lab card with its next sticker peeking out.
- **Mouse and keys**: the left button is the finger, a right click is the peel's first tap and a right drag rolls up, hover outlines what a click would take and a picked sticker's ghost follows the pointer. Ctrl and the wheel or a pinch never zoom the page. Keys 1 to 9 pick kinds (the digger is always 1, the oven 2), B the pencil, H the hedge, X the peel, Z or Ctrl+Z Oops, Space tickles the muncher nearest the middle, Enter presses Play and closes a card, Esc or P pause, M sound, F full screen.

### Things and the chain

- **Eight things, three raw.** Raw things are dull lumps and made things are bright and smooth, so she sees what a machine did; each is one shape and one key colour everywhere (belt, Beep's arms, a cheek, a recipe bubble, a card), designed at 22 px first with less detail below 30 px.

| Thing | Looks like | Comes from | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue rock | a soft slate-blue faceted chunk | blue patches, by digger or Beep | oven |
| Gold rock | a round lumpy nugget with specks | gold patches, by digger or Beep | oven |
| Bubble | an opaque pearl with one sheen arc | the pond, by pump or Beep | bubble towers, the orange bottle maker |
| Blue bar | a bright blue rounded bar | oven: 1 blue rock in 2 s | workshop, green bottle maker, the rocket's legs |
| Gold bar | a bright gold rounded bar | oven: 1 gold rock in 2 s | green bottle maker, bowls |
| Gear | a lilac cog with 6 chunky teeth | workshop: 2 blue bars in 4 s | orange bottle maker, the rocket's body and nose |
| Green bottle | a round flask of green fizz | green maker: 1 blue bar + 1 gold bar in 4 s | lab |
| Orange bottle | a tall flask of orange fizz under a gold star cork | orange maker: 1 gear + 1 bubble in 4 s | lab, the rocket's engine |

- Blue and gold mix to green (the paint mixing she knows); the orange bottle is the rocket's fire. The recipe shows inside a bottle (swirls, the gear in a bubble) only at 40 px and up: the start view, the tray and the cards. No red bottle sits next to a green one, and the bubble is a pearl, not a see-through bubble (both are other games' pictures).
- **The ratios she can feel**: one digger feeds one oven, one oven feeds one workshop, one workshop feeds one orange bottle maker, and a blue oven and a gold oven feed two green bottle makers.

### Buildings and stickers

- **Placing costs nothing.** Each building is a sticker in the tray with a count: placing uses one, peeling gives it back with everything inside (to where it is wanted), and discoveries bring more. Belts are free. "Can't afford" never happens and no store can be drunk dry by the lab. The systems judge wanted prices paid from a store the factory feeds, so the factory pays for its own growth; with science as the clock (section Science) it does, through bottles. Every other lens kept the stickers.
- **The roster is sized to the start block** (208 usable cells of its 240, about 205 of them filled by a tidy full factory; the rest of the board is room to grow into), and every sticker arrives with a need she can see:

| Building | Size | Takes in | Gives out | Stickers | They come with |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rocket pad | 3x3, fixed at x1..3, y4..6 | the open parts' things | the rocket grows | 1 | the start |
| Digger | 2x2 on 2 or more rock cells of one kind | | 1 rock every 2 s | 4 | the 3rd hand-made bar, Gold!, Gears!, More! |
| Oven | 2x2 | rocks of either kind, 4 waiting | 1 bar per rock in 2 s, in arrival order | 4 | one placed at the start, Gold!, Gears!, More! |
| Workshop | 2x2 | blue bars, 4 | a gear per 2 blue bars in 4 s | 1 | Gears! |
| Green bottle maker | 2x2 | blue bars 4, gold bars 4 | a green bottle per pair in 4 s | 2 | Science!, More! |
| Orange bottle maker | 2x2 | gears 4, bubbles 4 | an orange bottle per pair in 4 s | 1 | Stars! |
| Lab | 2x2 | every bottle, always (an uncapped shelf) | drinks one a second toward the discovery | 1 | Science! |
| Pump | 2x2 on the pond | | 1 bubble a second | 1 | Bubbles! |
| Bubble tower | 1x1 on a belt cell | bubbles from the belt under it, 2 held | a suds shot every 1.5 s at a muncher within 3.5 cells | 4 | Bubbles! (2), Nose!, More! |
| Snack bowl | 1x1 on a belt cell on a burrow's flowers | gold bars from the belt under it, 4 held | a picnic for that burrow's visits | 3 | Snacks! (2), More! |
| Hedge | 1x1, drawn like a belt | | munchers walk round | 14 | Hedges! (8), Nose! and More! (3 each) |

- **Machines have mouths.** A machine pulls from every belt that ends at its edge and pushes onto every belt that starts at its edge, taking turns and skipping full ones; a belt running alongside does nothing. No grabbing arms and nothing to turn.
- **Stickers cover belts.** A machine may go over any belt cells; the cells under its footprint go (their things hop into it if it wants them, else onward, else into the basket), every belt pointing into the footprint feeds it and every belt leading out takes its output. An oven dropped on a rock belt makes it a bar belt in one move. Towers and bowls sit on a belt cell and the belt runs under them. Only diggers may stand on rock cells, so a patch is always free for one; the pump only on the pond. A hedge may lie on rocks as a belt may (a digger placed there takes it back into the tray), so a fence can close a burrow's ring where its flowers grow on rocks. A ghost is grey only for reasons she can see: water, a tree, a burrow, the pad or the cells round it, another building, closed land, no rocks under a digger, no pond under the pump.
- **The ghost is the real sticker held up**: full colour at 85 percent, 1.06 times its size, a long shadow, a dashed footprint. It snaps to the nearest legal spot within 0.6 cell of where it is held (the hint's spot first); a muncher under it never greys it and hops aside with a giggle when the sticker lands. Lifting on grey flies the sticker back into the tray with a soft boing; lifting with the sticker's middle over the tray cancels (the finger may be in the tray holding a sticker over the board's bottom row).
- **State is motion, not icons**: a working machine's part moves on its notes of the band, a waiting one sits still with the thing it wants faint in its mouth, one with nowhere to put things stacks up to 4 on its roof and stops. A tap shows its recipe as pictures for 2 s.

### Belts

- **Drawing is the default**: with the pencil lit and no sticker picked, a drag draws a belt. Each cell the finger enters points to the next; a fast or diagonal finger is filled in with an L that keeps the current direction; the path keeps its row until the finger strays 0.85 cell from the row's middle line (never under 20 px), so a wobbly finger draws straight. At most 40 cells a stroke. Nothing is real until the finger lifts: the stroke is a see-through ghost whose end wears a sign (a mouth into a machine, a zip for a join, a hump for a bridge, a split arrow for a fork, a curl for rolling up, a stop sign). Each cell pops down with a rising tick.
- **One total stroke table** (`strokes.ts`): every cell a stroke touches is classified by what is there (ground, a straight belt, a bend, a fork, a join, a bridge, a building, a tower or bowl cell, a hedge, a stump, the board's edge) and what the stroke does on it (passes, turns, starts, ends), and each case has exactly one outcome: lay; re-point (same axis only, its things keep their places); bridge (only straight across a straight cell at a right angle); join (ending on a belt's side); feed (ending on a belt's tail); fork (starting from a straight belt's side, two or more cells from its end); carry on (starting on either of a dead end's last two cells); extend upstream (starting on a belt's first cell and drawing away behind it); roll up (going back over cells just drawn); take a hedge (the hedge returns to the tray and the belt leaves a gap in the line); or stop (the ghost ends a cell short under a stop sign). Out of a building a stroke starts a belt on the first cell past the footprint, rides one that already leads out, or bridges a plain straight belt running past that side (the bridge's lane is the building's spout), as it would one further on. Forgiving cases: a stroke ending one cell short of a machine's edge, pointing at it, steps into the mouth; one ending one cell past a belt it just crossed joins that belt; a wobbly trace of the hint's dotted path lays that path: it starts and ends where the path does, and at least two thirds of its cells off the footprints at the two ends are the path's. A way of her own between the same two buildings (a row kept free, the other side of a tree) lays as she drew it, and the hint leads on from there. A stroke never makes a head-on pair.
- **The helpful flip**: on lift, a belt between two buildings, or between a belt's side and a building, turns round when nothing its far end can use would ride it as drawn and something would ride it the other way; the turned chevrons show in the ghost before the lift. A stroke that re-points the belt it started on is never flipped.
- **The engine is slots and a hop.** One lane, two slots a cell (in and out), a bridge's over lane two more. Every belt hops half a cell together every 20 ticks, three hops a second on the eighth notes of the 90 bpm band: things ride 1.5 cells a second and a full belt carries 3 a second, so a digger line shows a thing every 3 cells and six diggers fill a belt. A hop is decided from the snapshot at its start by a memoised walk downstream: a thing moves if the tower or bowl on its cell takes it, or its successor is empty, empties this hop, or is a mouth with room, and it wins that slot's turn (joins take turns, straight first; forks alternate and send everything one way while the other is full). A full loop stands still, a loop with a gap turns whole, and the outcome never depends on the walk's order. Machines trade with belts only on the hop tick, so every machine starts and finishes on the band's eighth notes.
- **Not wanted now**: a thing at a belt's end that the machine does not want now waits and wiggles while the mouth shows what it wants. It counts as blocking when something the machine wants waits behind it, or when the machine never takes such a thing and the belt stands full behind it (gold bars on the gear oven's belt into the workshop: only Beep ever empties that belt); a bridge's hump into a mouth tells how long its thing has stood as a belt's end does; then after 5 s it becomes the hint, after 20 s more Beep lifts it away by itself (a carry like her tap's: while caught munchers wait for their tickle Beep stays with them, and the lift is credited all the same), and once Beep has lifted a jam's first blocker it lifts each further blocker of that jam every 2 s until the machine takes something. A belt ending on bare ground keeps its things nose to tail.
- **Taking back**: the peel hand dragged over belts rolls them up; with the peel, a tap on a belt lifts its run up to the next fork, join or building and a second tap rolls it up. Oops undoes edits from the last 60 s, at most 10, each one curling back toward the Oops button so she sees what it took; it restores the layout, never things (things on removed cells go where they are wanted, else into the basket), and greys out when there is nothing to undo.
- **Little mechanisms show the rules**: a fork is a paddle that flips side to side as it sends things each way, a join is a zip that closes a tooth per merge, things crossing a bridge ride 1.12 times bigger with a longer shadow, belt direction shows in rim ticks that point downstream and stay pointing when a jam stops the chevrons, a dead end wears an arrowhead cap and a belt's start a round cap.
- **Belt patterns** (one more per discovery, a tap on the lit pencil picks the next) are printed tone on tone in the belt body, looks only, for telling her bubble line from her bar line.

### Science

- **The factory sets the clock.** Each card costs about 5 minutes of what the bottle lines the hint has built by then can make, so a second green bottle maker visibly brings the next card sooner and a muncher napping on the bottle line visibly delays it. The economist's belt-level simulation showed the draft's small costs (128 green bottles in all, about 7 minutes of production) let her building speed set the clock: the cards clumped, the launch came at 11 to 18 minutes and a child who never defended lost nothing. Costs live in `catalog.ts` behind one cost-scale dial; the parent's Science switch (Normal, Quicker at 0.6) and the play test tune them.
- **The path**, one fixed order with one choice:

| # | Card | Needs | Brings | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Gold! | the rocket's legs full (20 blue bars) | digger, oven | the gold rocks bounce |
| 1 | Science! | the first gold bar | green bottle maker, lab | |
| 2 | Gears! | 5 green | workshop, digger, oven | the body (30 gears) |
| 3, 4 | Bubbles! and Hedges!, side by side | 80 green each | pump and 2 towers; 8 hedges | |
| 5 | Nose! | 90 green | tower, 3 hedges | burrow B says hello and wakes 2 minutes later; the nose (10 gears) |
| 6 | Stars! | 85 green | orange bottle maker | |
| 7 | Snacks! | 75 green, 20 orange | 2 bowls | |
| 8 | More! | 90 green, 45 orange | digger, oven, green maker, tower, bowl, 3 hedges | burrow C says hello and wakes 2 minutes later |
| 9 | Rocket! | 130 green, 40 orange | | the engine (40 orange bottles) |
| 10 | Fuel! | 180 green | | the launch, with the engine full |

- **Why these numbers**: each card is about 5 to 6 minutes of the bottle lines the hint has built by then (one green maker until More!, two after, the orange maker from Stars!), so no card keeps a child who defends waiting 8 minutes, and Fuel! is the long last card, where the waiting is the countdown's. Nose! brings no machine: the hint's plan opens its second window with it (the row along the top, the row along the bottom and the strip by burrow B, where no blue rock is free for a second green maker's line and every belt there is spoken for; the blueprint, The one next step). The nose takes gears like the body, so the workshop's belt fills it: on this board no gold line can reach the pad round the bubble line.

- **The one choice**: after Gears!, as its card closes, a card comes up in the middle with Bubbles! and Hedges! as two big pictures, bobbing, and Beep asks "Which one?"; her tap on one picks which one the lab works on first (a tap beside them makes them wiggle), and the other follows at the same cost. The lab card and the path view keep the two pictures too. It is the smallest real tree, with no wrong answer, and a "which one?" moment for the parent. The hint's hand points at the two pictures in turn and taps neither; the hint's own script and the scripted children pick Bubbles!. While the card is up it is the hint's one step, so nothing bounces behind it (no ghost, no New! in the tray). While the choice waits the lab works toward Bubbles! and her tap only changes the order, what it drank counting toward the card she picks: a child who never taps it is never stuck at Gears!.
- **The lab** is a night-indigo dome, a little planetarium whose stars light up as the current card fills, and the one dark sticker on the board. It drinks one bottle a second and takes every bottle at every time (bottles the card does not need yet go on its shelf and are drunk first when needed), so a mixed bottle belt never clogs; once Fuel! is found it drinks on from its shelf for no card, whatever it holds most of. The lab card shows the next card's picture and one jar per bottle kind that fills like a jar, never rows of outlines: a five-year-old counts to about 20, and 75 marks are noise.
- **No upgrades**: nothing ever gets faster, bigger, cheaper or wider. A card brings a new kind of thing, more stickers or a belt pattern.
- **The need comes before the tool**: the scout and a raid come before Bubbles!, a raid by burrow B before Snacks!.
- **The celebration**: the dome brightens once and its stars join into a picture of the new thing (nine star points: a gear's teeth, three bubbles, the rocket), a rising chime plays, a big card flips out of the lab to the middle with one picture and one word while Beep says the word aloud and jumps under falling confetti in item colours (nothing flashes). A tap, or 4 s, flies the new stickers into the tray, where the first one the chain needs wears "New!" and bounces. A card that comes when nobody touched the board for 30 s stays up until a tap; a card or the launch cut off by the page hiding plays again on return.

### The rocket

- **From minute 0 the pad shows the dotted rocket in four parts**: legs (20 blue bars, open at the start), body (30 gears, opens with Gears!), nose (10 gears, opens with Nose!) and engine (40 orange bottles, opens with Rocket!). Each part fills from the bottom like a jar, on the pad and on the rocket card, and pops on with a fanfare when full.
- **The climax is the whole factory at its loudest.** From Rocket! on, the pad drums and counts for every burrow as a machine on its flowers; Fuel! keeps the green lines feeding the lab while the orange line feeds the engine, so every line runs to the end. No finale monster.
- **The launch**: with the engine full and Fuel! found, no visit starts, every muncher out hops home, and the launch button bounces on the pad. A tap: Beep climbs into the window, the band hushes to a drum roll, every muncher comes to sit by the pad with a little flag, big number stickers count down from 5 (a tap on the rocket pops each), the rocket lifts slowly on rainbow puffs, never toward her, the board slides down, stars, the moon; Beep hops out with a flag, plants it beside the little flags of the flights before and pats it, Moony (a round moon critter) peeks up out of its crater and the two wave across the rocket, and Beep brings Moony aboard, both faces in the window; at home Moony sits on the pad's coral ring and waves. About 40 s. The launch is credited only once the flight lands, so a reload mid-countdown returns to the full rocket and the bouncing button.
- **After the launch every sticker is endless** (an infinity mark on its count) **and the rocket fills again**: free play becomes a kid-sized megabase that can grow as big as the board, and the munchers keep visiting. Once the flight lands, the new rocket on the pad opens its four parts again with the same things and counts, so the lines she built for the rocket run on and fill it again (about 7 to 8 minutes on the hint's own factory, the engine's orange line the slowest); when all four are full the launch button bounces again and a tap launches it again (Fuel! stays found, no new card), and each landing leaves one more little flag on the moon. ### Munchers

- **Who**: walking raspberries. A pear-shaped berry-pink body outlined by 12 to 14 round bumps, a green leaf calyx on top, a cream belly patch, feet hidden under the bumps, big sleepy-lidded eyes and a small soft smile; the side bumps puff out as the cheeks fill, one step for each thing eaten. No teeth, claws or frowns; they giggle, hop, pat full cheeks and burp. Each burrow's munchers wear their own small mark (a bow, freckles, a flower, a star, stripes, a diamond), so she can name them. Berry pink means muncher everywhere: nothing else on the board is pink. A round pink critter with a tuft would be another company's character.
- **Burrows** are mossy mounds with a round door lit warm and honey-gold inside, never a dark hole, each in a ring of purple flowers one cell deep (drawn as five-petal marks on a faint lavender tint, kept visible round any building standing on them). The burrow shows its hunger on its own mound: a nightcap on and a snore (0 to 399), the nightcap slipping (400 to 699), the tips of a calyx over the sill (700 to 899), the door open a crack with a whole sleepy face leaning out, blinking and yawning (900 to 999). Eyes alone in a doorway never appear: eyes watching from a dark hole frighten children of four to six. At 1000 comes the 5 s warning (the mound wobbles, "boing boing"), then they hop out 1 s apart.
- **What wakes them: the factory's song, loudest close by.** Each second a burrow's hunger grows by 4, plus 1 for each working machine on the board (up to 8), plus 5 for each working machine with a cell on its flowers, plus 2 for each within 2 cells of them; from Rocket! on the pad adds 5. A machine is working while a batch is in progress and nobody naps on it: the same thing the band plays and the eye sees. Hunger is frozen while the burrow's munchers are out or it sleeps, starts from 0 at each visit, and waits at 999 while the kind rules hold a visit back. Every working machine on or near a burrow's flowers sends that burrow a music note on its beat (big from on, small from near, at most one a machine a bar), and a sticker's ghost shows its notes before it is placed. A spreading smoke lost: it would lay a haze over 22 px things, and the counter is one sentence a parent can say.
- **The kind rules**: one visit at a time. A visit starts only when no muncher is out and the last one got home at least 60 s ago, the board was touched in the last 30 s, no card is up or closed in the last 20 s, the pause menu did not close in the last 20 s, the session is more than 2 minutes old, and the rocket is not full. B says hello when Nose! is found (its door opens a crack and a sleepy face waves for 4 s) and wakes 2 minutes later, C the same with More!. D, E and F, far out east, south-east and south, sleep with their nightcaps on and never visit yet: waking them by the song of the machines near their flowers, and the long walk home (40 to 65 cells, against the 60 s out), is open work (Left to build).
- **How many**: 1, plus 1 for each working machine on its flowers (at most 2), plus 1 when 8 or more machines work; at most 2 before Nose! and 3 after it (with 4 after More! a child who never defends could lose a card to naps for good: three burrows took turns on two green makers).
- **They love the fizz.** On waking, each muncher of a visit takes, in hop-out order, the nearest target by walking distance that no earlier one took, in this preference: a green or orange bottle maker (to nap on) or a bottle belt cell (to sit on); else the workshop or a gear belt cell; else an oven or a bar belt cell. It walks over every other belt without stopping. A sitting spot is a plain or bending belt cell with no tower, bowl or bridge on it. The economist's simulation showed that munchers stopping on the first bar belt they crossed cost nothing (bar belts carry twice what the makers use), and that going for the bottle line is what makes defending pay.
- **The havoc**, all in sight and nothing broken: a sitting muncher faces upstream, its cell neither gives nor takes, everything behind bunches up and the machines after it starve; every 1.5 s it takes the thing trying to enter, "nom nom". With 4 in its cheeks it burps and hops home; with nothing arriving for 5 s it walks on to its nearest untried target, and with none it waves and hops home. A napping muncher curls up on the roof for 45 s on a bottle maker and 20 s elsewhere (the long nap on a bottle maker is what a child who never defends loses most, and what a tower by the bottle line takes back at once): the machine stops, its part of the band turns into a silly kazoo of the same tune (so she hears which machine across the room), and it adds no hunger; on waking it takes one shiny thing (from the stack, else the input, never a rock) and hops home. Home is a straight bouncing hop over everything at 1 cell a second; the cheeks land on the burrow's heap by the door, drawn growing up to 12 and flagged from 6, and one tap on the heap brings everything back. A muncher out for 60 s goes home whatever it is doing. Munchers never touch the pad or the cells round it, the lab, the pump, towers, bowls, Beep, the basket, a rocket part or a discovery.
- **Walking**: 1.2 cells a second as grid steps (50 ticks a cell), along a shortest path recomputed from its own cell at each decision (waking, walking on, an edit, a finished nibble) over the 240 cells: belts, flowers and ground open; buildings, water, trees, other burrows and the pad's ring closed. A fence round a burrow holds them at the door, where a tickle or a tower gets them: walls buy time for towers.

Two munchers are easy; four outrun her, which is what towers are for.
- **Bubble towers**: every 1.5 s a tower with a bubble shoots a puff of suds at a muncher within 3.5 cells, sitting and napping ones first, then the nearest, reserving it so other towers pick another; the puff lands 0.4 s later on its nose, it sneezes its cheeks back, and it giggles home wearing a hat of suds at 1.5 cells a second; its burrow sleeps 30 s longer and the tower earns a star (up to 5). A tower holds 2 bubbles, 2 shots each, refilled from the belt under it; empty, its wand droops. Nobody floats away inside a bubble: that is another game's picture.
- **Snack bowls**: when a visit starts, each muncher first hops to its burrow's bowl. One that finds 2 gold bars has a picnic (eats them, waves, hops home; its burrow sleeps 30 s longer); one that finds it empty peers in, shrugs and goes looking for fizz. A burrow's first picnic makes it a friend: a heart flag goes up and its heap flies home. 2 bars a muncher keeps 2 to 4 raids after Snacks!, so the towers still matter at the climax.

- **Why the layout must plan for them**: the bottle line is what they come for, so it wants towers on its approaches; machines near the flowers bring visits sooner and bigger, and the best rocks touch the flowers; 14 hedges cannot ring the factory, and every belt through a hedge line leaves a gap that wants a tower; the pond is far west and the burrows east, so the bubble belt to the towers is the longest line of the game and the orange maker wants the same bubbles; a bowl needs a gold bar belt into muncher land. - **The havoc's size**, measured by the scripted children on the same seeds: a child who never defends (no tickles, towers, hedges or bowls, but fetching heaps when they bounce) launches at 1.15 to 1.30 times the careful child's time; a child who only tickles at most 1.10; the careful child at most 1.03 times a game with the munchers off; a slow child's never-defends ratio at most 1.25; and on every seed the careful child launches no later. - **The parent's switch**: Many; Few (hunger at half speed, nothing extra from the flowers, every visit 1 muncher; switched on mid-visit it sends all but the visit's first muncher home at once); Polite (they come out, wave, sit beside the bottle line and go home after 20 s, never eating, sitting on a belt or napping; tickles, towers and bowls still send them home with a giggle, towers still earn stars, bowls still make picnics and friends); None (the burrows snore all game; the defence cards still come, and the hint offers a tower, a hedge or a bowl only as its first New! ghost).

A part drops out when every machine of its kind has stopped; a napped-on machine's part plays as a kazoo, as loud as the line it replaces (the two bottle makers' kazoos an octave apart); a visit adds a cheeky pizzicato line, the drumming pad its big drum. Music off keeps each kind's part as one soft hit on its beat, so the factory still sounds alive, and a kazoo's whole line, since it tells her where a muncher sleeps.
- **The band leads the mix**: the machines' own small sounds (a batch, a push, a gulp, the lab's drink) go through a sprinkle about 9 dB down, halved while munchers are out, so the kazoo and the pizzicato are heard over a busy factory; through a tablet speaker's model and A-weighted, a green bottle's ding sits some 7 dB under a note of the tune, and the good sounds some 20 dB over it (`sounds.test.ts` holds the order). The levels are still to be tuned by ear on the iPad.
- Machine sounds wait for the next eighth note (at most two of a kind per eighth); every sound a tap makes plays at once. The band runs on the audio clock with a 100 ms lookahead and re-anchors to the sim after a pause or a slow frame; which parts play is a pure function of the state (`band.ts`). Key sounds: tink, rising ticks, slap, gulp, fwoomp, ding, the discovery chime, boing boing, nom nom, a-choo, giggle, burp, crunch, the countdown, a soft whoosh; the good ones loudest, none scary.

### Save, pause and sessions

- **The save** is one JSON object under `rocket-factory-2` in localStorage (version 2: the 60 x 36 board; a version 1 save of the 20 x 12 board stays untouched under its own keys, `rocket-factory` and `rocket-factory-old`, and is never read), its own shape (a Keep) and never the State: belt cells as one string per board row (36 rows of 60 cells), buildings as kind names with their contents by thing name, counts as {thing: count}, burrows' hunger and remaining sleep, the lab's progress and shelf, the rocket, the launches, the patterns found and the switches. Sticker counts are derived (granted by the path, the rocket and the launches, less what is placed), never stored, so a building that no longer fits is not placed and its sticker is in the tray by construction. Munchers, flights and Oops are not saved: each muncher's cheeks go onto its heap. Times are saved as seconds left, never as ticks. It is written every 2 s while anything runs and at once after an edit, a card, a rocket part, the end of a visit, a pause, a page hide and pagehide; read field by field (a bad cell is ground, a bad count 0, an unknown name dropped) and judged by the stroke table's own rules (`strokes.ts`: a belt cell where a stroke could lie, a building where its sticker could be placed, a hedge or a stump where her hedge tool could draw one, rocks too), never by copies of them; ids and letters are only ever added. A test steps from a load of the save and from the state itself (everyone sent home), through the new session's calm and 4 minutes past it with visits, and finds them equal every 5 s.
- **Start again** keeps her factories: behind its 3 s hold it puts the factory aside under `rocket-factory-2-old`, the last three newest first, and never one that found fewer cards and flew no more rockets than the newest kept, so a second hold on the young board keeps her real factory; the pause menu shows each with its picture and Bring back, which swaps it with the factory she has now. A curious five-year-old holds a filling ring just to watch it fill, and holds it again once she knows what it does. Behind a second 3 s hold the grown-ups find Keep a copy (the save as a dated file) and Bring back a copy (the factory now put aside first); a small Not saved says when the factory is not being kept (a test run, blocked storage, another tab's newer save). A tab whose save another tab has written since stops writing it, and takes the newer factory when it is next shown.
- **Sessions**: a return after 10 minutes or more away (hidden or paused) starts a new session: Beep wakes with a stretch, the machines rejoin the song kind by kind over 4 s, no visit for 2 minutes, the hint at once. Nothing runs while the game is closed and nothing catches up. The game pauses itself when the page hides, the window blurs, full screen ends, on a back-forward return, or untouched for 3 minutes while nothing works and no muncher is out, and for 10 minutes anyway: watching the belts is play here (no visit starts without a touch in the last 30 s). It takes no wake lock.
- **Good night is a tuck-in she does herself**: every muncher out sneezes its cheeks back and yawns home, a stroke or ghost is dropped, no visit can start and the band slows to a lullaby; dusk falls, things on the belts get tiny warm lights, and one kind of machine at a time wears a nightcap, the first of them bouncing (still the one thing that bounces): her tap tucks in every machine of that kind and its part leaves the song (after 8 s untapped it tucks itself in). When the last part is quiet Beep curls up beside the basket, and a still card shows her factory asleep, the rocket so far and today's cards as small pictures. The still card stays asleep: a tap on it makes Beep murmur in its sleep and a z float up, nothing more. Morning is the grown-up's Keep going in the pause menu (its button stays over the card), or the next session's start card (a reload, or a return after 10 minutes away); every morning is a new session, and a return after 10 minutes to a night still falling is morning too. No card is found during the night: one whose need is met at dusk comes in the morning. The next session's start card shows the next card's picture.
- **The pause menu**: Keep going, Good night, Sound, Music, Voice, Munchers (Many, Few, Polite, None, as pictures with their word under each), Science (Normal, Quicker: one bottle, and one rushing along), Full screen, More games, and Start again behind its hold, with Bring back and its picture for each factory put aside, and For grown-ups behind a second hold.
- **URL switches**, which may read the save and never write it: `?demo=<scene>`, `?nosave`, `?seed=<n>`, `?autoplay` (a scripted child plays), `?debug` (the grid, slots, paths, hunger, hit areas, the frame rate), `?sheet=<group>`, `?fast=<n>`, `?all`, `?munchers=many|few|polite|none`.

### Engineering

- **The whole game is a pure state** stepped at 60 Hz (`createState`, `act`, `step`, `cloneState`), driven by one `Action` union; the browser glue (`game.ts`, `touch.ts`) turns pointers, keys and HUD buttons into actions. Derived arrays (the slot successor graph, cell owners, flower rings, the board's paths) live in the State and are rebuilt by one `rebuild(state)` after every edit and every load; nothing lives outside the State between steps.
- **Determinism across engines**: no sim rule draws a random number (every choice falls by cell, id or turn; the scripted children and the demos have generators of their own), and looks take their variety from a hash of an id and the tick inside the draw code. Transitions use integers, `+ - * /`, `Math.floor` and squared distances, never `sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `pow`, `exp` or `hypot`, whose last bits differ between V8 and JavaScriptCore; the picture points the sim reads (a muncher's, where its hops start; a thing's, the cell it is sent from) keep to that too (`where.ts`). Every speed is a whole number of ticks per cell.
- **Belt slots** (section Belts): per cell its in side and out side (a bend is stored as drawn, never guessed), a fork side, a bridge's over direction, join and fork turn bits, a moved count for the chevrons and a blocked-since stamp for each lane (the main lane and a bridge's hump; saved) for the hint and Beep's lifts; slots 2c (in) and 2c+1 (out) and 480+2c, 481+2c for an over lane. Successors are rebuilt after every edit; a fork's out slot has two and never feeds a side join; a side join lands mid-cell and takes turns with the cell's own in slot.
- **Edits go through one pure pair**: `planStroke(state, path)` and `planPlace(state, kind, cell)` give the ghost, its snapped spot and its end sign, and `applyEdit(state, edit)` commits and returns the inverse for Oops. The ghost, the commit, the hint's dotted paths, the scripted children and the tests all call the same functions, so the end sign never lies. The whole stroke table is tested case by case, with a network lint after every case (no head-on pair, at most three predecessors, no fork into a join).
- **Drawing**: pixel ratio capped at 2; over the cells in view (never the whole 60 x 36 board), per size and region, the print is baked once and the layout (belts with their patterns, mouths, spouts, hedges, footprint shadows) is re-baked after an edit; cells that pop in or curl away are drawn live until their stamp runs out and then baked; building bodies are stamped every frame from one bitmap per kind and size with their squash (on the board their sticker shadow in it); chevrons are one path; things are stamped from one bitmap per kind and size wherever their picture holds still (riding a belt, on a roof, a heap, in the basket, a cheek or Beep's arms, faint in a mouth or a rocket part). Every draw function runs on the checking fake context, and `?sheet=<group>` lays out every drawing. A headless Chromium check at 4x CPU slowdown on a busy factory with 4 munchers out and the hint's hand showing (`?sheet=scenes`, its scene of that name) holds the frame under 12 ms at p95.
- **Tests, in a budget the deploy can carry**: `npx vitest run rocket-factory` runs well inside a minute (about 20 s on the 12-core dev machine, some 150 s of test time across its cores) and holds every rule alone, the stroke table, a belt property test over random networks with forks, joins, bridges and loops (things conserved, never two in a slot, none stuck with room ahead, a full loop still, a loop with a gap turning, the same moves in any walk order) against a slow reference that moves one thing at a time, a ledger checked on every hop tick (everything dug equals what is on belts, in buildings, used, in the rocket, drunk or shelved, in cheeks, on heaps and in the basket), determinism over 10 minutes and across a save and load (through the new session's calm and 4 minutes past it, visits and all), the careful child with the finger to Gears! at 1024x768, 1180x820 and 820x1180, the pure children to the launch, one seed each (careful at every pace, the never-defending child at table pace, the careful child with the munchers off), a chaos child and a belts-everywhere child for 5 minutes from a fresh game and from the busy factory, a fuzz of 20 seeds of 10 minutes, the hint leading on from boards the chaos child made (three moments, one seed) and from the board it once gave up on (`repair.test.ts`), the cases no random child reaches (a hedge taken back under a nibble, a hedge on rocks across a load, a tap on what her other hand took away), the board lint (`board.test.ts`: every one of the six doors reaches the core, rings whole, patches take diggers, ponds take the pump; a tidy factory in the start block with every sticker fits), the palette test, every draw function, and two benchmarks held to bounds loose enough for a busy runner (`speed.test.ts`, a mean step under 300 microseconds under the test runner; a hint look under 25 ms), which only a hot path gone badly wrong breaks. Every long test's timeout is at least three times its run on a quiet machine, so a runner busy with every game's tests at once stays green. With `FACTORY_LONG=1` come the heavier runs: the finger child's whole game at 1024x768 and its first Nose! at the other two screens, the statistics (the havoc ratios as medians over 10 seeds of every child, the pacing windows), 200 fuzz seeds, the chaos boards on six seeds from seven moments to the launch, and the benchmarks' working bounds on a quiet machine (a mean step under 60 microseconds under the test runner, against the target of 20 in plain node; a look under 5 ms).
- **The scripted children** play whole games through the real hit test and pointer router, with a finger measured in millimetres (taps scatter 2.5 mm, one in ten lands 5 to 8 mm off, taps last 0.1 to 1 s and slide up to 4 mm, strokes wobble 4 mm to their very end (the sway fading in over the first half cell, as a finger lands where she means), a carried sticker is aimed by the sticker held above the fingertip and lands with a tap's scatter, one drag in twenty loses contact for 50 to 120 ms, on one stroke in ten the heel of the hand lands 40 to 60 mm below the fingertip 0.05 to 0.3 s before it and slides with it, a thumb rests on the glass in one session in five, a frantic tapper hits at munchers every 0.4 s during visits), at the three iPad full screens, Safari's 1024x662, 1180x710 and 820x1070, and a 750x320 phone. No touch aimed at a muncher may draw, place or peel. Profiles: eager (2 to 8 s pauses, launch 45 to 55 minutes), table (4 to 14 s pauses and 15 to 60 s of watching after one step in five; first factory by 3:00, Gears! by 6:00, Bubbles! 8:30 to 11:00, the first Nose! 18:00 to 22:00, launch 50 to 60), slow (5 to 20 s pauses, 20 to 90 s of watching after one step in three; launch 55 to 70); each careful, never-defending, never-fetching or tickle-only. Every turn touches the board, whether or not the child takes the hint's step: a child who skips the defence still plays, and the kind rules (a touch within 30 s) must see her there. Every seed holds the orders (the scout and a raid before Bubbles!, a raid by burrow B before Snacks!); every child who defends at all, and every game with the munchers off, holds never 8 minutes without a new card or a rocket part and never 60 s with the lab and the pad both starved (the child who never defends is slower by the havoc itself, which the ratios measure).
