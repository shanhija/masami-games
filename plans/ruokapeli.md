# Monster Lunch (`ruokapeli/`)

The brief is [`ruokapeli/design.md`](../ruokapeli/design.md), the inventor's own words (she is five): a plate to fill with food from the ground, a pole that knocks all the food off, water, a choice of hamburger or croissant and of what goes inside, pink, green and blue gates opened by buttons of the same colour, poop not to pick up, and a monster on a track fed from a hot air balloon with a slot in its floor. Sami asked for a good game for a five-year-old built on her words, the open parts invented.

## Decisions

- **A 2D course seen from above at a slant, on the canvas.** A different kind of play from the repo's side-view platformers (Pia Jump, Super Me, Chase): walking about, timing poles, a little gate puzzle. The finale is a side view. Her other game, Slime Guy (`limaukkopeli/`), is a 3D obstacle course; this one stays 2D so the two feel different.
- **Five courses, five monsters.** Park, Pond, Garden, Farm, Castle, each with its own hungry monster on the course's card (Munch, Gulp, Nibble, Chomp, Gobble). The Park is her course in her order, every thing once and easy: foods, one slow spinner, water beside the path and a little bridge, a pink, a green and a blue gate with each button just before its gate, poop, the balloon. Each later course adds one thing: Pond narrow bridges and poles by the water, Garden hedge rooms where one pink button opens two pink gates and a spinner turns on the fountain, Farm a puppy that leaves poops and a slider in a gap of the fence, Castle gates that close again (10, 9 and 8 seconds) and two spinners in a row. A course opens when the one before is done; after the fifth, once, a party with all five monsters.
- **The pile is the dish.** No recipe to read and no order to follow: every filling she picks lies about the course, the pile shows what she has, and the bread at the balloon makes it a burger or a croissant. Four fillings of each dish to start; each of the first four courses done opens one more of each (all eight are open for the castle), with a "New!" on it until she has seen it in the kitchen.
- **Nothing ends a course early, nothing is a game over.** Losses are foods: a pole's knock costs time (re-pick what landed on the ground), the water costs what is on the plate, a zap costs the top food. Every course ends at the balloon and every finale ends with a happy monster. Stars (one to three) count the fillings the monster ate against the foods the course had: 80 percent or more three stars, 40 percent two, else one. Breads count for nothing, a poop is spat out.
- **Controls a five-year-old already has.** Touch and hold where she should walk: she walks toward the finger, from where her feet are drawn (the camera keeps her near the middle, so it works like pointing). Arrows or WASD on a keyboard. In the finale the monster rolls toward the finger, or with left and right. The first course and its finale show a ghost hand. A child points at the balloon across the lake, so the teeter lasts 0.8 seconds: long enough to see the windmilling and let go.
- **Fair hazards.** A pole's shadow on the ground is exactly where it hits. Thrown foods prefer land (a landing spot in water is drawn again once) and never land in hedges, gates or posts. A gate that closes on her pushes her out without a zap. Spinners never sweep a wall, a button, a flag, the start or the balloon. The puppy never poops beside a food or beside her. No food lies past the balloon: walking into the balloon ends the course.
- **The finale is a catch.** The balloon sways on a fixed, uneven curve inside the track; the dish falls from the slot under it a layer at a time, the bottom bread first and the top bread last, quicker toward the end (1.7 to 1.15 seconds apart). A shadow on the track shows where each layer comes down; the monster's top speed (8 tiles a second) reaches any drop in time.
- **Proof without playing.** The sims are plain numbers stepped at 120 Hz and cloned with `structuredClone`. Each course is linted (everything reachable, each gate from its button's side, nothing past the balloon, nothing on water, poles clear) and a bot walks it like a careful child, picking up every food with no knock, no splash and no zap; a catcher eats every layer of the finale at the monster's speed; the renderer draws every course and finale on a checking fake context.

## The look

Its own: a sunny picnic. Chocolate-brown ink round everything in the play layer, juicy saturated food with a white shine, flat lit tops and darker sides on anything that stands (seen from above at a slant), soft oval shadows on the ground, turquoise water with moving white ripples, and red-and-white gingham in the HUD. Foods have no faces; faces are for the hero, the monsters and the animals. The rules are at the top of `ruokapeli/src/draw/look.ts`.

## Left to build

- Watch her play and tune by what she does: the teeter time near water, how fast the poles turn, the drops' pace in the finale, the zoom (about 10.5 tiles fit the screen's height), whether follow-the-finger needs a joystick instead.
- Listen: nobody has heard the sounds yet. The mix is balanced by numbers only; the farm's banjo, the castle's horns, the basket's creak and the burp first.
- The frame rate on the real iPad. The ground is baked in chunks, but every visible water tile draws its ripples every frame; if the Pond in portrait drops frames, bake the ripples into a few animated frames.
- Ask her about "ihminen heiluttaa monsteria": the game reads it as the player steering the monster; she may have meant waving to it.
- The synthesiser's envelopes, noise and bells repeat the other games' approach; a shared module all games import would remove the copies.
