# Silly Billy

A 3D room full of people, a cat and things, seen from high up like Castle Builder. A child calls an avatar by name and says what to do, in Finnish or in English as the pause card is set: "Aino, heitä pallo Eetulle". What the game already knows happens at once. What it does not know, a fast model (Haiku) works out, and a designer model (Opus) writes as code while the avatar visibly thinks it through; the new action or thing is tested in a sandbox, joins the library on disk, and is instant the next time. New avatars are made and dressed the same way: "tee uusi tyttö", "anna hänelle iso kihara tukka". The brief is Sami's [`silly-billy/design.md`](../silly-billy/design.md).

## Decisions

- **Name**: Silly Billy, the folder's name.
- **Language** (Sami, 2026-11-08): the pause card's setting, Suomi or English, Finnish by default, rules everything the child hears and sees: what Whisper listens for and its prime, every bubble, the line of what was heard, the job cards, the pause card and the voices. For this game it replaces the repo rule that audience-visible words are simple English, and the old English bubbles with a Finnish voice. Each answer is also spoken aloud by the browser's voice for the language when it has one, because six-year-olds do not read. Words for the child are `{ en, fi }` (`engine/lang.ts`'s `Words`), picked when shown or said; a switch renames the starting avatars at once, clears the bubbles still up and hushes the voice. In each language the starting avatars answer to that language's names and calls and to their ids. Ids, kinds, colours, parts and the interpreter's `about` texts stay English: the child never sees them.
- **Models through the Claude Code CLI on the laptop's subscription**, as in belay-that: no API key. The CLI runs from a **Vite dev-server plugin** (`silly-billy/src/server/plugin.ts`, wired in the root `vite.config.ts`, `apply: 'serve'`), so `npm run dev` is all Sami starts and the page reaches it as `./api/` over the same https origin. The static build (the hosted page) has no server: it plays the library it shipped with and the small parser, and invents nothing.
- **Hearing**: push-to-talk (hold the big button or Space). With the dev server, Whisper large-v3-turbo through belay-that's whisper.cpp build on the GPU (`~/src/belay-that/vendor`, overridable with `SILLY_WHISPER_BIN` and `SILLY_WHISPER_MODEL`), in the language of the setting, port 8179, started on the first status request or on the first words after the server's code reloaded. Every clip, live and final, is asked with every field set: the language, the page's prime (`voice/prime.ts`: the names of who is here, each told to do something; the server has its own per language), temperature 0 with no fallback, a beam of 5. Letters the language does not write are folded to its own unless a name here has them, inventions on silence are dropped and loops cut (measured below). Without Whisper, the browser's recognizer (`fi-FI` or `en-US`). Typing for the parent. `?say=` and `window.game.hear(text)` feed the same path for tests and screenshots.
- **Understanding**: the normalised words are looked up first among phrases heard before (`library/phrases.json`), then the small parser (`brain/parse.ts`) tries: one known avatar (or "she/he/kaikki"), one known action by its Finnish stems, its params filled by known things. Confident: done, no model. Otherwise Haiku (`--model haiku --effort low`, one process per command with the next one already started, structured output) returns a Plan (`engine/protocol.ts`) with its lines in both languages. A misheard name goes to the closest-sounding avatar; misunderstanding is part of the fun, so the interpreter always does something. The parser is sure only of what it matched exactly or by a Finnish ending: a verb or thing one letter off a known word ("lennä" is not "kävele", "kaiva" is not "kasva"), an unknown word left over with a case ending ("trampoliinilla"), or a name no param took sends the words to Haiku, so new verbs and things get made instead of played as the wrong basic one (round 1 of review: 9 of 100 everyday verbs had been played sure and wrong). A plan that comes back after the child said something newer to the same actors leaves them alone ("seis" holds), changes no room when the room changed or anyone got newer words meanwhile, ends "(sanoit jo jotain uutta)" ("(you said something newer)") when nothing of it is left, and is not kept as a phrase when it was cut short (round 2). The page waits at most 45 s for Haiku and 90 s for a job (every round together); past that whoever waits shrugs and the request is called off, and the dev server stops the CLI turn when the page stops waiting.
- **Inventing**: for each new item the plan carries Haiku's rough draft (2 to 5 boxes, balls, cylinders and cones), which appears at once; Opus (`--model opus --effort low`, one process per invention, all in parallel) writes the model code, and the draft is swapped for the model with a sparkle. For each new action Opus writes generator code against the engine's API while the avatar thinks: it says "Hmm, mietitäänpä!" in its bubble and aloud ("Let me think this through!" in English, the brief's words), a thought cloud with turning cogs fills as the designer's reply streams, the HUD's job card says "Mietitään: <name> 12 s" with the name in the game's language (then "Kokeillaan", "Yritetään uudestaan"; a thing says "Piirretään"; in English "Thinking this through", "Trying it out", "Trying again", "Drawing"), and the plan's own answer ("Watch me!") waits until the made action starts. A thing given to a built-in verb that nobody listed as new (Haiku's "wear magic-wand" on an empty library, also as wear's and make's text `thing`) is added to the plan's new things by the server, so it is made instead of shown as a mystery box; a room, the ground, the sky, an avatar's name or body type is never listed. When the speaker's first step is a say, the plan's own answer is not said: the line itself is the answer, and an "Okei!" before it was cut off at once. Measured 2026-11-07 on the CLI: Haiku answers in about 3 s, Opus writes a 40-line action in about 6 s.
- **Code, not a fixed set of primitives** (the brief): an action is `function* (me, args, w)` with full control of positions, every joint, items, physics, particles and sounds through a small API (`engine/api.ts`, described for the designer in `engine/apidoc.ts`); a thing's model is `function (m)` over a modelling kit (`engine/kit.ts`: boxes, spheres, cylinders, tori, capsules, lathes, extrusions, tubes, named moving parts, anchors); a thing may have a process `function (it, w, dt)`. The basic verbs and the built-in things are TypeScript against the same API, on equal footing.
- **Sandboxed and tested before it is taken in**: the server parses the designer's code (Vite's `parseAst`), refuses code that names anything but its own names, its params and a short list of plain built-ins, or reaches for keys like `constructor`, `prototype`, `__proto__` or `getPrototypeOf` (round 1 of review escaped the first sandbox through `({}).constructor.constructor`), checks keys worked out at run time (`__k`, naming the line of a refused one), and puts a guard call at the top of every loop and function body; a refusal or a syntax error goes straight back to the designer. The page runs new code first in a **disposable validation worker** against a copy of the world for up to 20 simulated seconds (killed after 4 s of real time), an action a second time with its optional args left out, a thing's process for a second lying about and half a second worn (a worn thing is a stand-in in no list of the room's things), timed against the budget it gets in play: an error, a loop without yield or an empty model is sent back to the designer once with the error to fix; a thing that works but whose kit notes say shapes were lost plays at once and goes back once with the notes. Only code that passed is saved and run, and the server guards it afresh when it saves it and when it reads the library (a file guarded by an older guard is rewritten); `server/shipped.test.ts` reads the committed library through the store before every build, so stale files are rewritten there too (and belong in the commit), and fails on a file the store would leave out. At play time made code runs in strict mode with the page's globals shadowed, the guard reset before every call (a turn budget and a 250 ms clock), a real-time budget per action and per kind's process, every error caught (the action ends with a shrug) and every number it wrote made finite and kept in the room. The dev server's API answers only the game's own page (403 for another site's). Generated code never runs on the laptop's Node side.
- **The library on disk**: `silly-billy/library/{items,actions,avatars}/<id>.json` and `library/phrases.json`, written by the dev server, read back after a reboot; an action or a thing keeps its Finnish name. The build ships what is there (`import.meta.glob`; `brain/shipped.ts` leaves out phrases without a Finnish line), so things made at home play on the hosted page too. `library/log.jsonl` (git-ignored) logs every utterance, plan, invention and timing for the parent.
- **The look**: Castle Builder's camera (its yaw, pitch and 30° lens, imported from `castle-builder/src/scene/look.tsx`) over a diorama slab per room, flat-shaded low-poly solids, one warm sun with soft shadows and a hemisphere fill, a sky-blue background. With nobody busy the frame keeps 15 m of ground around the room's middle (Castle Builder keeps 24). When someone does something, the camera moves in on them, on the actors, things and places their step names (and the step a thinking actor waits for), on anyone playing a reaction and on loose things still flying faster than 1.5 m/s, down to 6 m of ground (`scene/frame.ts`), and goes back to the whole room 2.5 s after everyone is idle: at the whole room a kid is about 30 px tall on a laptop and a thrown ball is a dot, which a six-year-old cannot follow. When a made thing's real model replaces its draft, the camera shows it for 3 s (by then everyone is often idle again). The lens, yaw and pitch stay Castle Builder's.
- **Rooms**: the playground (grass, sand, slide, swings, seesaw, sandbox, climbing frame, benches, trees, balls), the swimming pool (tiles, the pool you can wade in, diving board, loungers, parasols, swim rings, a beach ball, rubber ducks, palms) and the forest (pines and leafy trees, rocks, logs, stumps, big red mushrooms, a tent, a campfire, a pond, pinecones and sticks). Buttons and voice ("mennään metsään") switch; each room keeps its things.
- **The cast**, each with a name in each language and the same id in both: Aino / Alice (a girl with a long ginger ponytail and a purple top), Eetu / Steve (a boy with a red cap and a blue shirt), Mikko / Mike (a tall grown-up with glasses and a green jumper), Noora / Nora (a girl with blonde pigtails and a pink dress) and Mirri / Luna the orange cat. New avatars join them and stay, named from a list for each language; a made avatar's name, and any name the child gives, stays in both languages.
- **Basic verbs** (built in, a Finnish name and Finnish stems in `calls`, every line in both languages): walk, run, come, pick-up, drop, give, jump, wave, sit, sleep, spin, say, follow, look, make, remove, wear, take-off, paint, grow, shrink, rename. Everything else (throw, ride, dance, swim, climb, kick...) is invented.
- **Group games** (Sami, 2026-11-08, after playing: "this is quite simple and slow. can you add something that they could do together? like play tag, football, etc?"): five more built-in actions in which several avatars play together, `content/games.ts`: tag (hippa), football (jalkapallo, with two goals put up for the game), hide and seek (piilosta), a race (kilpajuoksu, a swimming race in the pool) and a train (juna, follow the leader). The step's actor starts it and has the first part ("it", the seeker, the train's front); its `players` text arg names the others, and empty is everyone here. A game takes them in with `w.join`, which now ends what they were doing as a command would (off the swing, up from the bench), and lets them go when it ends or is stopped ("kaikki seis" stops the starter's step); a command of their own takes one out (the game then sees it `busy`) and plays on without it. Each lasts about 25 to 50 s and ends on its own. Built in like the basic verbs, they play at once, offline and in the hosted build too.
- **Nothing hurts**: the interpreter turns rough requests into something silly and kind; a hit is a bonk, a tumble and a giggle.

## The engine (`engine/`)

**World** (`types.ts`): plain data; the Sim keeps the running generators in a side map keyed by actor id. `createWorld` places each room's layout (`content/layouts.ts`) as items `#i1`, `#i2`... (fixed ones `fixed: true`), the current room's in `items`, the others in `away`, and stands the actors on the room's spawn spots facing +z (toward the camera). `World.lang` is the language it is played in; `useLang` sets it and gives each starting avatar the child has not named (`Actor.names` not null) that language's name and calls, its id among them.

**Stepping** (`Sim.step`, 1/60 s): `t += STEP`, then for each actor: if `joinedBy` is set, skip its own task; else if it has no doing and its queue's front step's action is in the library, start it (resolve args, make the generator, push a `started` event, clear `thinking`); if the front step's action is not in the library yet, the actor waits in the think pose (`thinking` stays). A running generator gets one `next()` per step inside try/catch with `resetGuard()` before; done → `done` event and `rest`; thrown → `failed` event with `describeError`, then the `shrug` reaction. A step runs at most 60 s, then it is ended. Ending a step (done, failed, stopped, replaced, timed out) releases joined actors (`joinedBy = null`), gets the actor off anything it rides unless the action left it riding on purpose (a 'ride' that ends riding stays riding until the next command: keep it simple, get off), and eases the pose to rest over 0.3 s. Then items: held items follow the hand frame (`body.handFrame`, the item's 'hand' anchor at the hand, upright, turned with the actor's yaw, plus the item's own pitch and roll while held), hugged ones the `hugFrame`, worn ones are stand-in items outside the room (in the Sim's side map, not in `items`), placed at their slot every frame so their process runs and the action's handle stays live (a worn wand turns its star); their parts are published on `Actor.wornParts[slot]` for the scene, not in the look, so a saved look never changes with them (`Sim.wearItem`, `takeStandIn`, `putItem`, `removeItem(id, keepHandle)`), ridden ones carry their rider (the rider's hips on the 'seat' anchor through the part chain, its yaw the item's), item processes (`tick`) run in try/catch (an item kind whose tick throws 3 times, or runs over its real-time budget of about 4 ms a frame plus 500 ms, loses its tick; an action over about 8 ms a frame plus 500 ms fails with "it took too long"), then physics, puffs (move, gravity, drop when old; at most 600), bubbles and moods run out.

**Physics** (loose items): gravity 9.8, ground at `groundAt` (the pool's floor under water) but a thing over water floats with its bottom at `WATER_Y`, bobbing; bounce on the ground with restitution `bouncy` (0.2 → 0.85), rolling friction; walls at ±(ROOM_HALF - radius) bounce; solid items (circles of their footprint radius) push loose things out and bounce them; an actor's body (a capsule from feet to head, its radius) bounces a loose thing that hits it and raises a `hit` event (once per throw) so actions can react; a thing at rest (speed < 0.05 on the ground for 0.3 s) stops being loose. Thrown things spin.

**Actors move only through the API**: nothing walks by itself except the idle life (blink, breathe: a tiny bob, look around now and then, turn toward the camera when idle for long). Actors keep out of each other and out of solid items when walking (`walkTo`, runtime.ts over `path.ts`: straight at the goal while no solid thing is in the line, else along a way planned round the solid things, A* on a 0.2 m grid with each thing grown by the walker's radius, pulled straight by line of sight; a field of clearances is kept per set of things and a grid per walker size, so a plan takes about 0.05 ms and happens only when the goal is out of sight, has moved 0.75 m or the walker has stalled; each frame it steps round other actors, turning as little as it must, never into a thing and never deeper into an actor it overlaps; after a second without progress it plans round the actors standing in its way; on the last stretch it stops when no step brings it nearer: a goal inside or behind a thing, the room's edge, or others standing at the goal (as near as they allow, going round them when that is 0.3 m nearer); starting inside a thing it walks out the way that leads on; it gives up after 4 s without progress or after twice its way's walking time and 4 s, at least 12 s and at most 50 s). Things one can step onto, and water, stay walkable. Actors wading in water move at 60%. Keep everyone inside the room.

**Args** at a step's start, by the action's param kind: 'actor' → the actor by id, name or calls, else null; 'item' → an instance id ('#i3') if it exists, else the kind (via `library.itemKindFor`) → the nearest free instance in the room, else a new one made 1.5 m in front of the actor (a second item argument 4 m in front, so there is room to act); an unknown word → null; 'thing' → actor first, then item; 'place' → 'x,z' or a thing's position as a Spot; 'text' → the string; 'number' → a finite number or null.

**Runtime** (`runtime.ts`): one ActorApi per actor and one ItemApi per item (cached by id, dropped when the thing goes), a WorldApi per Sim, implementing `api.ts` exactly as `apidoc.ts` says. Setters clamp: position within the room (±ROOM_HALF), y within [-2, 30], scale [0.2, 4], angles finite; `pose` is the live object, cleaned after every step (non-finite → rest value, angles to ±2π, squash [0.3, 2], bob [-1, 5]). `feel` takes any word: a known mood, or the nearest by a small table ('confused' → 'surprised', 'focused' → 'happy', 'excited' → 'happy', 'tired' → 'sleepy', 'cross' → 'angry', 'shy' → 'love'), else 'happy'. `sound` maps unknown names to 'pop' ('cheer' → 'yay', 'laugh' → 'giggle', 'splash' stays). Helpers: `walkTo` (walk cycle on the legs and empty arms, 0.6 rad at 2.4 steps/s scaled by speed; `run` doubles the speed and the cycle), `turnTo` (at 6 rad/s), `pickUp` (walk to `radius + item.radius + 0.2`, bend, the item to the hand; a 'hug' item fills both hands; a 'no' item says "Too big!" ("Liian iso!") and fails softly), `jump` (a real arc with gravity, squash before and after, forward motion), `poseTo`/`rest` (ease from the current values), `ride`/`sitOn`/`getOff`, `wear` (into `look.worn[slot]`: worn things are part of the look, not items in the room; the slot's old thing goes in a sparkle, since old hair lying at the feet looked like a mistake; slot defaults to the item's meta.wear, else 'hand'; a worn 'hand' thing sends one-hand things to the left hand, `Runtime.handFor`), `takeOff` (the very same item comes back, its state and parts too), `tumble`, `speak(words, secs)` (the bubble shows 48 characters, the voice says the whole line up to 200; the same line in the same step is not started again while it lasts (`Side.voiced`), at most two lines a frame from each actor and eight in all, so "kaikki sanokaa hei" is everyone's hello; the built-in say uses it). `wear` takes the thing out of the hand that held it before it frees the right hand, so a ball in the right hand moves to the left. An item's `.wearer` is the actor wearing it (a worn thing has no `.holder`). `turnTo` and `me.yaw = v` while riding turn the ridden thing; while sitting they turn nothing and cost one frame. Writing 0 to a speed of a thing that is not loose changes nothing (a placed thing stays where code put it; only a non-zero speed sets it loose). Small actors in water deeper than 0.6 of their height swim at the surface, head out. `w.make` creates any kind in the library, a mystery box for an unknown one. `w.throw` computes a ballistic launch from the item's current position to land at the target's body centre (or the spot), with flight time from distance/speed and the arc, releases it from the hand, and returns the flight time. `w.together` steps several generators each frame until all finish. `w.join` takes the other actor in as a command would (`Sim.enlist`): what it was doing, had queued or was reacting ends, and it is `joinedBy` this one until the action ends; code that moves another actor without it joins it too, but then its own doing only waits and goes on after. A command of its own (`Sim.give` replacing, `stop`, a reaction: the child spoke to it) takes a joined actor out: the step it left is kept (`Side.left`) and cannot take it in again, and to that step's code it is `busy`. `say(words, secs)` and `speak` take `{ en, fi }` or a plain string (a name, a number, the child's words) and show the game's language, picked when said; any other object shows nothing. An item's `.name` is its `nameFi` in Finnish. Setting an actor's `.name` sets `names` to null, so the name stays in both languages; the same name again changes nothing.

**Reactions** (`Sim.react`): short built-in generators for 'shrug' (palms up, "Hmm?"), 'yay' (a hop with arms up, "Yay!" / "Jee!"), 'huh' (head tilt, "Huh?" / "Häh?"), 'hello' (a wave, "Hi!" / "Hei!"), 'think' (hand on chin while waiting for an invention, "Hmm..."). The one playing is `Actor.reacting`, which the camera reads.

**Library** (`library.ts`): built-ins first; `addItem` compiles `guarded` (and `tickGuarded`) through `sandbox.ts`, runs the model in a Kit (`buildRecipe`) and stores the recipe, or the draft recipe (`roughRecipe`) when `code` is null; an entry that fails keeps the previous version and returns the error; a stored entry's `nameFi` is its own, else its first call, else its name. `itemKindFor`/`actionFor` match a word to an id, a name in either language or a call: lowercase, exact, or the word starting with a call of 4+ letters followed by a Finnish ending (`lle`, `lla`, `llä`, `lta`, `ltä`, `n`, `a`, `ä`, `aa`, `ää`, `ssa`, `ssä`, `sta`, `stä`, `seen`, `han`, `hin`, `ksi`, `t`, `in`, `ja`, `jä`, `ita`, `itä`, `ineen`, `ni`, `si`, `kin`), or an edit distance of 1 for words of 5+ letters that keeps the first letter. `matchWordForm` says how a word matched (exact, ending or near); `itemKindFor` and `actionFor` take `{ near: false }` and `Library.matchForm` exists, so the parser can be sure only of exact and ending matches. The mystery box for unknown kinds: a 0.4 m purple box with a yellow "?" made of boxes.

**Kit** (`kit.ts`): `m.part(name, fn)` works without opts; a part name used twice makes a copy at its own pivot (`light0~2`, with `PartDef.pose = 'light0'`, so `it.part('light0')` moves every copy); at most 120 parts, and past that what is made inside a part keeps its place (the part's pivot is added to its shapes and anchors, `body.ts`'s `anglesXYZ`) and only stops moving; `parseColor` takes every CSS colour name (from Maps, so 'constructor' is no colour), `#rgba`, `#rrggbbaa`, `rgb()` and `hsl()`, but `BASIC_COLORS`, the game's own 56 names, is all `content/colors.ts`'s `colorOf` takes from a child's words, with the Finnish stems and `#hex` ('seashell' is a thing, 'peru' no colour).

**Sandbox** (`sandbox.ts`): `new Function('__g', ...SHADOWED, '"use strict"; return (' + guarded + ')')` called with the guard and `undefined` for each shadowed name: window, self, globalThis, document, fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, EventSource, localStorage, sessionStorage, indexedDB, caches, navigator, location, history, postMessage, importScripts, Worker, SharedWorker, setTimeout, setInterval, requestAnimationFrame, queueMicrotask, alert, open, close, Function, performance, top, parent, frames, opener, Reflect, WebAssembly, Image, Audio, speechSynthesis, BroadcastChannel, MessageChannel, Notification (not eval or arguments: strict mode forbids binding them); the server's guard already refuses all of these, so this wall is for code saved before a change to the guard. The guard counts calls; past GUARD_LIMIT since the last reset, or past GUARD_MS (250 ms, looked at every 1024 turns), it throws GuardError. Compiled models and processes reset the guard themselves. `describeError` gives "TypeError: Cannot read properties of null (reading 'x') (line 5)" with the line counted in the designer's own code (the offset found once by compiling a probe that throws on its first line).

## The content (`content/`)

- `items/*.ts`: one file per family, each exporting `BuiltinItem`s with `build(m: Kit)` in TypeScript, built like the designer is told to build (front +z, metres, bold parts, named moving parts, anchors). Playground: slide, swing (a frame with two seats as parts 'seat1' 'seat2', anchors 'seat1' 'seat2' and 'seat' = seat1), seesaw (the plank a part 'plank' with seats at both ends), sandbox (with a sand heap), climbing-frame, bench, roundabout (its top a part), ball (red, bouncy 0.75), football (white with black patches), goal (a white frame and net open toward +z, not solid, so the ball goes in and players run through; 'maali', which no paint word reaches: football puts two up and takes them away), bucket, spade, tree (leafy), bush, flowers, lamp-post. Pool: diving-board, lounger, parasol, swim-ring (bouncy, floats), beach-ball, rubber-duck, towel, palm (in a pot), lifeguard-chair, pool-ladder. Forest: pine, rock, log, stump, mushroom (big red with white dots), tent, campfire (fire shapes glowing, a tick that flickers them), pinecone, stick, flower-patch, berry-bush. People's things: hair-ponytail, hair-short, hair-curly, hair-pigtails, cap, glasses. Every one has Finnish `calls` (base forms and the forms a child says: 'pallo', 'pallon', 'palloa'; 'liukumäki', 'liukumäkeen'; 'keinu', 'keinuun'...) and a `nameFi`, the nominative and one of its calls ('pallo', 'liukumäki', 'kuusi' for the pine, which looks like a spruce).
- `actions.ts`: the basic verbs as `BuiltinAction`s with params and Finnish `calls` (verb stems a child says: walk 'kävele', 'mene', 'mee', 'kulje'; run 'juokse', 'juoks'; come 'tule', 'tuu'; pick-up 'ota', 'nosta', 'poimi'; drop 'pudota', 'laske', 'laske maahan', 'päästä' (the parser is sure of a bare 'laske' only with a thing that can be carried: "laske liukumäkeä" slides down, "laske kolmeen" counts); give 'anna', 'vie'; jump 'hyppää', 'hypi', 'pomppaa', 'loikkaa'; wave 'vilkuta', 'heiluta', 'moikkaa'; sit 'istu', 'istuudu'; sleep 'nuku', 'käy maate', 'makaa'; spin 'pyöri', 'pyörähdä', 'pyörii', 'käänny'; say 'sano', 'huuda', 'laula'?; follow 'seuraa', 'tule perässä'; look 'katso', 'kato'; make 'tee', 'tehkää', 'luo', 'rakenna', 'taio'; remove 'poista', 'häivytä', 'vie pois'; wear 'pue', 'laita päähän', 'pukeudu', 'anna' (with a wearable); take-off 'riisu', 'ota pois'; paint 'maalaa', 'värjää', 'väritä'; grow 'kasva', 'suurenna', 'iso'; shrink 'kutistu', 'pienennä', 'pieni'; rename 'nimeä', 'nimi'; round 2 added everyday forms: 'pukee', 'katselkaa', 'hyppiä', the passive 'vilkutetaan', 'nostetaan', 'otetaan', 'pudotetaan', 'kasvetaan'), each a short lively generator using only the API. ## The dev server (`server/`, Node, dev only)

`plugin.ts`: a Vite plugin `sillyBilly()` with `apply: 'serve'` (and not in vitest's mode 'test') whose `configureServer` adds middleware at `/silly-billy/api/` and nothing at import time; it imports nothing of the game and asks Vite's module runner for `api.ts` (`server.ssrLoadModule`) on every request: a new module (a file api.ts uses changed) closes the old Api first (its CLI processes and Whisper stop) and builds a fresh one, and a module that does not load answers 500 and leaves the old Api alone. So loading `vite.config.ts` reads no file and starts no process, an edit to the engine does not restart the dev server, and an edit to the server's code takes effect on the next request. `vite.config.ts` imports it as `plugin.js`, which TypeScript resolves to `plugin.ts`. Endpoints as in `protocol.ts`: `GET status` (starts Whisper and the interpreter's processes on the first call), `POST hear` (starts Whisper if it is not up and waits up to 30 s for it), `POST interpret`, `POST invent` (NDJSON progress lines, then done or error), `GET library`, `POST library`, `POST forget`, `POST log`. Requests over 20 MB (hear) or 1 MB (others) are refused, and so is any request a browser sends for a page of another site (403: `Sec-Fetch-Site` other than same-origin or none, or an `Origin` whose host is not the request's `Host` or `:authority`; curl and tests send neither). Errors are JSON `{ error }` with 4xx/5xx, never a crash of the dev server. `api.ts` tidies a plan before the page sees it (`tidyPlan`: an empty id is taken from the name, a new thing with neither is left out and logged as `interpret-broken`; a missing `nameFi` is the first Finnish call (`finnishName`) and a missing `heardFi` the `sayFi`; the lines and args lose em and en dashes; an answer in pieces is asked once more in fresh sessions, 502 after two) and lists things the plan's steps use but nobody listed (`listLostKinds`, which reads every action's params, the item kinds, the actor ids and the scene's words (rooms, avatars' names and body types) from the context's own lines with `contextNames`; item and thing args skip those and `NO_THING` (ground, sky, water, me, everyone...), wear's and make's text `thing` skips slot words; a lost kind's `nameFi` is its English name until the designer gives one). Only a new thing with neither an id nor a name is logged as `interpret-broken`. `interpret` hands the CLI a signal that ends when the response closes (`stopped(res)`), as `invent` does. `hear` primes Whisper with the page's sentence, or the server's own for the language (`defaultPrime`), and logs what Whisper wrote (`raw`) when the words were changed after it.

`claude.ts`: finds the CLI (`CLAUDE_BIN`, the PATH, `~/.config/Claude/claude-code/<version>/<hash>/claude` newest first, `~/.vscode/extensions/anthropic.claude-code-*/resources/native-binary/claude` newest first); runs it from an empty temp directory with `-p --model <m> --tools "" --strict-mcp-config --setting-sources project --no-session-persistence --system-prompt <s> --json-schema <schema> --effort low --output-format stream-json --input-format stream-json --verbose --include-partial-messages`; reads `structured_output` from the `result` event (or JSON in the text), streams text deltas for progress. The interpreter uses a `Pool` of two `Session`s of one turn each: one process per command, the next one already started (with 12-turn sessions, one stray `room` in the first plan was copied by every later plan). Each invention is a process of its own (`once`), all in parallel. One retry on `error_max_structured_output_retries`. Timeouts: interpret 30 s, invent 90 s. Models from `SILLY_INTERPRET_MODEL` (default 'haiku') and `SILLY_INVENT_MODEL` (default 'opus').

`prompts.ts`: the interpreter's system prompt (the game, the rules for who/steps/newActions/newItems/create/room/say, Finnish and misheard words, gentle silliness, general reusable actions and items with colour as a tint, the look-change verbs; a new action's id is the English of the verb said and its about and params never name this command's things, so "rakenna lumiukko" makes a 'build' for any thing; 'tee' with a move is an action, "tee kärrynpyörä" a cartwheel and not a wheelbarrow; long things lie along z; the plan's lines in English and Finnish (`heard`/`heardFi`, `say`/`sayFi`) and a `nameFi` on each new action and thing; words to say and names in text args in the game's language, which the message states (PLAYED IN FINNISH or PLAYED IN ENGLISH); kinds, colours and parts in English; avatars by id, never by name) and its JSON schema built from `Plan`; the designer's system prompts for actions (ACTION_DOC, ACTION_RULES, ACTION_EXAMPLE) and items (ITEM_DOC, ITEM_RULES, ITEM_EXAMPLE, TICK_DOC) and their schemas (`{ code }` and `{ code, tick, nameFi }`, the last only when asked: a lost kind has only its English name, `lacksFinnishName`); the user messages built from the request (spec, the child's words, the context, a fix's code and error). The designer writes every bubble as `me.say({ en, fi })`, a plain string only for a name or a number, and a `.name` stands alone in Finnish (it takes no ending). The interpreter's examples (one Finnish game, one English, no name that is its id) show the verbs as the context writes them (`wear(thing: text, color?: text)`); `prompts.test.ts` pins the length targets (the interpreter's prompt and schema at most 17,947 characters, their size before the two languages), the rule lines the review rounds added, and no designer bubble in one language only.

`guard.ts`: wraps the body (`function* __action(me, args, w) {\n` + code + `\n}`, `function __model(m) {...}`, `function __tick(it, w, dt) {...}`), parses it with `parseAst` from 'vite', walks it with real scopes and refuses any free name but the code's own, its params and Math, Number, String, Boolean, Array, Object, JSON, Map, Set, WeakMap, WeakSet, Symbol, Date, Error, TypeError, RangeError, Infinity, NaN, undefined, isFinite, isNaN, parseInt, parseFloat and console; any name starting with `__`; the keys `constructor`, `prototype`, `getPrototypeOf`, `setPrototypeOf`, `getOwnPropertyDescriptor(s)`, `getOwnPropertyNames`, `defineProperty(ies)`, `eval`, `Function` and anything starting with `__`, written as a member, an object key, in destructuring or as a string; `import()`; and writes to built-ins. Keys worked out at run time (`o[k]`, `{ [k]: v } = o`) go through `__k((key), line)`, defined on the header line only when needed, which throws for those keys and for object or function keys with the line in its message (the key's own parentheses keep `o[(x, y)]` whole). It inserts `__g();` at the start of every loop body and function body (a body that is not a block becomes `{__g();BODY}`; an arrow's expression body becomes `(__g(), (BODY))`), inserting no newlines so lines keep their numbers; returns the guarded function expression or the refusal or syntax error with its line in the designer's code.

`store.ts`: the library folder (`SILLY_LIBRARY` or `silly-billy/library`), one JSON file per entry (pretty-printed, ids sanitised to `[a-z0-9-]` by `protocol.ts`'s `slugOf`), `phrases.json`, `log.jsonl`. Saving guards the code afresh instead of keeping the page's `guarded`; reading re-guards every entry's code so a guard change applies to old entries, and writes back a file that was stale (one it cannot write back is still served, freshly guarded, with a warning), so what a commit ships is today's guard's code (`shipped.test.ts` reads the library the same way). Reading also brings a library from before the two languages up to date: an action or a thing saved without `nameFi` takes its first call that is not its id or English name (`finnishName`) and is written back, and phrases whose plan has no `heardFi` are dropped and `phrases.json` is written back; saving such a phrase is refused. Forgetting a thing forgets the phrases that use it by `protocol.ts`'s `planUses`, the page's rule too; a starting avatar's entry is only its look, so its phrases stay.

`whisper.ts`: spawns `whisper-server -m <model> --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8179 -t 4 -nt` (or uses one already answering on the port) and waits until it answers; `ready(ms)` starts it if need be and waits (the hear route, up to 30 s). `hearFields` sets every field of a request as Hearing above says (no fallback: the server's own sampled hotter, dropped the prime from 0.5 up and kept its last try whatever it was); `heardWords` folds the letters (`foldLetters`: ð to d, ú to u, æ to ä, in English ä to a; other scripts left out; a letter of the prime, so of a name here, kept), drops inventions on silence (`cleanHeard`) and cuts a loop to three (`untangle`). `close()` leaves word on globalThis, so a fresh Whisper on the port (after a reload) waits for the old one to go (5 s, then a kill); killed with the dev server.

## The brain (`brain/`)

`brain.ts` (`class Brain implements BrainApi`), per heard words: a HeardLine; normalise; phrase → plan; else `parse.ts` → a confident plan; else if `link.online`, the addressed actor (if the parser found one) gets `listening` and `link.interpret` gets the words, the language (`world.lang`, the brain's only source of it) and `context.ts`'s text (room, actors with ids, names in the game's language, kinds and pronouns, who was last spoken to and last made, the things here by kind with counts, every action with its params and about, every item kind, the rooms, and that language's names free for a new avatar, none a starting avatar has in either language: `takenNames`); else an offline guess (the named or last actor does a known action with what it could find, or 'huh'). Then `execute(plan)`: the room first, then a new avatar (`newActor` beside the others, by the interpreter's name unless someone has that id or goes by that name in either language, else the next free one of `NEW_NAMES[lang]`; 'hello' reaction, saved as an AvatarEntry), then each new item: a draft entry into the library (`code: null`, `rough`) at once and an invention job; each new action: a job, and its waiting steps make the actors think; the steps go to the actors (`sim.give(who, steps, true)`); the answer (`say`/`sayFi`) in the world's language, bubble and voice alike, while the line of what was heard keeps both languages. A job: `link.invent` with progress into the job and the actors' `thinking.progress`; the entry is tested by `validate.ts` in a fresh worker (4 s limit) against a snapshot of the world (and, for an action, the step that wants it); a failure goes back once as `fix`; a pass is added to the library, `sim.refreshKind` for an item, saved with `link.save`; a final failure drops the waiting steps (`sim.dropSteps`) with a 'shrug' and says it in the line. When a plan's jobs all succeed and their things were saved, its words and plan (with no new things left) are saved as a phrase, unless the words point at someone by context ("hän", "hänelle", "heille", "se", "sille", "toiselle", "molemmille", "kaikki", "kaikille", "these", "both": `meansTheSame`), the plan's room is the room already, or the plan would not play (`playable`, which also checks a text param named `thing` names a known kind); old phrases with such words are left out when the library loads; a phrase plays again only if every thing it names is still in the library, and forgetting a thing forgets its phrases on the page too. Look changes persist: after a wear, paint, grow, shrink or rename step finishes, the look of the actor and of anyone the step names is saved as an AvatarEntry; the colour a worn thing's process gives it (a rainbow wand) is left out of that check and kept only when the thing is painted; a starting avatar's look saved under one of its own names keeps following the language (`castNamesFor`), and `lookKey` leaves out a name that follows the language, so a switch saves nothing. When many actors reach a new action at once, only the one who answers says the thinking line aloud. A runtime failure of a made action is logged; after two failures the action is sent back to the designer as a fix with the error. Each actor remembers the newest command that gave it steps or stopped it: a plan that comes back later skips those actors, and its room if the room changed meanwhile. Forgetting a made action someone waits for drops those steps and ends the line; forgetting a thing still being made cancels its job (hidden from the HUD, not taken in or saved) unless it is asked for again. `BrainApi.see(events)` is how the game passes each frame's events.

`parse.ts`: `normalise(words)` (letters composed (NFC), lowercase, punctuation out, spaces collapsed, numbers as digits, "kuusi kertaa" six); `understand(words, library, world, addressee)` → a stop, or a plan with a confidence flag (never null); an unknown word in a text param makes it unsure, colour words count as known, and a misheard name must keep its first letter ("kaikille" was read as Mike's call "maiki"); it is sure only of exact and ending matches (the Vocabulary's `{ near }`), two-word calls may have up to two words between them ("ota lippis pois"), "kaikki" never ends up in a colour or text, small words alone in a text param are unsure, and paint fills `part` with a worn thing's slot ("värjää tukka") only when the one painted wears something there (else the worn kind is the thing to paint). Round 2: two words of a call are joined across a gap only when the words between name one thing or person at most and nothing after the second names any ("laita pallo penkin päälle" puts the ball on the bench); a text param holding two kinds is unsure; a `color` param takes only a colour word (the first; two colours are unsure), a colour word in another text param is unsure ("make a tomato"), and only a text param named `thing` takes a kind ("maalaa pallo ja puu punaiseksi" is no part 'tree'); a bare 'laske' is sure only with a thing that can be carried; a room with a word after "ja", "sitten", "and" or "then", or with a name after the room word, is unsure ("mennään metsään ja alice tanssii"); verb stems double their last letter for every ending, "yhden" is 1, and "kättä", "käsiä", "kädet", "monta" are small words. Heard lines come in both languages: English ("Steve: give the ball to Alice"); Finnish from the actions' and things' `nameFi`, in the child's order and word forms ("Eetu: anna pallo Ainolle"), a name taking the ending the child put on it (`nameWith`: vowel harmony, kk/pp/tt gradation, an i after a consonant: "stevelle" shows "Eetulle", "maxille" "Maxille"), and a thing said in English or one letter off shown by its Finnish name; rooms.ts has only English room names, so the Finnish room line comes from parse.ts's own map ("mene metsään"). `validate.ts`: `validate(req)` (pure, used by the worker and by tests; `ValidateResult.forgiven` lists the kit's notes on a thing that still passed) and `validateInWorker(req)`. `link.ts`: `interpret(req, signal)` and `invent(req, onProgress, signal)` abort with the page's deadlines; `status()` is null in a build (no 404 asked for).

## Open

- Whether Whisper or the browser hears a real child better, in either language, and whether the names need other spellings in `calls`: the 2026-11-08 Whisper numbers come from synthetic clips, and a few final clips saved under `library/` (not in git) would let the next round measure real voices. The prime's commands leak a little (a word of the prime that was not said, such as 'laula', in 21 of 374 clips); beam search adds 10 to 15 ms a clip, a decode that loops takes up to 1.1 s, and the final words wait for a live ask still in flight.
- Saying it back: whether the voices of both languages are good enough on Sami's tablet, and whether the first-touch unlock makes iOS Safari speak (not tried on a real iPad).
- The two languages: actions made before them (Sami's slide and swing) keep their English-only bubbles ('No slide!') in a Finnish game until they are forgotten and made again; bubbles up at a switch are cleared, not translated (a thinking avatar keeps its cloud but loses its line until its next wait); a command said in English in a Finnish game shows bare names ("Aino: anna pallo Eetu"), and Haiku sometimes writes the English heard line with the English name it guesses from an id; a starting avatar renamed to its own name in the other language ("Alice" for Aino) follows the language again after a reload. The Finnish lines want a native speaker's read: a few could be more natural ('Kas noin!', 'Mitä poistan?', 'Ei ole mitään päällä!', 'Minulla ei ole mitään!').
- A parent's view of the library (list, rename, forget) beyond "forget the newest".
- One long call into the browser from made code (an array of a billion) can still hold the page once: only running play-time code in a worker would stop that. Changing a built-in through another name (`const M = Math; M.max = ...`) breaks the page until reload; it is not an escape.
- The parser asks Haiku whenever it is not sure, so a new way of saying a known thing ("Mirri sano miau", "Eetu nosta kädet ylös") takes a few seconds the first time (then it is a phrase). Haiku's answer still sometimes runs past 4 words ('Ride is over? Okay! I sit down.').
- A short actor right in front of a tall one still puts its tag on the tall one's head (Mirri on Aino).
- A worn thing's own scale is not kept (its size follows the wearer); `w.remove` on a worn thing does nothing.
- A gold crown on blond hair is hard to see from the game's camera: the designer could be told to give worn things a colour that stands out from the hair under them.
- A second avatar made while the first is still being made gets a free name, but its line and speech keep the first one's.
- A worn 'hand' thing is drawn from its anchor's rest place, while the engine's stand-in follows the posed one: a worn thing's puffs can land a little off when the anchor is inside a moving part. With a worn wand and the left hand full, the pick-up verb cheers with the wand arm.
- A new action's optional text param named `thing` is not listed as a lost kind; a word the library knows only as another kind's call ('hat' for cap, 'flower' for flowers) is listed as a new thing and gets designed; a lost kind's calls are only its id and the designer's Finnish name, so its other forms ('joulukuusen') rest on the matcher's endings.
- Phrases keep the plan an older prompt made (an old library had "mike tee kärrynpyörä" as make wheelbarrow): nothing cleans phrases when the prompts improve; forgetting the thing, or deleting the phrase, does. A phrase also keeps the text args of the language it was heard in and is not kept per language, so the same words in the other language's game replay them (rare); a `lang` on `PhraseEntry` and in the brain's phrase key would fix it.
- Walking: made code can still leave actors standing inside each other (the made slide ends everyone on one spot), and two who just stand stay so until one walks (separating them when a step ends would belong in `sim.ts`); two meeting head on in a passage barely one walker wide stop facing each other (the rooms have none so narrow); a walk may now take up to 50 s, and validation only notes a made action still going after 20 s; five sent to one spot end in a loose ring within 1.2 m of it, with a little jostling.
- When the server's code reloads, an `interpret` or `invent` in flight fails (its CLI is stopped), and the first words after wait for the fresh Whisper, up to 30 s. Folding keeps a letter in every word when a name here has it (with an Äijä here, 'tänne' stays in English); `cleanHeard` still drops a lone 'you'.
- "Eetu hyppää penkiltä" (jump off the bench) jumps onto it.
- In a thick crowd of wide bubbles one name tag can be mostly covered for a couple of seconds; a worn thing that changes its tint every frame still builds a model a frame (memory is bounded, CPU is not).
- The group games: a game's words reach it through the parser and the brain (play words, several actors merged into one step with the rest as `players`); `content/games.ts` only names its calls, and 'juna' or 'kilpaa' alone are now a game's ("tee juna", make a train, meets the train game). Football's teams show only by their goals' colours and a puff at the start; the race is mostly won by the cat (it runs fastest, though it stops to sniff half the time) and lasts about 25 s; hide and seek with three players can be over in 22 s. A game stopped midway leaves hiders inside a bush or the tent until their next command walks them out. Made actions that called `w.join` now end the other's own step instead of pausing it; moving another actor without it still pauses. Measured over 720 headless games (five games, three rooms, four line-ups, twelve seeds): nobody walked on the spot, every hide and seek found everyone, 4 football games in 144 ended 0 to 0.
