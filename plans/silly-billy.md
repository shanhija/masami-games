# Silly Billy

A 3D room full of people, a cat and things, seen from high up like Castle Builder. A child calls an avatar by name and says what to do, in Finnish or in English as the pause card is set: "Aino, heitä pallo Eetulle". What the game already knows happens at once. What it does not know, an interpreter (Opus at low effort) works out, and a designer (Opus) writes as code while the avatar visibly thinks it through; the new action or thing is tested in a sandbox, joins the library on disk, and is instant the next time. New avatars are made and dressed the same way: "tee uusi tyttö", "anna hänelle iso kihara tukka". The brief is Sami's [`silly-billy/design.md`](../silly-billy/design.md).

## Decisions

- **Name**: Silly Billy, the folder's name.
- **Language** (Sami, 2026-11-08): the pause card's setting, Suomi or English, Finnish by default, rules everything the child hears and sees: what Whisper listens for and its prime, every bubble, the line of what was heard, the job cards, the pause card and the voices. For this game it replaces the repo rule that audience-visible words are simple English, and the old English bubbles with a Finnish voice. Each answer is also spoken aloud by the browser's voice for the language when it has one, because six-year-olds do not read. Words for the child are `{ en, fi }` (`engine/lang.ts`'s `Words`), picked when shown or said; a switch renames the starting avatars at once, clears the bubbles still up and hushes the voice. In each language the starting avatars answer to that language's names and calls and to their ids. Ids, kinds, colours, parts and the interpreter's `about` texts stay English: the child never sees them.
- **Models through the Claude Code CLI on the laptop's subscription**, as in belay-that: no API key. The CLI runs from a **Vite dev-server plugin** (`silly-billy/src/server/plugin.ts`, wired in the root `vite.config.ts`, `apply: 'serve'`), so `npm run dev` is all Sami starts and the page reaches it as `./api/` over the same https origin. The static build (the hosted page) has no server: it plays the library it shipped with and the small parser, and invents nothing.
- **Hearing**: push-to-talk (hold the big button or Space). With the dev server, Whisper on the GPU in the language of the setting, port 8179, started on the first status request or on the first words after the server's code reloaded: Silly Billy's own build (`~/src/silly-billy-whisper`, made by its `setup.sh`: whisper.cpp 1.9.5 patched to take `suppress_regex`, the letters the language does not write kept out of the decoder, and Finnish-NLP's Finnish fine-tune of large-v3 made q8_0) when it is there, else belay-that's build and large-v3-turbo (`~/src/belay-that/vendor`); `SILLY_WHISPER_BIN` and `SILLY_WHISPER_MODEL` name others, and a server already on the port is used with the fields it says it understands (`X-Whisper-Extras`). Every clip, live and final, is asked with every field set: the language, the page's prime (`voice/prime.ts`: the names of who is here, each told to do something; the server has its own per language), temperature 0 with no fallback, a beam of 5, and the letters to suppress. Letters the language does not write are also folded to its own unless a name here has them, inventions on silence are dropped and loops cut; a clip with no voiced sound in it is heard as nothing (`voice.ts`'s `voiceless`, the voice gate: Whisper's own no-speech probability was 0.00 on noise it heard as 'Kiitos!'). On a tablet or a phone the microphone is asked for no processing (`voice/mic.ts`'s `processingFor`: with echo cancelling on, Chrome on Android records through the phone-call path, whose noise suppression and gain squash a child's high, quiet voice); on the laptop all three stay on. Every whole clip is kept on the laptop to measure hearing with (Sami, 2026-11-09: `server/voice.ts`, `library/voice/`, not in git, the newest 3000 or 1.5 GB) with what came with it and what Whisper made of it; `tools/mark.mjs` lets Sami write in what was really said and who said it, and `tools/bench.mjs` runs the clips through candidate recognizers on its own Whisper (port 8199) and reports (measured below). Without Whisper, the browser's recognizer (`fi-FI` or `en-US`). Typing for the parent. `?say=` and `window.game.hear(text)` feed the same path for tests and screenshots.
- **Understanding**: the normalised words are looked up first among phrases heard before (`library/phrases.json`), then the small parser (`brain/parse.ts`) tries: one known avatar (or "she/he/kaikki"), one known action by its Finnish stems, its params filled by known things. Confident: done, no model. Otherwise the interpreter (`--model opus --effort low`, one process per command with the next one already started, structured output; Haiku until the second play of 2026-11-09, when Sami asked "should we use opus in place of haiku? or sonnet" and the measurement below answered: with principles instead of cases, Opus is right most often for the same time to the whole plan; `SILLY_INTERPRET_MODEL` sets another) returns a Plan (`engine/protocol.ts`) with its lines in both languages. A misheard name goes to the closest-sounding avatar; misunderstanding is part of the fun, so the interpreter always does something. The parser is sure only of what it matched exactly or by a Finnish ending: a verb or thing one letter off a known word ("lennä" is not "kävele", "kaiva" is not "kasva"), an unknown word left over with a case ending ("trampoliinilla"), or a name no param took sends the words to the interpreter, so new verbs and things get made instead of played as the wrong basic one (round 1 of review: 9 of 100 everyday verbs had been played sure and wrong). A plan that comes back after the child said something newer to the same actors leaves them alone ("seis" holds), changes no room when the room changed or anyone got newer words meanwhile, ends "(sanoit jo jotain uutta)" ("(you said something newer)") when nothing of it is left, and is not kept as a phrase when it was cut short (round 2). The page waits at most 45 s for the interpreter and 90 s for a job (every round together); past that whoever waits shrugs and the request is called off, and the dev server stops the CLI turn when the page stops waiting. From Sami's play of 2026-11-09 (half the commands misheard, half of the rest odd): a stop is looked for before the phrases, and a bare "ei" or "älä" (in English "no") stops the one spoken to (Haiku had made Noora say "Ei? Okei!" and Eetu "Okay, I stop!"); a word nobody knows beside a thing is the thing meant, so the parser asks the interpreter ("Mirri, syö tammen terho" had Mirri eat the oak; "puu maja", "joulu kuusi"), and so does the room everyone is in already ("Mikko, käy uimassa" in the pool did nothing); 'iso', 'pieni', 'toinen' and a few more before a thing leave it sure. The interpreter and the designer get the whole scene (`brain/context.ts`, below), and the interpreter's rules gained what that morning's plans got wrong.
- **Scripts** (Sami, 2026-11-09): a plan may carry code for this moment when known actions run whole one after another cannot say it ("hyppikää trampoliinilla ja tehkää voltti siellä": a bounce ends hopping off, so a flip after it was on the grass; "tee puumajaan tikkaat", "vaihda yöksi ja sytytä lamput"). The interpreter writes a short one itself in the plan (`scripts: [{ id, about, code }]`, at most 10 lines, from a short list of calls in its prompt); a longer one it leaves to the designer, who writes it from its `about` with the whole scene while the avatar thinks. The dev server guards the interpreter's code with the interpreter's answer (`guardScripts`); the page tries it in the validation worker against a copy of the world with the step that wants it, and code the guard refused or the test failed goes to the designer as a fix (`invent` kind 'script'). A script runs as a step of each avatar that does it, as a one-off action of the command (`moment-<serial>-<id>`) that waits for the new things the plan makes, plays, and leaves the library when its last step ends; it is never saved, and words whose plan has a script are not kept as a phrase (a script fits its moment). It can use the library's actions as helpers (`yield* me.do('bounce', { thing })`, three deep at most). A verb the child may ask for again is still a new action, made and kept; simple commands are as fast as before (the parser, or Haiku with no code to write). Measured below.
- **Principles, not cases** (Sami, after the second play of 2026-11-09: "i'm also experimenting with the idea of using this as an 'always novel' engine on a game. fixing special situations isn't sustainable"): what the models are told states principles, never the cases that taught them, and what the engine gains is general (a capability any request may need), never aimed at one command, word or thing. The interpreter is told: read garbled words against the scene (what is here, what the avatar holds or wears or just did); a known action fits only when its about covers all of what was asked, done with things it is made for, else a script rather than a bent action; use each thing as what it is, by its kind's line (ridden, sat on, carried, too big to carry, food), and never fill a param with a thing that does not fit its role just because it was named; what a step makes or builds is new ('new:kind'); a new action's or thing's Finnish name and calls are the proper words for what it is or does, never a verb that could mean many moves and never a word as misheard; a thing made for a thing here is fixed to it (make with at, side and reach, or a script); the answer is about what happens, never about the words or how they were heard. The case lines of the first rounds were folded into these or cut, and the interpreter's prompt and schema came down from 21,405 to 21,240 characters with the new engine calls in it. Then, on how a thing's role is judged (Sami: "1. that list would grow uncontrollably. no. 2. well, the game is silly, so mounting a chicken would be funny. don't restrict it. 3. ok, but don't make it specific. think of 'can it be done'. you really can't drive a train track, since it doesn't move. but you can ride a chicken, given that you won't crush it. again, don't be specific. try to utilize the distilled world knowledge the llm must have."): no list of roles or of what is not ridden anywhere, in the context or the prompts; nothing is ever kept from being mounted, ridden or stood on, since silly is the point; and one principle of world knowledge, for the interpreter and the designer alike, takes the place of the role lists: picture it happening with these real things and use what you know of them (what each is, what it can do, what moves and what carries whom); silly is welcome whenever it could be done at all; when what was literally asked cannot be done, do the reading that can; and what a thing is comes from its kind and its look, never from a name that may be misheard. The engine meets it with general movement: anything and anyone goes along any route at any pace, and whatever rides or stands on a moving thing goes with it (below). And (Sami, playing: "the net cannon isn't a net. it's a ball." The made shoot had `w.find('net-cannon')` and `w.make('ball')` in its code, so every launcher fired a ball): a made action names no kind and no name in its code; whatever takes part, what it uses and what comes out of or is made by what it uses, arrives by a param (a step may give 'new:kind') or is what the avatar holds, and a null arg is done without or with what is held, never looked up or made by kind. The interpreter makes whatever takes part a param, also what comes out of the thing used or is made by it, and fills it from what it knows of those very things. No list of verbs or things, and no meta for it. The prompts' size stayed flat (21,206 characters for the interpreter's prompt and schema), the worn slots' and the draft sizes' example lists folded too. Measured below: the principles alone cost Haiku the cases its old lines had named, while Opus at low effort meets them, and others, from the principles.
- **From the log of 2026-11-09's afternoon** (Sami: "read the log and make any fixes you find suitable"; two children, about 220 utterances, 45 minutes), each a general rule:
  - **Stops:** a stop word with more words is no longer the whole command. "Kaki uudestaan sinne ja älä vedä vessaa! Ja näytä se minulle" stopped everybody and did nothing else, and the child asked four times. Now whoever it is said to stops at once and the interpreter plans the rest: who is called in its own part of the words, else who was called before it, else everybody for a stop said to many ("lopettakaa"), else the one spoken to. The prompt says whoever was told to stop has stopped, so plan the rest and never what was forbidden. With no interpreter the stop is all.
  - **Verbs said to many:** a Finnish verb said to many with nobody named is for everyone, as "let's" is ("ampukaa ilotulituksia" had only Aino shoot). A double k before the ending is one ("pakkaa").
  - **Where-words:** a param for where (a place, or one named to, at, on or target) takes a word said as where first: before a postposition, or with an ending for where in a Finnish game. Any other param takes a plain word first. "Mirri, tee puu kerrostalon viereen" made a block of flats at the tree; now it makes the tree at the block.
  - **Rooms:** a room word beside another verb is a room change only when said as where to go ("hyppää altaaseen"). "Mirri maalaa piha pinkiksi" moved everyone to the playground and painted Mirri; now it goes to the interpreter.
  - **New things:** a 'new:kind' after the same avatar's step made that kind is the one just made, never a second (two jump ropes and two trampolines). The prompt says so too.
  - **Nothing nicer instead:** the interpreter does what was asked, never something nicer in its place. Gross or messy fun happens as asked, cartoon-like, and only hurting someone turns gentle. "Noora, kakkaa Mirrin päälle" became flowers on Mirri. This is a call Sami can overturn.
- **Inventing**: for each new item the plan carries the interpreter's rough draft (2 to 5 boxes, balls, cylinders and cones), which appears at once; Opus (`--model opus --effort low`, one process per invention, all in parallel) writes the model code, and the draft is swapped for the model with a sparkle. For each new action Opus writes generator code against the engine's API while the avatar thinks: it says "Hmm, mietitäänpä!" in its bubble and aloud ("Let me think this through!" in English, the brief's words), a thought cloud with turning cogs fills as the designer's reply streams, the HUD's job card says "Mietitään: <name> 12 s" with the name in the game's language (then "Kokeillaan", "Yritetään uudestaan"; a thing says "Piirretään"; in English "Thinking this through", "Trying it out", "Trying again", "Drawing"), and the plan's own answer ("Watch me!") waits until the made action starts. A thing given to a built-in verb that nobody listed as new (Haiku's "wear magic-wand" on an empty library, also as wear's and make's text `thing`) is added to the plan's new things by the server, so it is made instead of shown as a mystery box; a room, the ground, the sky, an avatar's name or body type is never listed. When the speaker's first step is a say, the plan's own answer is not said: the line itself is the answer, and an "Okei!" before it was cut off at once. Measured 2026-11-07 on the CLI: Haiku answers in about 3 s, Opus writes a 40-line action in about 6 s.
- **The world's state** (the engine, 2026-11-09): the sky is the world's and the same in every room (`World.sky`, `engine/sky.ts`: the time of day, night easing at dusk and dawn with the moon and stars, rain, snow, fog, wind, a sky colour and a light colour, each faded by code over seconds and going on after the action); each room keeps its water level, water colour and ground colour (`World.terrain`): the pool can be emptied a bucket at a time, people wade on its floor and things lie on it. A body has states with a time each (`Actor.effects`, `engine/effects.ts`: sick is green, wobbly and slow and lies down a moment to get better, dizzy, wet from water and rain, cold from snow, sleepy, hot, glowing), shown on the body (`scene/States.tsx`). A thing can be put at or fixed to another thing or an actor at a side, on top or at an anchor or part (`Item.attached`, `engine/attach.ts`: it goes and turns with it until picked up, thrown or let go), get shapes added to it alone (`Item.added`: a ladder on this treehouse), have a part painted, hidden or lit, and its lamps switched off or on or given a real light; lamps and fires light what is near at night by themselves, the two strongest near the middle of the view with a real light (`scene/Sky.tsx`'s `LAMP_LIGHTS`, a weak tablet has few to spare) and the rest with a halo. Things are food or not (`ItemTraits`: yummy, yucky or poison, hot or cold, soft, from the model's return or guessed from its names); eating takes bites until it is gone and decides by them (poison makes one sick, nobody is hurt); what a made action picks up it puts down nicely when it ends unless it keeps it (`me.keep`), and things can be put down or put back where they were picked up. Flips, somersaults and cartwheels turn round the body's middle (`me.pivot`, `me.flip`). Since the second play (2026-11-09): an avatar gets up onto any thing's top and stays there (`me.climbOnto`: a hop onto a low top, a climb up a high one, onto its 'top' anchor or its top middle; `Actor.on`), jumps and flips up there land on it again (a flip goes no further than the top reaches), and walking anywhere off it, or `me.climbDown`, gets it down; a thing of any size is moved (`me.moveThing`: carried when it can be, pushed along the ground when too big, by magic when built into the room or going onto another, and fixed there when it goes on top); a thing put at or fixed to another can be sized to reach up to a point of it (`{ up: 'top' }`: stairs, a ladder or a slide up to a floor); a step's 'new:kind' arg is a new one made for it, so what is made or built never takes the one already standing there; and the built-in make makes a thing for another thing too (`at`, `side`, `reach`). The new World, Item and Actor fields are optional, so a world or a room saved before them loads.
- **Code, not a fixed set of primitives** (the brief): an action is `function* (me, args, w)` with full control of positions, every joint, items, physics, particles and sounds through a small API (`engine/api.ts`, described for the designer in `engine/apidoc.ts`); a thing's model is `function (m)` over a modelling kit (`engine/kit.ts`: boxes, spheres, cylinders, tori, capsules, lathes, extrusions, tubes, named moving parts, anchors); a thing may have a process `function (it, w, dt)`. The basic verbs and the built-in things are TypeScript against the same API, on equal footing.
- **Sandboxed and tested before it is taken in**: the server parses the designer's code (Vite's `parseAst`), refuses code that names anything but its own names, its params and a short list of plain built-ins, or reaches for keys like `constructor`, `prototype`, `__proto__` or `getPrototypeOf` (round 1 of review escaped the first sandbox through `({}).constructor.constructor`), checks keys worked out at run time (`__k`, naming the line of a refused one), and puts a guard call at the top of every loop and function body; a refusal or a syntax error goes straight back to the designer. The page runs new code first in a **disposable validation worker** against a copy of the world for up to 20 simulated seconds (killed after 4 s of real time), an action a second time with its optional args left out, a thing's process for a second lying about and half a second worn (a worn thing is a stand-in in no list of the room's things), timed against the budget it gets in play: an error, a loop without yield or an empty model is sent back to the designer once with the error to fix; a thing that works but whose kit notes say shapes were lost plays at once and goes back once with the notes. Only code that passed is saved and run, and the server guards it afresh when it saves it and when it reads the library (a file guarded by an older guard is rewritten); `server/shipped.test.ts` reads the committed library through the store before every build, so stale files are rewritten there too (and belong in the commit), and fails on a file the store would leave out. At play time made code runs in strict mode with the page's globals shadowed, the guard reset before every call (a turn budget and a 250 ms clock), a real-time budget per action and per kind's process, every error caught (the action ends with a shrug) and every number it wrote made finite and kept in the room. The dev server's API answers only the game's own page (403 for another site's). Generated code never runs on the laptop's Node side.
- **The library on disk**: `silly-billy/library/{items,actions,avatars}/<id>.json` and `library/phrases.json`, written by the dev server, read back after a reboot; an action or a thing keeps its Finnish name. The build ships what is there (`import.meta.glob`; `brain/shipped.ts` leaves out phrases without a Finnish line), so things made at home play on the hosted page too. `library/log.jsonl` (git-ignored) logs every utterance, plan, invention and timing for the parent.
- **Two pages at once** (the log of 2026-11-09's afternoon: two children played on two pages of the one dev server, and seven things were made twice, each overwriting the other's: the trampoline, bounce, climb, eat, decorate, throw and the house): the dev server tells every page what was saved or forgotten (`LIBRARY_EVENT` on Vite's own websocket, the entry as saved and the page that saved it), and every other page takes it in at once (`Brain.news`): a thing's model, an action and a phrase play there too, and what was forgotten goes there too. An avatar is each page's play (its look, its clothes) and comes in only when the page loads again.
- **The look**: Castle Builder's camera (its yaw, pitch and 30° lens, imported from `castle-builder/src/scene/look.tsx`) over a diorama slab per room, flat-shaded low-poly solids, one warm sun with soft shadows and a hemisphere fill, a sky-blue background. With nobody busy the frame keeps 15 m of ground around the room's middle (Castle Builder keeps 24). When someone does something, the camera moves in on them, on the actors, things and places their step names (and the step a thinking actor waits for), on anyone playing a reaction and on loose things still flying faster than 1.5 m/s, down to 6 m of ground (`scene/frame.ts`), and goes back to the whole room 2.5 s after everyone is idle: at the whole room a kid is about 30 px tall on a laptop and a thrown ball is a dot, which a six-year-old cannot follow. When a made thing's real model replaces its draft, the camera shows it for 3 s (by then everyone is often idle again). Heights count (Sami, 2026-11-10: action on top of a tall building went off the screen): the frame is points with heights (an actor from what it stands on, the top of a thing it got onto or else the ground, to the top of its head; a thing from its bottom to the corners of its top), aims at their floor (someone alone on a roof is framed as on the ground, only higher, and a jump does not bob the camera), counts what reaches over 2 m above that floor as far back as it shows on the screen, and widens until a top 4 m or more over that floor shows below 0.7 of the screen's height (room for its name and bubble under the faces; lower tops may go higher, so people on the ground are framed exactly as before); the whole room widens while anything in it would reach over the screen's edge (the starting rooms' far trees reach 0.97, so they are framed exactly as before). The player's zoom (`scene/zoom.ts`) goes on top: out always works (Sami, 2026-11-10), past the game's close-up to the whole room (zoom -1) while someone is busy, and stays out until a new command, a face tapped or everyone done; a double tap and letting a face go go to the whole room too. The lens, yaw and pitch stay Castle Builder's.
- **Rooms**: the playground (grass, sand, slide, swings, seesaw, sandbox, climbing frame, benches, trees, balls), the swimming pool (tiles, the pool you can wade in, diving board, loungers, parasols, swim rings, a beach ball, rubber ducks, palms) and the forest (pines and leafy trees, rocks, logs, stumps, big red mushrooms, a tent, a campfire, a pond, pinecones and sticks). Buttons and voice ("mennään metsään") switch; each room keeps its things.
- **The cast**, each with a name in each language and the same id in both: Aino / Alice (a girl with a long ginger ponytail and a purple top), Eetu / Steve (a boy with a red cap and a blue shirt), Mikko / Mike (a tall grown-up with glasses and a green jumper), Noora / Nora (a girl with blonde pigtails and a pink dress) and Mirri / Luna the orange cat. New avatars join them and stay, named from a list for each language; a made avatar's name, and any name the child gives, stays in both languages.
- **Basic verbs** (built in, a Finnish name and Finnish stems in `calls`, every line in both languages): walk, run, come, pick-up, drop, give, jump, wave, sit, sleep, spin, say, follow, look, make, remove, wear, take-off, paint, grow, shrink, rename. Everything else (throw, ride, dance, swim, climb, kick...) is invented.
- **Group games** (Sami, 2026-11-08, after playing: "this is quite simple and slow. can you add something that they could do together? like play tag, football, etc?"): five more built-in actions in which several avatars play together, `content/games.ts`: tag (hippa), football (jalkapallo, with two goals put up for the game), hide and seek (piilosta), a race (kilpajuoksu, a swimming race in the pool) and a train (juna, follow the leader). The step's actor starts it and has the first part ("it", the seeker, the train's front); its `players` text arg names the others, and empty is everyone here. A game takes them in with `w.join`, which now ends what they were doing as a command would (off the swing, up from the bench), and lets them go when it ends or is stopped ("kaikki seis" stops the starter's step); a command of their own takes one out (the game then sees it `busy`) and plays on without it. Each lasts about 25 to 50 s and ends on its own. Built in like the basic verbs, they play at once, offline and in the hosted build too.
- **Nothing hurts**: the interpreter turns rough requests into something silly and kind; a hit is a bonk, a tumble and a giggle.

## The engine (`engine/`)

**World** (`types.ts`): plain data; the Sim keeps the running generators in a side map keyed by actor id. `createWorld` places each room's layout (`content/layouts.ts`) as items `#i1`, `#i2`... (fixed ones `fixed: true`), the current room's in `items`, the others in `away`, and stands the actors on the room's spawn spots facing +z (toward the camera). `World.lang` is the language it is played in; `useLang` sets it and gives each starting avatar the child has not named (`Actor.names` not null) that language's name and calls, its id among them.

**Stepping** (`Sim.step`, 1/60 s): `t += STEP`, then for each actor: if `joinedBy` is set, skip its own task; else if it has no doing and its queue's front step's action is in the library, start it (resolve args, make the generator, push a `started` event, clear `thinking`); if the front step's action is not in the library yet, the actor waits in the think pose (`thinking` stays). A running generator gets one `next()` per step inside try/catch with `resetGuard()` before; done → `done` event and `rest`; thrown → `failed` event with `describeError`, then the `shrug` reaction. A step runs at most 60 s, then it is ended. Ending a step (done, failed, stopped, replaced, timed out) releases joined actors (`joinedBy = null`), gets the actor off anything it rides unless the action left it riding on purpose (a 'ride' that ends riding stays riding until the next command: keep it simple, get off), and eases the pose to rest over 0.3 s. Then items: held items follow the hand frame (`body.handFrame`, the item's 'hand' anchor at the hand, upright, turned with the actor's yaw, plus the item's own pitch and roll while held), hugged ones the `hugFrame`, worn ones are stand-in items outside the room (in the Sim's side map, not in `items`), placed at their slot every frame so their process runs and the action's handle stays live (a worn wand turns its star); their parts are published on `Actor.wornParts[slot]` for the scene, not in the look, so a saved look never changes with them (`Sim.wearItem`, `takeStandIn`, `putItem`, `removeItem(id, keepHandle)`), ridden ones carry their rider (the rider's hips on the 'seat' anchor through the part chain, its yaw the item's), item processes (`tick`) run in try/catch (an item kind whose tick throws 3 times, or runs over its real-time budget of about 4 ms a frame plus 500 ms, loses its tick; an action over about 8 ms a frame plus 500 ms fails with "it took too long"), then physics, puffs (move, gravity, drop when old; at most 600), bubbles and moods run out.

**Physics** (loose items): gravity 9.8, ground at `groundAt` (the pool's floor under water) but a thing over water floats with its bottom at `WATER_Y`, bobbing; bounce on the ground with restitution `bouncy` (0.2 → 0.85), rolling friction; walls at ±(ROOM_HALF - radius) bounce; solid items (circles of their footprint radius) push loose things out and bounce them; an actor's body (a capsule from feet to head, its radius) bounces a loose thing that hits it and raises a `hit` event (once per throw) so actions can react; a thing at rest (speed < 0.05 on the ground for 0.3 s) stops being loose. Thrown things spin.

**Actors move only through the API**: nothing walks by itself except the idle life (blink, breathe: a tiny bob, look around now and then, turn toward the camera when idle for long). Actors keep out of each other and out of solid items when walking (`walkTo`, runtime.ts over `path.ts`: straight at the goal while no solid thing is in the line, else along a way planned round the solid things, A* on a 0.2 m grid with each thing grown by the walker's radius, pulled straight by line of sight; a field of clearances is kept per set of things and a grid per walker size, so a plan takes about 0.05 ms and happens only when the goal is out of sight, has moved 0.75 m or the walker has stalled; each frame it steps round other actors, turning as little as it must, never into a thing and never deeper into an actor it overlaps; after a second without progress it plans round the actors standing in its way; on the last stretch it stops when no step brings it nearer: a goal inside or behind a thing, the room's edge, or others standing at the goal (as near as they allow, going round them when that is 0.3 m nearer); starting inside a thing it walks out the way that leads on; it gives up after 4 s without progress or after twice its way's walking time and 4 s, at least 12 s and at most 50 s). Things one can step onto, and water, stay walkable. Actors wading in water move at 60%. Keep everyone inside the room.

**Args** at a step's start, by the action's param kind: 'actor' → the actor by id, name or calls, else null; 'item' → an instance id ('#i3') if it exists, else the kind (via `library.itemKindFor`) → the nearest free instance in the room, else a new one made 1.5 m in front of the actor (a second item argument 4 m in front, so there is room to act); an unknown word → null; 'new:kind' (any item or thing param) → a new one made 1.5 m in front (4 m for a second), never one already here; 'thing' → actor first, then item; 'place' → 'x,z' or a thing's position as a Spot; 'text' → the string; 'number' → a finite number or null. A 'thing' takes a spot 'x,z' too (the room's water as a target: "heitä se lampeen"). `me.do(action, args)` gives a library action its args the same way from code, as things, actors, spots or words.

**Runtime** (`runtime.ts`): one ActorApi per actor and one ItemApi per item (cached by id, dropped when the thing goes), a WorldApi per Sim, implementing `api.ts` exactly as `apidoc.ts` says. Setters clamp: position within the room (±ROOM_HALF), y within [-2, 30], scale [0.2, 4], angles finite; `pose` is the live object, cleaned after every step (non-finite → rest value, angles to ±2π, squash [0.3, 2], bob [-1, 5]). `feel` takes any word: a known mood, or the nearest by a small table ('confused' → 'surprised', 'focused' → 'happy', 'excited' → 'happy', 'tired' → 'sleepy', 'cross' → 'angry', 'shy' → 'love'), else 'happy'. `sound` maps unknown names to 'pop' ('cheer' → 'yay', 'laugh' → 'giggle', 'splash' stays). Helpers: `walkTo` (walk cycle on the legs and empty arms, 0.6 rad at 2.4 steps/s scaled by speed; `run` doubles the speed and the cycle), `turnTo` (at 6 rad/s), `pickUp` (walk to `radius + item.radius + 0.2`, bend, the item to the hand; a 'hug' item fills both hands; a 'no' item says "Too big!" ("Liian iso!") and fails softly), `jump` (a real arc with gravity, squash before and after, forward motion), `poseTo`/`rest` (ease from the current values), `ride`/`sitOn`/`getOff`, `wear` (into `look.worn[slot]`: worn things are part of the look, not items in the room; the slot's old thing goes in a sparkle, since old hair lying at the feet looked like a mistake; slot defaults to the item's meta.wear, else 'hand'; a worn 'hand' thing sends one-hand things to the left hand, `Runtime.handFor`), `takeOff` (the very same item comes back, its state and parts too), `tumble`, `speak(words, secs)` (the bubble shows 48 characters, the voice says the whole line up to 200; the same line in the same step is not started again while it lasts (`Side.voiced`), at most two lines a frame from each actor and eight in all, so "kaikki sanokaa hei" is everyone's hello; the built-in say uses it). `wear` takes the thing out of the hand that held it before it frees the right hand, so a ball in the right hand moves to the left. An item's `.wearer` is the actor wearing it (a worn thing has no `.holder`). `turnTo` and `me.yaw = v` while riding turn the ridden thing; while sitting they turn nothing and cost one frame. Writing 0 to a speed of a thing that is not loose changes nothing (a placed thing stays where code put it; only a non-zero speed sets it loose). Small actors in water deeper than 0.6 of their height swim at the surface, head out. `w.make` creates any kind in the library, a mystery box for an unknown one. `w.throw` computes a ballistic launch from the item's current position to land at the target's body centre (or the spot), with flight time from distance/speed and the arc, releases it from the hand, and returns the flight time. `w.together` steps several generators each frame until all finish. `w.join` takes the other actor in as a command would (`Sim.enlist`): what it was doing, had queued or was reacting ends, and it is `joinedBy` this one until the action ends; code that moves another actor without it joins it too, but then its own doing only waits and goes on after. A command of its own (`Sim.give` replacing, `stop`, a reaction: the child spoke to it) takes a joined actor out: the step it left is kept (`Side.left`) and cannot take it in again, and to that step's code it is `busy`. `say(words, secs)` and `speak` take `{ en, fi }` or a plain string (a name, a number, the child's words) and show the game's language, picked when said; any other object shows nothing. An item's `.name` is its `nameFi` in Finnish. Setting an actor's `.name` sets `names` to null, so the name stays in both languages; the same name again changes nothing. `me.do(action, args)` runs a library action, by id or a word for it, inside the running one, its ending too (a script bounces, then flips), three deep at most; an action nobody has is an error. `climbOnto(thing)` walks to it, climbs its side hand over hand when the top is over 0.8 of the climber's height, hops onto the top's middle and sets `Actor.on`; `groundUnder` is then that top while the feet are over the thing's footprint as it lies (its box turned and sized with it, within its reach: `overTop`), so `jump`, `flip` (whose forward is cut to stay on the top), `fall` and the settling after a step keep it up there, `pushOut` leaves it alone, and walking within the top goes straight along it, a long thing from end to end; `walkTo` a goal off the top first `climbDown`s toward it (a hop, or a climb down a side over 1.2 heights); a thing gone, held or ridden lets it down. `moveThing(it, to, where)` carries what a hand or both arms take, pushes a thing too big to carry from behind along the ground at 0.9 m/s (dust, no "Too big!"), and moves one built into the room, held, or going 'top' by magic (it lifts 0.6 m in a sparkle, glides over and settles); with a side of a thing it ends exactly where `putAt` puts it, and 'top' fixes it there. `putAt` and `attachTo` take `{ up }`: a height in metres or an anchor of the other, and the thing is scaled (0.2 to 4) so its top is there. Movement is general: `walkTo` takes a route (a list of points or things, walked in turn) and a pace (`speed`, metres a second); `moveThing(it, route, null, { speed })` takes a thing along a route, carried by the actor when it can be carried and nobody is on it, else going by itself on what is under it (or at the heights the route's points give), turning to face its way, and fixed again where it stops to whatever it was fixed to; the sim carries whoever stands on a thing (`Actor.on`) along when the thing moves or turns, by however far it went since the last frame (`Side.standing`, `Sim.carryStanders`), as riders always went with what they ride, so a ridden or stood-on thing carries its people along whatever route it takes, by `moveThing` or by code; and `it.point(x, y, z)` gives a point of a thing's box in its own frame (each -1..1: right to left, bottom to top, back to front), so code can work out a route from any thing's size, heading and ends.

**Reactions** (`Sim.react`): short built-in generators for 'shrug' (palms up, "Hmm?"), 'yay' (a hop with arms up, "Yay!" / "Jee!"), 'huh' (head tilt, "Huh?" / "Häh?"), 'hello' (a wave, "Hi!" / "Hei!"), 'think' (hand on chin while waiting for an invention, "Hmm..."). The one playing is `Actor.reacting`, which the camera reads.

**Library** (`library.ts`): built-ins first; `addItem` compiles `guarded` (and `tickGuarded`) through `sandbox.ts`, runs the model in a Kit (`buildRecipe`) and stores the recipe, or the draft recipe (`roughRecipe`) when `code` is null; an entry that fails keeps the previous version and returns the error; a stored entry's `nameFi` is its own, else its first call, else its name. `itemKindFor`/`actionFor` match a word to an id, a name in either language or a call: lowercase, exact, or the word starting with a call of 4+ letters followed by a Finnish ending (`lle`, `lla`, `llä`, `lta`, `ltä`, `n`, `a`, `ä`, `aa`, `ää`, `ssa`, `ssä`, `sta`, `stä`, `seen`, `han`, `hin`, `ksi`, `t`, `in`, `ja`, `jä`, `ita`, `itä`, `ineen`, `ni`, `si`, `kin`), or an edit distance of 1 for words of 5+ letters that keeps the first letter. `matchWordForm` says how a word matched (exact, ending or near); `itemKindFor` and `actionFor` take `{ near: false }` and `Library.matchForm` exists, so the parser can be sure only of exact and ending matches. The mystery box for unknown kinds: a 0.4 m purple box with a yellow "?" made of boxes.

**Kit** (`kit.ts`): `m.part(name, fn)` works without opts; a part name used twice makes a copy at its own pivot (`light0~2`, with `PartDef.pose = 'light0'`, so `it.part('light0')` moves every copy); at most 120 parts, and past that what is made inside a part keeps its place (the part's pivot is added to its shapes and anchors, `body.ts`'s `anglesXYZ`) and only stops moving; `parseColor` takes every CSS colour name (from Maps, so 'constructor' is no colour), `#rgba`, `#rrggbbaa`, `rgb()` and `hsl()`, but `BASIC_COLORS`, the game's own 56 names, is all `content/colors.ts`'s `colorOf` takes from a child's words, with the Finnish stems and `#hex` ('seashell' is a thing, 'peru' no colour).

**Sandbox** (`sandbox.ts`): `compile(guarded, what, budget, own)` is `new Function('__g', ...SHADOWED, ...ownNames, '"use strict"; return (' + guarded + ')')` called with the budget's guard call, `undefined` for each shadowed name and its own value for each of `own`'s (another game hands its code a Math with its world's seeded random and Date as undefined; Silly Billy's own is `{}`). The shadowed names: window, self, globalThis, document, fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, EventSource, localStorage, sessionStorage, indexedDB, caches, navigator, location, history, postMessage, importScripts, Worker, SharedWorker, setTimeout, setInterval, requestAnimationFrame, queueMicrotask, alert, open, close, Function, performance, top, parent, frames, opener, Reflect, WebAssembly, Image, Audio, speechSynthesis, BroadcastChannel, MessageChannel, Notification (not eval or arguments: strict mode forbids binding them); the server's guard already refuses all of these, so this wall is for code saved before a change to the guard. A `Budget` counts the guard calls: a run starts with `start(turns, message)`, `spent` is what the run has taken (so a caller weighs a call or a run of calls by its turns), past `turns` it throws GuardError with that message, and a budget made with a clock (`{ ms, message }`) also throws once a run takes longer than `ms` (looked at every 1024 turns); one made with `null` counts turns only and never reads the clock. Silly Billy's `compileAction`, `compileModel` and `compileTick` compile against one budget with GUARD_MS (250 ms) that `resetGuard` starts at GUARD_LIMIT turns; compiled models and processes reset the guard themselves. Code that runs before its budget was started throws at its first turn. `describeError` gives "TypeError: Cannot read properties of null (reading 'x') (line 5)" with the line counted in the designer's own code (the offset found once by compiling a probe that throws on its first line).

## The dev server (`server/`, Node, dev only)

`plugin.ts`: `gameApi(game, apiModule)`, a Vite plugin for any game's API with `apply: 'serve'` (and not in vitest's mode 'test') whose `configureServer` adds middleware at `/<game>/api/` (`apiPath(game)`) and nothing at import time; it imports nothing of any game (it types the module by its own `ApiModule` interface: an `Api` class built from the deps `depsFromEnv(root, log, tell)` finds, with `handle(req, res)` and `close()`) and asks Vite's module runner for the API module (`server.ssrLoadModule`) on every request: a new module (a file the module uses changed) closes the old Api first (its CLI processes and Whisper stop) and builds a fresh one, and a module that does not load answers 500 and leaves the old Api alone. So loading `vite.config.ts` reads no file and starts no process, an edit to the engine does not restart the dev server, and an edit to the server's code takes effect on the next request. `vite.config.ts` imports it as `plugin.js`, which TypeScript resolves to `plugin.ts`, and calls `gameApi('silly-billy', '/silly-billy/src/server/api.ts')`. Endpoints as in `protocol.ts`: `GET status` (starts Whisper and the interpreter's processes on the first call), `POST hear` (starts Whisper if it is not up and waits up to 30 s for it), `POST interpret`, `POST invent` (NDJSON progress lines, then done or error), `GET library`, `POST library`, `POST forget`, `POST log`. Requests over 20 MB (hear) or 1 MB (others) are refused, and so is any request a browser sends for a page of another site (403: `Sec-Fetch-Site` other than same-origin or none, or an `Origin` whose host is not the request's `Host` or `:authority`; curl and tests send neither). Errors are JSON `{ error }` with 4xx/5xx, never a crash of the dev server. `api.ts` tidies a plan before the page sees it (`tidyPlan(plan, lang)`: an empty id is taken from the name, a new thing with neither is left out and logged as `interpret-broken`; a missing `nameFi` is the first Finnish call (`finnishName`) and a missing `heardFi` the `sayFi`; the lines and args lose em and en dashes; an arg that is the plan's answer or line in the other language is said in the game's ('Okay, I stop!' in a Finnish game is the plan's 'Okei, lopetan!'); an answer in pieces is asked once more in fresh sessions, 502 after two), guards each script's code for the page (`guardScripts`: `guarded`, or `refused` with the guard's reason; a script's id is tidied like a new action's, in its steps too, and a kind its code makes with `w.make` and nobody listed is a lost kind), meets the room's water (`meetRoomWater`: a script's args too; an item, thing or place arg 'pond', 'water' or 'lake' in a room with water is the spot in its middle, and a new thing that is the room's water is left out unless a step makes one: "heitä se lampeen" in the forest had made a new pond) and lists things the plan's steps use but nobody listed (`listLostKinds`, which reads every action's params, the item kinds (their Finnish names aside), the actor ids, the room's water and the scene's words (rooms, avatars' names and body types) from the context's own lines with `contextNames`; item and thing args skip those and `NO_THING` (ground, sky, water, me, everyone...), wear's and make's text `thing` skips slot words; a lost kind's `nameFi` is its English name until the designer gives one). Only a new thing with neither an id nor a name is logged as `interpret-broken`. `invent` takes a 'script' too: the designer's action prompt with a message that it runs once for these very things, may name them by id and may do the library's actions. `interpret` hands the CLI a signal that ends when the response closes (`stopped(res)`), as `invent` does. `hear` primes Whisper with the page's sentence, or the server's own for the language (`defaultPrime`), and logs what Whisper wrote (`raw`) when the words were changed after it.

`claude.ts`: finds the CLI (`CLAUDE_BIN`, the PATH, `~/.config/Claude/claude-code/<version>/<hash>/claude` newest first, `~/.vscode/extensions/anthropic.claude-code-*/resources/native-binary/claude` newest first); runs it from an empty temp directory with `-p --model <m> --tools "" --strict-mcp-config --setting-sources project --no-session-persistence --system-prompt <s> --json-schema <schema> --effort <effort> --output-format stream-json --input-format stream-json --verbose --include-partial-messages`; reads `structured_output` from the `result` event (or JSON in the text), streams text deltas for progress. The effort is the `Spec`'s (low, medium, high, xhigh or max, as the CLI takes them); Silly Billy's interpreter and designer both ask low. The interpreter uses a `Pool` of two `Session`s of one turn each: one process per command, the next one already started (with 12-turn sessions, one stray `room` in the first plan was copied by every later plan). Each invention is a process of its own (`once`), all in parallel. One retry on `error_max_structured_output_retries`. Timeouts: interpret 30 s, invent 90 s. Models from `SILLY_INTERPRET_MODEL` (default 'haiku') and `SILLY_INVENT_MODEL` (default 'opus').

`guard.ts`: `guard(made, code)` wraps the body in the function its caller names, a `MadeFunction`: `header`, one line with a name starting with `__` and the opening brace (any other header throws, as the game's mistake), and `allowed`, the plain JavaScript the code may use. Then `execute(plan)`: the room first, then a new avatar (`newActor` beside the others, by the interpreter's name unless someone has that id or goes by that name in either language, else the next free one of `NEW_NAMES[lang]`; 'hello' reaction, saved as an AvatarEntry), then each new item: a draft entry into the library (`code: null`, `rough`) at once and an invention job; each new action: a job, and its waiting steps make the actors think (a new action or thing loses the calls another one already answers to, by its verb endings too: 'hyppää' and 'hyppikää' stay the jump, 'kaada' and 'kaatakaa' the fell made first); each script: a job too (`kind: 'script'`), which first tests the interpreter's own guarded code (no thinking aloud for it, the answer still waits for the start) and asks the designer only when there was none or it failed, then waits for the plan's new things before the one-off action joins the library (never saved, its params from what its steps give it: a place, a number, a thing, else words), and leaves it when its last step ends (`forgetMoment`); a plan with a script places no draft (the script makes what it needs) and is not kept as a phrase; the steps go to the actors (`sim.give(who, steps, true)`); the answer (`say`/`sayFi`) in the world's language, bubble and voice alike, while the line of what was heard keeps both languages.
