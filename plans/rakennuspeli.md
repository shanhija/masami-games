# Digger Town (`rakennuspeli/`)

A 3D construction site game for a four-year-old. The brief is Sami's [`rakennuspeli/design.md`](../rakennuspeli/design.md); on 2026-11-07 Sami added: select a machine and direct it by talking Finnish. The contract every module builds against is [`game/model.ts`](../rakennuspeli/src/game/model.ts) (the state) and [`game/map.ts`](../rakennuspeli/src/game/map.ts) (the places). Read both before anything else here.

How the brief was read:

- "tee hahmot itse": every machine, animal and building is modelled in code, no asset files. The child also picks their own builder (animal and hat), which covers the other reading of the words.
- "käytä pienempiä llm-malleja": an instruction for how this is built (Sonnet and Haiku agents wherever they do). The game itself holds no LLM: it is static files under nginx with no key and no backend. Speech is understood by the small Finnish grammar below.
- Audience-visible words are simple English like every game here, but nothing needs reading: pictures, arrows, sound, a hint hand. Finnish shows only small, for the parent: the words to try on the pause card, the words the game heard beside their pictures, and the wanted machine's word after two misses.

## The game

**Digger Town.** The animals want a town in an empty valley, and Pip the hedgehog (site boss, yellow hard hat) has machines but nobody to drive them. The child picks a builder, hops from machine to machine, and does jobs: digging, carrying, pushing, rolling, mixing, lifting. Materials change on the way: sand becomes bricks and concrete, rock becomes gravel, logs become planks, gravel becomes road. New jobs keep coming forever: when the story ends, the animals keep asking.

Nothing can be lost, broken or failed. No lives, no timers that punish, no machine can tip over or get stuck. Everything digs anywhere; a child who only wants to dig holes and honk can do that forever, and the job waits.

### The story arc

Each chapter builds a landmark, ends in a party (the machines in view drive out, the camera goes there, the animals gather, the chapter's own show plays) and moves the day on (`DAY_OF`): morning, noon, afternoon, sunset, starry night. After the fireworks it is morning again and the jobs go on. A job's `brings` machines arrive when that job starts.

After the story, `nextMoreJob` in `jobs.ts` (seeded, never the same template twice in a row) makes jobs from: `house` on a free lot while one is left, using builds 4 to 6 (`setup` 3 bricks at the press's slots and 2 planks at the saw's; floor stamped ready; carry the bricks, lift 5 pieces; the build's `owner` moves in), `pile` (a sand heap appears on the main road: clear it), `hill` (scoop and tip sand where an animal wants it), `delivery` (`setup` 2 bricks: carry them to a ring), `logs` (lorry to forest and saw), `gravel` (rocks through the crusher). The asker is any animal but Pip.

### Scoring

- **Coins** ring all the time (`COINS` in the model): every bucketful, tip, pick and piece. They buy paint in the paint shop.
- **Stars**: each job gives 3 stars within its `par` seconds, 2 within twice that, otherwise 1. Never 0. In pro, one more star for a job done within `par`. A job's `par` is 2.5 times the tap bot's time for it (`PAR_FACTOR`), set from what the test measures.
- **Rank**: total stars earn a rank (`RANKS`: Helper, Builder, Big Builder, Boss, Golden Builder), shown as a small hard hat in the rank's colour beside the star counter, which bounces with a small burst when the rank changes, and by name in the pause card. The builder wears the hat colour the child picked on the title; only the end of the story changes it, to the golden one.
- The town itself is the real score: every job leaves something standing, and animals move in and wander about.

## Controls

There are three ways to drive and all of them work together.

### Easy (the default, one finger or one mouse button)

- **Tap or hold on the ground**: the machine drives there. Holding follows the finger. Letting go keeps going to the last point and stops there. A ring marks the point.
- **Tap a machine** (or its portrait along the bottom): the builder hops into it. The portrait the job needs pulses. When a step finishes and the next needs another machine, the builder hops by itself after 1.2 s, unless the child hopped away during the step. Only easy mode does this: `stepJobs` checks the mode when the hop is due, so switching to pro mid-step stops it too.
- **The big button** does the machine's job, and its picture shows what. Holding it repeats. A tap that goes down and up between two ticks is latched (`Hands.tapped`, set by `setAction`) and runs as one frame of press; a guarded step keeps its hold, so such a tap gets `uhuh` as a short press does, and an unguarded one counts each time. `Game.releaseInputs` calls `dropTap`, so a tap made but not yet ticked does not fire when play comes back (pause, hide, a new screen). Pressing again while the button is still down, up and down between two ticks, is not seen. It pulses when it is what the job wants. It is not on screen while nothing is asked: between jobs, under a card and during a party `actionFace` is null (`nothingToDo` in `hands.ts`). Two rules keep mashing harmless: after any hop the button does nothing until it has been let go and pressed again at least 0.6 s later (`Hands.blocked`); and while a step needs what a machine is carrying, dropping it outside that step's ring takes a 1 s hold, with a ring filling on the button (`Hands.heldFor`); a short press there gives `uhuh`. With no such step open, a press just does it.
- **The horn button** honks, always. Every machine has its own voice.
- **Crane**: a tap moves the hook over that point; a truck crane drives closer by itself if it is out of reach. The big button with an empty hook grabs what is within 5 m of the hook, or else the next thing the `lift` step needs if it is in reach, going there first. With something on the hook: if the child tapped a point since picking it up, it goes down there (and becomes the piece if within 2.5 m of a piece spot); otherwise it goes to the `lift` step's next open spot if in reach; otherwise it goes straight down. So a child who only presses still builds the house, and a child who taps chooses where things go. The pointer's ring is on the next thing while the hook is empty and on the next spot while it holds something.
- A machine that needs to line up does it by itself. A wheeled machine may turn on the spot at 2.5 rad/s while its hands line it up, a toy cheat nobody will mind. In a plant's ring the truck backs to `feedSpot` and then tips. A forklift finds a pallet within 5 m.
- **Friends**: a small animal (`FRIEND_OF`) in the cab brings a truck or the mixer alongside the digger when a scoop step needs it, and comes when called ("kippiauto, tule tänne"). When a step starts and its machine is more than 25 m from the step's place, that machine sets off there by itself at `pace` 2.5, with or without the child in it; a tap takes over at once.
- **Hints grow with `sim.idle`** (seconds without progress on the step: its count grew, or its machine came closer to the pointer). After 6 s the arrow bounces bigger and the button pulses harder; after 12 s a hand appears on the screen and shows the tap (on the ring, the button or the portrait). Pro mode has no hints (`hintTier` is 0). The job never forces a hop on a child who is playing.
- Keys for a child at a keyboard: arrows or WASD drive that way on the screen; Space or Enter is the big button; H the horn; 1 to 9 the machines; Esc or P pause; M mute; F full screen (in pro F is a lever, so full screen is the pause card's button).

### Pro (a switch in the pause card: mouse and keyboard, the harder way)

Every joint is under the child's hands, and the sim's easy hands stay out of the way. Nothing helps: no hints and no automatic hop (the portrait the job needs still pulses, and `hopTo`, 1 to 9 and spoken names work). The camera follows behind the machine (`C` cycles behind, high and in the cab). W/S or up/down drive and brake; A/D or left/right steer (a tracked machine pivots). The rest are `Levers`. Lever `a` turns the way the steering keys do on the three machines that swing: `proCmd(kind, levers, tap)` flips its sign for the digger, the tower crane and the truck crane, because their joint angles turn counter-clockwise seen from above, so E (positive) swings right as D steers right and Q swings left as A steers left. On the bed, blade, forks and chute `a` keeps its sign, and `b`, `c` and `d` are never flipped.

| digger | swing | boom | stick | bucket | |
| dumper | bed | | | | |
| dozer | blade | | | | |
| roller | | | | | stomp |
| mixer | chute (pours when out) | | | | |

Holding the left mouse button and moving works a (x) and b (y). The big button is on screen only where Space does something: `actionFace` is null in pro for the digger, dumper, dozer and mixer (and `actionReady` false), and the easy picture for the roller (stomp), forklift (pick, drop), tower and mobile (hook, unhook) and semi (unload). `stepPointer` sets `press` only when the face is not null, so the idle hint, the voice bot and the tap bot never aim at a button that is not there. A key held when the active machine changes (a tap, a hop, a number key) is ignored until it is let go and pressed again, and the mouse pulses drop (`Game.ignored`, `lastActive`); in easy there are no levers to hold, so it only records the machine. The pause card's key legend is `KEYS` and `LEVERS` in `Hud.tsx`: the active machine's rows first (the table above), then W A S D, left drag, wheel, right drag, 1-9, H, C, M, Esc. Physics are the same as in easy mode: a bucket below the surface digs and one tipped far enough spills, wherever it is. A tapped hook grabs what is within 1.5 m across and 1.2 m up or down. Hook or forks let go within 2.5 m of a piece spot to place it. Jobs count the same.

### Speech (Finnish, Sami's addition)

A microphone button on the HUD turns listening on and off. While on, the child can select a machine by naming it and direct it by talking, and the whole game can be played that way (the voice bot below proves it).

The browser does the hearing: the Web Speech API (`SpeechRecognition` or `webkitSpeechRecognition`), `lang = 'fi-FI'`, `continuous`, `interimResults`, `maxAlternatives = 5`. Measured on Chromium 156: `SpeechRecognition.available({ langs: ['fi-FI'], processLocally: true })` is `unavailable` (en-US is `downloadable`), so Finnish goes to the browser's speech service (Google in Chrome, Apple in Safari) and no on-device path is built. There is no key and no bill; it is free for the page but with no published quota. Listening starts only on a tap, and only in a play that is running on a page that shows: `Game.syncVoice` is the one rule (screen `play`, not paused, page visible) and every change of those calls it, so the mic sleeps on the title, in the paint shop, under the pause card and while the page is hidden, and comes back by itself with the play. A new game or the old town back goes through `toTitle`, which turns the voice off until the next tap. It turns itself off after 60 s with nothing accepted. The pause card says in one line for the parent where the voice goes, or what is wrong (below), and the README says it plainly. Firefox has no recognizer: the button does not show, and the pause card says why.

**What the parent is told** (`VOICE_TROUBLE` and `voiceLine` in `Hud.tsx`, by `Voice.errorName`; the mic button's own note is `Mic blocked` for `denied` and `Can't hear` for `broken`): `not-allowed` says the microphone is blocked and to allow it in the browser settings; `service-not-allowed` says talking to the game is off on this device and, on an iPad, to turn on Dictation; `language-not-supported` says the browser cannot hear Finnish; `network` says hearing needs the internet; `audio-capture` says no microphone was found; any other name says listening stopped and to tap the microphone again. `unsupported` says Chrome, Edge or Safari can hear. In every other state the line says where the voice goes. A refused automatic restart is no block: it goes quietly to `off` and shows nothing.

The sentence is understood by `game/speech.ts`, a pure parser with no model behind it, and acted on by `say` in the sim.

**Semantics** (`say(sim, spoken, sameBreath)`, in order):

1. `stop`: the active machine drops its goal. Done.
2. `help`: a `hint` event and the hand shows now.
3. `come` (with `who`, else the machine the job needs): a friend drives it next to the active machine; the driver stays. If `who` is the active machine and it was hopped into within the last 4 s (`lastHop`, the sentence came in two breaths), hop back first, then the friend comes. Done.
4. `who`: hop to it. A machine not yet come: `uhuh`, done.
5. `honk`: honk.
6. `to`: where to go. A place (from `PLACES`; `hole`, `house`, `there` from the job's pointer, and when the pointer's machine is not the active one, hop to it first) or a machine (drive beside it). With `work` and a place, the goal gets `thenPress` and step 8 is skipped ("kippaa kuoppaan"). With `work` and a machine ("kaada kippiautoon"): if that machine is in reach, press now; otherwise a friend brings it, and the child says it again. A place that means nothing now gives `uhuh`. A tower moves its hook there if within reach. Where the place is, by step: `there` and a tip ring stop within 1.5 m of the middle (a truck's tail is then inside the ring); `hole` for a dig step is the digger's park spot; `hole` for a push, or `road` for a roll or clear, is the marker of the pass the dozer or roller is on (it moves along the work); `house` for a crane is the next spot in the building. A machine already backing up to its hopper or form ignores a repeated "kippaa" instead of turning round. A `to` that names the machine being driven, with a `work` or a `who` in the clause (`toSelf` in `say.ts`), is skipped, so the clause's own move or work runs and the `ack` goes out; a bare `to` of itself gives `uhuh`.
7. `move`, only when there is no `to` (so "aja sinne" goes there): `forward` a goal 12 m ahead; `back` a `reverse` goal 6 m behind; `left`, `right` a goal 10 m that way on the screen (`viewYaw`); `around` a goal 8 m behind, nose first. `up` and `down` work the lift where a machine has one (dumper bed, dozer blade, forks, hook) and otherwise go 10 m up or down the screen. On a tower, `left`, `right`, `forward`, `back` move the hook 6 m.
8. `work` with no `to`: if the machine has a goal and `sameBreath` ("aja kuopalle ja kippaa"), it goes on the goal as `thenPress`. A load a step wants, with the machine out of that step's ring (the 1 s hold's case), goes to the ring first and is let go there; a mixer only has to be within 25 m of its form. A digger or forklift told to take while it is away from its marker goes to the marker and takes on arrival. Otherwise the goal is dropped and the job is done now, by this table (speech is deliberate, so it skips the 1 s hold and the block after a hop):

| Machine | `take` | `give` | `push` / `roll` |
|---|---|---|---|
| digger | press (the dig cycle moves on); with a machine to dump into ("kaiva hiekkaa kippiautoon") a second press queues for the lift | press if holding or filling the bucket, else `uhuh`; a `give` in the same breath as a dig queues behind it | press |
| dumper | press | press | press |
| dozer | blade up | blade down | blade down, go to the step's marker (else 12 m forward) |
| roller | stomp | stomp | go to the step's marker (else 12 m forward) and stomp |
| mixer | `uhuh` | press | press |
| forklift, tower, mobile | press if holding nothing, else `uhuh` | press if holding, else `uhuh` | press |
| semi | `uhuh` | press | `uhuh` |

Every accepted command gives an `ack` event: a tiny double toot from that machine. Words it could not use give `uhuh`.

**Matching words** (`speech.ts`):

1. Lower-case. Punctuation and `-` become spaces. Split into raw tokens.
2. Split into clauses at the raw tokens `ja`, `sitten`, `sit`, `sitte`. Each clause is one `Spoken`.
3. Fold a token for matching: `r` and `j` to `l`, `d` to `t`, `w` to `v`, then doubled letters to single. So a four-year-old's "lekka" is "rekka", "kaivuli" and "kaivuji" are "kaivuri", "tlukki" is "trukki", "jyjä" and "jylä" are "jyrä". Table entries are folded the same way when the table is built.
4. For each token in order, try:
   - **`raw` entries**: equal to the raw lower-case token, no folding. These win outright.
   - **A pair**: the token and the next one, joined and re-folded, as an exact or prefix match with no edit distance, against machine and place stems only, and only when the stem is longer than the first token alone (it really uses the second word). It catches compounds the recognizer splits ("kippi auto", "pusku traktori", "katu jyrä", "auto nosturi", "vesi torni"). Both tokens are used up.
   - **`stems`**: on the folded token. A prefix match is distance 0. A stem of 6 or more folded letters also matches within edit distance 1 of the token's first letters; 9 or more, within 2. Stems of 5 or fewer folded letters match only by prefix.
   - Precedence between candidates: lowest edit distance, then longest stem, then machine before verb before place.
5. When a clause names several places or machines (not as `who`), the last is `to`: the object comes first, where it goes last ("työnnä hiekka kuoppaan", "kaada hiekka kippiautoon"). A few everyday words never match fuzzily (`pelataan` is not `peruuta`).
6. A bare `auto` or `kone` directly after a machine word is absorbed into it, not a second machine ("rekka auto"). Other words are ignored.

**A machine word is `who`, except:** ending in the allative `-lle` it is `to` ("aja kaivurille"). Followed by a postposition (raw `luo`, `luokse`, `viereen`, `vierelle`, `lähelle`, `taakse`, `eteen`) it is `to`, and the postposition is used up ("aja kaivurin viereen"). In the illative (on the raw token: a doubled vowel then `n`: `kippiautoon`, `kaivuriin`, `rekkaan`, `jyrään`) it is `who` after a jump-in verb (raw `hyppää`, `hypätään`, `mene`, `mee`, `mennään`, `menen`, `vaihda`, `vaihdetaan`, `siirry`, `kiipeä`, `nouse`) and `to` otherwise; a jump-in verb used this way is not `forward`. A second machine word in a clause is `to` ("kippiauto aja kaivurille"), unless it names the clause's `who` again, or is that machine's own job as a verb: then it is dropped ("jyrä jyrää", "kaivuri kaivuri kaivuri", "betoniauto sekoittaa"). Adessive `-lla`, `-llä` is an ordinary `who` ("aja rekalla").

`ei` is a stop only when every token in the clause is a stop word ("ei", "ei ei ei", but not "ei kaiva"). `älä` is always a stop. A whole text that is only `ei` (`isBareNo`) is not at-once like `seis`: it waits for the stable-text rule below, so "ei se ole" is not a stop.

`parseBest(alternatives: string[])` returns the clauses of the recognizer's top alternative; only if it has no parts at all does it take the first alternative that has some.

**Hearing** (`game/listen.ts`, class `Voice`): takes the recognizer's constructor as an argument (null when the browser has none), so tests pass a fake. States: `unsupported`, `off`, `starting`, `on`, `denied` (the child's own tap was refused: error `not-allowed`, or a `start()` that threw `NotAllowedError` or `SecurityError`), `broken` (every other error code: network, audio-capture, service-not-allowed, language-not-supported, or the name of a `start()` that threw). `errorName` holds the code while `denied` or `broken` (`not-allowed` for denied) and is what the pause card writes its line from. `start(state, byChild)` takes the flag with no default: the mic button passes true, `resume`, a restart after `end` and a restart after the wait pass false. A start the child did not ask for that the browser refuses (a `not-allowed` error after the mic was `on` since the last tap, or a thrown `NotAllowedError` or `SecurityError`) goes quietly to `off` and the next tap starts again: only a child's own tap can prove the mic is blocked. `no-speech` and `aborted` are normal: it restarts by itself on `end` while wanted, at once after a session that lasted `RESTART_EVERY` (1 s) and after waiting out the second for a shorter one, so a recognizer that fails cannot spin (at most 7 starts in 5 s). `hearing` is true between `speechstart` and `speechend`, so the mic can show it hears. `turnOff()` ends wanting it (a new game).

**The commit rule.** `stop`, `honk` and `help` fire on any hypothesis, at once. Every other part fires when the result is final, or when an interim's text has not changed for 500 ms. So "kaivuri" reacts in about half a second, and "kippaa kuoppaan" is never cut off into tipping on the spot. For each result index, what has committed is a set of parts (`who:digger`, `work:take`, `to:sand`...); only new parts go on to `Game.heard(clauses, text)`. When `come` commits, `who` goes along with it even if already fired. The first clause from an utterance has `sameBreath` false, later ones true. **One utterance fires once.** A track is one utterance, keyed by the index of its first result that is not final; the non-final results from there on are joined into its text (alternative j from each result's j-th), and a final ends the track at that index. Parts that went are remembered by name in `recent`: the same part inside `REPEAT_AFTER` (1.5 s, the game clock) is not sent again, which covers a browser that cuts one sentence into several results or sends the list again under another index; naming another machine clears the memory of machine names, so "kaivuri, kippiauto, kaivuri" fires all three. `stop`, `honk` and `help` are exempt from that 1.5 s rule (two honks are two honks). The session's final text (`finalText`) is cut off the front of a longer result, so a browser that repeats earlier words does not repeat the command; a text equal to it stays whole, so a real repeat works. A bare `ei` is not at-once (see `isBareNo`). A result list that arrives from a lower `resultIndex` deletes only the non-final tracks at or above it, and the final ones stay, so a re-sent list after more than 1.5 s does not fire a final command twice. `recent` and `finalText` clear on every `start`.

While the mic is on, the game's music and engines duck to a third so the recognizer hears the child. The HUD shows what it understood for 2.5 s as pictures (machine, arrow, place or action), with the words themselves small beside them for the parent. A final result with words but no parts shows the words and a small "?"; two in a row also show the wanted machine's portrait with its Finnish word under it, for the parent to read out. A `?say=` switch and `window.game.hear(text)` feed the same path, so all of it runs without a microphone: nothing in a test or a screenshot needs one.

## The sim's rules

### Ground (`terrain.ts`)

- `createTerrain()` makes the valley from `map.ts`: 1.2 m of dirt under grass, the sand hill and the rock pile as loose stuff of their kind (`h * smooth(r, r * 0.35, d)`), the road beds (dirt, no turf, `bed` set), the ready roads (road surface) and the river (hard floor, no loose), the holes and the sand pit cut as bowls (turf gone). The heaps and bowls are gentler than `REPOSE`, so nothing moves until touched: a test proves settling has nothing to do at creation. Slight lumps in the height add life, under the repose slope too.
- `createTerrain()` makes the valley once and hands out copies of it, so a job that wants the untouched valley to measure against (`risenIn` in `jobs.ts`) pays a copy of a fraction of a millisecond, never a frame.
- `heightAt`, `looseAt`, `kindAt`: bilinear or nearest, safe beyond the edge (clamped).
- One point holds one kind of loose stuff. `spill` sets the kind of every point it touches to what landed: a thin spill turns the top kind, and a digger digging there gets that kind. `take` reports the kind of most of what came. No job checks a kind: any stuff counts, so a child's mess cannot jam anything.
- `take(t, x, z, r, volume)`: removes up to `volume` within `r`, weighted to the middle, never below the hard level; returns `{ amount, kind }`.
- `spill(t, x, z, r, volume, kind)`: the same in reverse.
- `bladePush(t, cx, cz, dirX, dirZ, width, depth, y)`: every point in the blade's strip above `y` gives its excess; all of it lands in the strip just ahead. Returns the volume moved. Settling then spreads the heap sideways.
- `rollOver(t, x, z, dirX, dirZ, width, dt)`: flattens what lies under it toward the local mean and raises `rolled` on road-bed points, whatever is on them. At 1 a point becomes road: its loose goes to `packed`, `hard` to `ROAD_Y`, surface road. So no heap or pit a child made can ever block a road. Returns how many points turned, for the coins and the `rolled` event.
- `settle(t, budget)`: walks the `settling` list; a point higher than a neighbour by more than `REPOSE * CELL` passes a quarter of the difference to it (never more than its loose stuff) and both stay on the list. A point with nothing to do leaves. Deterministic.
- **Every write goes through one helper**: it clears `turf` on any point that lost or gained stuff, and bumps the `version` of every chunk whose vertex range, grown by 1 point, contains that point (up to 4), so normals stay right across borders.
- Reading a zone: `surplusIn(t, zone, level)` (stuff standing above `level`) and `deficitIn(t, zone, level)` (room below it), both in cubic metres. Ordinary ground is all loose dirt, so counting loose stuff in a zone means nothing: these two are how jobs measure. `pondY(t, p: Pond)`: the water's height, from the pool's lowest point to its rim by `p.water`.
- **The law**: `loose * POINT_AREA` over all points, plus what machines hold, plus `packed`, stays the same to 1e-3 over minutes of random digging, dumping and pushing. A mixer holds `sand + concrete * MIXER_SAND`, so mixing changes nothing, and pouring on the ground spills that volume as loose `concrete`. The exceptions, each a named sink or source: plants (a hopper eats stuff, a batch makes stuff), forms (they eat concrete), and the world stamps.

### Machines (`machines.ts`)

- Every machine: `speed` eases toward `drive * top * pace` (2 s to full speed, quick to stop). A pivoting machine's yaw rate is `steer * pivot` (its two sides roll opposite ways on the spot); a wheeled one's is `speed / wheelbase * tan(steer * lock)`. `y`, `pitch`, `roll` come from the ground under its four corners, smoothed, with a limit of 20 degrees. A built bridge (`decks`) rides above the river.
- **A goal** (easy, speech, friends): a pivoting machine turns to it, then drives. A wheeled machine steers to it, slowing in hard turns; if it is behind and near it backs up, turning; inside its turning circle it backs away first. The lorry never backs up by itself: it loops. It stops within `goal.within`, turning to `goal.yaw` if there is one. `reverse` backs up to it, tail first. `thenPress` presses the button on arrival.
- **Collisions**: `MACHINE_SPECS[kind].body` circles against other machines, `OBSTACLES` and built things (a build's body is its `BUILD_HALF` box from its first piece on) push the moving machine out sideways, never stop it dead. The river is a soft wall unless a deck is under the machine. The world's edge is a soft wall at 47 m. A test drives every machine toward every place in the map from every other, with every build standing, and every one arrives.
- **Standing things and the route.** Machines plan round what stands. `Standing`, one record per `Sim` in a `WeakMap` (a clone makes its own), holds the placed builds and the river's boxes as flat `Float64Array`s and one 96 by 96 `Uint8Array` grid per body radius (`ROUTE_RADIUS`, from `bodyReach`: a few distinct ones). A grid is made by rasterising each thing's bounding box, with the cosine and sine taken once per thing. `stands` compares a numeric signature of the builds and decks with no allocation; a build's first piece, a move or a deck rebuilds, and each call that finds the standing things unchanged warms one more radius, so a rebuild never costs more than one grid. `standingGrid(s, own)` gives the grid. `route(s, m, g)` runs an A* over it with a typed-array binary heap, the other machines as a cell mask made once per plan and only when the straight run is blocked, and keeps its aim for 0.4 s. The search has no per-tick cap (Open work). `standing.test.ts` proves the grid equals a plain every-cell-against-every-thing grid for every radius and every state of the story.
- **No garbage in the hot loops.** `pushOut` works on module-level `Float64Array` scratch and indexed loops, `Math.sqrt` stands in for `Math.hypot` there, and `route` and `goalDrive` return scratch objects that the next call overwrites (nothing outside `machines.ts` keeps one). What remains is in `stepMachine`, `groundY`, `rollOver` and `forStrip` (Open work).
- **The digger's arm**: forward kinematics from `BOOM_ROOT_Z`, `BOOM_ROOT_Y`, `BOOM_LEN`, `STICK_LEN`, `BUCKET_LEN` and the joint ranges. A bucket tip below the surface and not full takes stuff (`take` with r 1.3 at a rate that fills the bucket in about 0.9 s of biting). When the bucket's opening points below level it spills, at a rate growing with how far, as a `Stream` down to whatever is under it: the dumper's bed (`BED_LEN` by `BED_WIDTH`, adds to its load, the overflow to the ground), the mixer's hopper (`HOPPER_R`, adds to `sand`, any stuff), a plant's hopper (any stuff), otherwise the ground.
- **The dumper's bed**: above `bed` 0.3 its load runs out at a rate growing with `bed`, as a `Stream` from `bedTail`. It lands in a plant's hopper when the tail is within `hopperR + 0.5`, otherwise on the ground by `spill` at r 1.4. Spill inside a `tip` step's ring counts toward its `need`.
- **The dozer**: going forward with the blade down it calls `bladePush` at `y` = its ride height, so a flat field stays flat and only what stands above the ground moves. The more it is pushing, the slower it goes, down to half speed and never less. `pushing` holds the volume for the sound. A `push` event every 0.5 s.
- **The roller**: `rollOver` under the drum at `ROLL_WIDTH`; `shake` raises the rate and makes a `stomp`.
- **The mixer**: at `sand >= 0.9 * MIXER_SAND` and `water` 1, `mixed` climbs over 3 s and then `concrete` is 1 with `sand` and `water` 0 (a `mixed` event). Standing in `WATER_RING` with sand in it fills `water` (the tower's `Stream`). The drum always turns, faster as it mixes. Pouring runs `concrete` down over 4 s; inside a shown form that form's `fill` rises (one batch fills any form), otherwise the ground gets loose `concrete`. A full form sets in 2.5 s (`setting`, then `hard`, its lot's surface becomes slab, a `set` event).
- **The lorry**: with `logs` under 1 standing in `FOREST_RING`, `logs` climbs to 1 over 2 s. The trailer follows by `dψ = speed / TRAILER_AXLE * sin(yaw - ψ)`, its fold limited to 1.2 rad. `trailerY`, `trailerPitch`, `trailerRoll` come from the ground under its hitch and axle.
- **The truck crane**: stows its boom while driving (see the constants); legs down before the hook moves.
- **Forklift and cranes**: a held thing's position is its holder's fork or hook point, every tick. Set down on open ground, a thing lands on the surface; set down in a ring it takes the `RING_SLOTS` offset nearest to where it was put down that nobody lies in and no machine stands in (`ringSlot`; `placeInRing` does the same for a restored carry). Within 2.5 m of the next open spot of a shown build that wants its kind, it becomes that piece: `gone`, `placed` plus one, a `place` event. A hook's sway eases by a spring, for the look only.
- A machine left in mid-sequence finishes it (the sim runs every machine's hands, not only the active one's), and stops.

### Hands (`hands.ts`, easy mode)

Sequences move joints through targets with rate limits: nothing teleports. The digger: `rest` with an empty bucket, press: pick a dig point (within the step's `dig` or `zone` if in reach, else the most raised ground in the front half of `DIG_MIN` to `DIG_MAX`, else straight ahead: it can dig anywhere) and go `reach` (turret turns, wrist to just over the surface by inverse kinematics), `bite` (draws in along the surface, curling, 0.9 s), `lift` to the carry pose, `hold`. Press in `hold`: pick where to open and `swing`, `open`, `close`, `rest`. Where: the receiver the step names (`Aims.into`: a scoop step's `into`, and `'ground'` for a `digOut`; `receiverOf` finds it) if it is in reach, and no other receiver is ever filled, so a truck standing nearer than the mixer cannot take the mixer's sand; when no step names one (`into` is null) the nearest receiver in reach; else 90 degrees aside, on the side clear of every receiver and away from the zone being dug. A press during a sequence is remembered once and runs when it ends. Holding the button runs it round and round.

Dumper: press: `turn` (in a plant's ring, back to `feedSpot`), `up` until empty (or 4.5 s), `down`. Forklift: press with nothing held: `seek` a pallet within 5 m (turning and creeping to it), `raise`; press holding: `lower`. Cranes: as in Controls; mobile: `drive` closer if out of reach (to `standFor`: 10 m from the piece on the crane's side, else the first turn round the piece, in steps of 30 degrees, that is dry ground inside the town, as a crane close to the pier has no side and the river is no place to stand), `legs` down, then the hook. A crane's `take` picks the nearest piece of the kind the lift step wants next within 5 m of the hook, else the step's own piece, else any nearest; its `give` with the right piece already held and the span's spot in reach puts it on that spot, as the big button does. Mixer: `turn`, `pour`, stop. Semi: press at the saw ring with logs: `unload`. Dozer: the blade is up only while the button is held; it drops going forward and lifts going backward. Roller: stomp. `proCmd(kind, levers, tap)` makes pro's lever `a` turn right for a positive value on the digger, tower and truck crane (see Pro). The big button's picture is `actionFace`, null where nothing is asked or Pro has no button there (see Controls).

### Jobs (`jobs.ts`)

- Story job data as in the arc above, from `map.ts`. A friend drives each `brings` machine from `ENTRY` to `HOME` at `pace` 2.5, honking (a `comes` event). The tower grows (`up`).
- A step's progress, pointer and view are functions of the sim. `stepPointer` (and so the HUD's marker, the portrait that pulses and the idle hint) is null between jobs, under any card and during a party, as `actionFace` is (`nothingToDo`). `stepView` dots: scoop `scoops`, digOut `scoops`, tip and feed 3 (a share of `need`), push and clear and roll 5 (a share), carry `count`, lift `pieces`, pour 3, others 1.
- `push`'s pointer goes behind the heap first (on the far side of the heap's middle from the hole), then to the hole, and back as long as the job is open, so a child following it pushes the right way. `roll`'s pointer is the nearest bed point not yet road. A `lift`'s pointer is described under Crane.
- **Friends keep the way clear.** All of it is read from state and runs from `stepJobs`. `partners` (a scoop or `digOut` step) calls `clearOut`: every machine the step does not use (a friend may send any but the child's, the tower and one already on its way) that stands within `KEEP_OUT` (7 m) of the digger's park spot drives home, or, when home is in the way too, aside along the line away from the digger (`asideOf`, past the edge by `ASIDE_PAST`). `tidy` does the same once a second for a push, clear, roll and lift step, with the step's own area as the way (the heap and hole, the zone, the road's bed, a build's ground and margin `BUILD_MARGIN`, not for the bridge). `sweep`, every `SWEEP_EVERY` (0.5 s), looks for a parked machine that lies in another machine, a plant's body or hopper legs, or a house with a piece in it by more than `OVERLAP_MAX` (0.3 m, `overlap.ts`): standing things first, then other machines, and a friend drives it out the way it is pushed, or the nearest turn of 45 degrees that is clear of the lots where houses are to come. Not swept: the child's machine, the tower, the truck crane and a machine already on its way. A delivery job removes the earlier delivery's pallets from the ring when it starts. The tap bot and the voice bot meet none of this: it is what keeps a child's mess from jamming a step.
- **Parties clear their show.** When the last step of a chapter's last job ends, `finishJob` sets `Sim.partyWait` and calls `clearParty` at once; the party, the next job and the card wait until no machine touches the party's view or is bound for it (`stepPartyWait`, at most `PARTY_WAIT_MAX` 12 s), so the show and the camera's frame are empty when it begins. The view is `partyView`: `PARTY_AREAS` in `map.ts` (`Area`, `inArea`: the road's strip for the van, Bunny's door, the bridge's lane, the playground and the pond, the hall and the crowd before it) plus `PARTY_FRAMES` in `jobs.ts`, the ground under the middle of the party camera's picture: the inner 70 percent, across and up, of the free area (`PARTY_MIDDLE` in `frame.test.ts`), as strips 6 m deep, each as wide as the screen shows there (the view is a trapezoid, wide at the far end of the screen), clipped to the town, over every spot of that middle (`groundAt` in `frame.ts`) at 1024 by 768, 768 by 1024, 1280 by 800 and 390 by 844 and every half second of the party, with the camera `partyFit` gives. The house party is also swept over Bunny's walk (0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 percent of it, from where she sets off to her door), because where she is when the party starts depends on the wait, and the frame depends on where she is. The edges of the picture are left out on purpose: a machine may stand at the edge of a party's picture, and the tower crane's mast stays (its jib swings away or fades). The game cannot import the scene, so the rows are a table made from a run of that projection, and `frame.test.ts` fails when a spot of the middle is in no row. If the story's timing, the party tilt or `PARTY_FILL` changes, the rows are made again from the test's own camera maths, and the house rows more than once, as they depend on the wait. `clearParty` moves every unlocked machine that is in the view (`inPartyView`: within `SHOW_ROOM` of the show, or touching the ground the camera shows, by any part of it) or whose goal is in it, the child's included: `blobsOf` counts the body circles, the lorry's trailer, the truck crane's four pads and its boom, and the tower's jib and counter jib. The pads stand where `Mobile.tsx` draws them, from the outrigger constants in `model.ts` (`OUTRIGGER_Z`, `BEAM_X0`, `SLIDE`, `PAD_OUT`, `PAD_R`, `padX(legs)`: stowed legs count as the body). A machine drives out to a point of `EXIT_GRID` (the town's ground in a 2 m grid, nearest first) outside the view and at least `showReach(m)` plus `SHOW_ROOM` (1 m) plus `SHOW_WITHIN` (1.5 m) from every area. The tiers, in order: that distance plus `PARK_ROOM` (6 m) and clear; that distance and clear; that distance anywhere; `SHOW_MARGIN` (3.5 m) and clear; `SHOW_MARGIN` anywhere; any point outside. Clear (`clearGround`) means the machine's body with a metre's room round it, at that point, touches no road bed, no lot (`LOT_RECTS`), nothing that stands (`footprints`: plants' bodies and legs, houses with their pieces) and no other machine, where it stands or where it was sent: each leaving machine records its spot (`sent`) so that the next one does not park on it. `clearParty` throws only if no point of the grid is outside the view. The truck crane stows as it drives (legs up, boom in). The tower crane cannot drive: its jib swings to the nearest of 48 angles that leaves the least of it in the view (`swingAway`, through the crane's own hands, its trolley staying), and a tower whose jib cannot leave the view however it turns does not hold the party back. No machine's home or park spot is in a show's area (`layout.test.ts`); the truck crane's home touches the bridge's frame, so it is driven out of view at that party and goes home with a later job.
- **Animals keep a body's distance from machines.** `ANIMAL_ROOM` (0.6 m) outside every machine's body circles. `loadRooms` reads the circles of the unlocked machines once a frame into a typed array (middle, radius with the room, the yaw's sine and cosine; a machine's fields cost a boxed number each read), and `pushOut`, `inRoom`, `detour` and `easeOut` only walk that array and answer in shared scratch objects, so a walking animal makes no garbage; `clearOfMachines(s, x, z)` is `loadRooms` plus `pushOut` and `sim.test.ts` proves it equals the plain algorithm over a played story. `stepAnimals` eases a ground animal in a machine's way out of it at her walking pace (`TROT`; not one waving, sliding or swimming), and walks one to the edge of a goal that lies inside a machine's room, except a door, which waits for the machine to move. A walker takes the least of `DETOURS` (0, then 0.6, 1.2, 1.8 radians to either side) that keeps her step out of every room, so a machine standing on her path is gone round and not walked into; the detour is local, with no search. A walker that has not got `PROGRESS` (1 m) from her mark for `STUCK_FOR` (1.5 s), as in a pocket of machines, walks straight on through them for `THROUGH_FOR` (1.2 s) and then tries the detours again. Her yaw turns toward her step at most `TURN_RATE` (6 rad/s), and she steps only as fast as she faces her way: full pace within about 37 degrees (`FACING_FULL`), none while she faces square across or away, so she never slides sideways. The animal who moves in has `ARRIVE_IN` (7 s) to reach her door and goes at the pace that brings her there, between `TROT` and `RUN` (8 m/s: `moveInPace`; further than 56 m she is late and still in before the party ends). A newly spawned one starts `TROT * ARRIVE_IN` (21 m) from her door, one who was already about keeps her place and hurries, so nobody jumps. At her window she stands `WINDOW_OUT` (0.4 m) out from the wall and `WINDOW_UP` (2.3 m) up, below the eave.
- **Pip's spare.** `spareSpot(s, step)`, for a lift step whose pieces ran out after the child waited 10 s: the tower crane's spare goes to the point of `PALLET_RING` (the middle and four points 1.6 m out) with the most room from free things, which is inside the trolley's range; the truck crane's goes 9 m (`SPARE_OUT`) from the turret at `craneStand(build)`, to either side or behind, the first spot that is dry and clear of what stands. It throws if the build has no stand or no spot is clear. A truck crane the child drove elsewhere still gets the spare at its stand, which pro can drive back to.
- **The dozer's pass.** `pushEnds` starts a pass behind the whole heap: the heap's `reach` (twice the root mean square distance of its sand from its middle, from `risenIn`, at most `MOST_REACH` 4 m) plus `BLADE_Z` plus `RUN_UP` (2.5 m) back from the middle along the line to the hole, and ends 0.8 m past the hole's middle, on the side away from the heap, so the blade meets the heap at its far edge and carries all of it. In a pass the blade stays up (`aimsOf`) until the dozer faces within `BLADE_FACING` (0.3 rad) of its goal, so it does not shove the heap sideways while it turns. `passSide`, shared by `pushEnds` and `clearEnds`, runs the first pass straight and the next two 1.6 m to either side, in turn. At the start of a pass `wayClear` holds the dozer for up to `WAY_WAIT` (4 s, `JobRun.holdFrom`) while another machine with a goal is within `WAY_ASIDE` (8 m) of the pass's line, as a friend driving home across it goes round the dozer and the blade would only push the heap aside. `sim.test.ts` starts the push from 16 dozer positions (8 angles, 8 m and 14 m away) at the first hole and the sandpit and fails when the longest takes 1.25 times the median or the shortest under half of it.
- **The dozer's heap.** `heapNear` counts everything put on the ground within `HEAP_RADIUS` of the hole (outside it) as the volume, but its middle, where the dozer waits and pushes from, is the sand in the previous tip step's ring plus `TIPPED_SPREAD` (1.5 m) while any lies there. Sand left beside another hole therefore does not pull the dozer off the truck's heap.
- `refill`: when the truck runs empty short of the need, or for `push` when too little stands within 14 m of the hole (`surplusIn`) and the truck is empty, the job goes back to that step and the bubble says so. A fresh `scoop` after a refill counts from zero.
- Between jobs: 2.5 s, then a `wish` event with the next animal's bubble.
- What steps leave, all read from state, none on a timer of the step's own: a `digOut` done stamps the lot level at -0.3 with its form shown, or carves the pool and starts `Pond.water` rising (3 s). A `watch` waits for the thing itself: `Form.hard`, `Pond.water` at 1, `Plant.phase` idle with its `made` count reached, `Semi.logs` at 1. Each takes 6 s at most; the camera looks, the horn works.
- A `push` done stamps the hole level with the road, in a puff.
- `applyDone(s, jobId)` stamps a finished job's end state, so a save restored by `storyDone` rebuilds the town without replaying it: builds' `placed`, forms, ponds, decks, roads, plants' `made`, `unlocked`, and every machine at `HOME`. The plants' outputs are rebuilt too: a scoop into a plant, a feed and an unload go through `feedPlant` (world.ts), which gives the plant the stuff and runs every batch at once, leaving the gravel heap, the brick pallets and the planks where a played batch leaves them (`dropBatch`, `pourGravel`); a carry moves the pallets into its ring (`placeInRing`) and throws if too few were made; a lift consumes the things it used and throws when a piece's thing is missing, except for a job that puts its own pieces out as `setup` (span, slide, hallTop). Loose heaps left about and dug-down hills are not restored.
- **Times.** `BOT_SECONDS` holds the tap bot's seconds for each story job, as `sim.test.ts` measures them (the test fails when a number goes stale): hole1 47, hole2 38, rollMain 23, street 15, floorHole 23, cement 47, bricks 54, walls 20, logs 22, roof 18, rocks 22, gravel 72, span 27, sandpit 70, slide 24, pond 23, hallFloor 99, hallBricks 50, hallWalls 25, hallTop 41. The par is `PAR_FACTOR` times that. The bot idles through each chapter's 3 s opening card, which is in those numbers.

### The shell: the save, the page and the voice (`game.ts`, `progress.ts`)

- **Keys.** `digger-town` is the save, `digger-town-old` the old town, `digger-town-broken` the copy of text that could not be read. A tuning visit (`?job`, `?story`, `?autoplay`, `?fresh`, `?all`) writes none of them; with no storage at all the game runs and remembers nothing, and says so once on the console.
- **Reading.** `parseSave(text, freshSeed)` returns `{ save, trouble }`, field by field: a missing field takes its default quietly (how a save from before a new field carries on), a bad value is mended (clamped where a clamp means something, else the default) and the good fields are kept, text that is not an object starts fresh. Each trouble warns once and sets `trouble`. `load` then copies the raw text under the broken key (`keepBroken`, in a try of its own, so a full or blocked storage cannot make the mended save be thrown away) and sets `Game.saveTrouble` only when the copy was written; the pause card says so.
- **The old town.** `startFresh` keeps the save it wipes under the old key when `hasProgress` (story, jobs after it, coins, stars or a bought paint) and keeps the current sound and music settings. `Game.oldTown` says one is kept. `readOld` mends it the same way and copies unreadable text to the broken key unless that key already holds a copy. `restoreOldTown` throws when none is kept; otherwise it swaps: a current save with progress becomes the old town (so the pause card offers it back), else the old key is removed, then the old save becomes current, with its sound and music, and the game goes to the title with the voice off.
- **When it is written.** On a setting or paint change, when a job or a chapter ends, when the story is told (`golden`), every `SAVE_EVERY` (10 s) of play when the coins or stars differ from what was written, and when the page hides or leaves (`pageHidden(true)` through `keepProgress`, from `visibilitychange` and `pagehide`; not on the title). The story position is the sim's, so it moves only when a job ends.
- **The page hiding.** `pageHidden` pauses only a play. The title and the paint shop carry on when the page returns; the sounds and the voice sleep whenever it is hidden. A play with no input for `IDLE_FOR` (120 s) pauses by itself, unless the bot plays.
- **The voice** listens by one rule, `syncVoice`: screen `play`, not paused, page visible. Called from `setPaused`, `pageHidden`, `openShop`, `closeShop`, `begin`, `toTitle` and the constructor.
- **Audio unlocking.** `Sounds.play` does nothing until the first `unlock`, which makes the context. `useGameInput` calls `unlock` on every key and `pointerdown` and, as capture listeners, on `pointerup`, `touchend` and `click`, because on iOS a touch's `pointerdown` is no gesture for audio and its `touchend` and `click` are. The context's `statechange` takes it back at once when the system suspends it or marks it `interrupted` (iOS does while the microphone holds the audio session, or for a call), if the page is not hidden (`Sounds.suspend` is how a hidden page stops it) and sound or music is on; `unlock` tries again on the next gesture if the browser refused. A running context is left alone.
- **Party sounds.** `soundsFor` gives the road's party the ice cream van's jingle (`iceCreamJingle`) and the clock's party the strike (`clockStrike`), both starting with the party's fanfare.

`spareLook` draws a hidden copy of a mesh with its shadow flags and, for an instanced mesh, an instanced copy of the same instances, until the mark is taken back; `giveColours` gives an instanced mesh one colour per instance once and ignores null. `scene/fade.test.ts`: one see-through copy per solid material, transparent with no depth write, glass skipped, drawn by the upload and taken back on release; a hidden depth-only twin per solid mesh, drawn by the upload, its instanced twin following the mesh's count; a mesh that came later is prepared and one that left gives its twin back; a mesh with several materials is refused; swapping shows each prepared mesh as its copy after its twin and gives it all back. `scene/animals.test.ts`: `walkInSize` is 1 for an animal not moving in and grows with her distance from her door, down to 1 near it. `scene/look.test.ts`: `fogRange` keeps the day's own fog for a near camera, starts behind the framed ground however far back the camera stands with the same band, clamps a level view and works at every time of day. `scene/pick.test.ts`: see Taps.
- What the React files have no test for (the HUD, `BigButton`, the page listeners, Stage's upload) is proved by scripts in a browser with screenshots, and by a real iPad, which nothing here replaces (Open work).

## The look

**Die-cast toys on a sunny building site.** Machines look like glossy die-cast metal toys: enamel paint with real reflections from a soft studio light, chunky rounded edges, big wheels, big cabs, short bodies, dark matte rubber, bright steel rods, hazard stripes and an amber beacon that turns when the machine works (`isWorking`). That keeps it apart from the other games: `chase` is toon-shaded, `castle-builder` flat-faceted, `limaukkopeli` jelly and foam. Every part is `MeshStandardMaterial` for the iPad. The ground is soft and matte with a fine grain, so the machines shine against it. One warm sun with long soft shadows, a sky fill, soft fog into the horizon, and big white clouds. The fog starts behind the ground the camera looks at, however far back it stands (`fogRange` in `look.ts`, from the distance along the view ray to the ground): at least `FOG_START` (0.55) of the day's `fogFar` away and `FOG_CLEAR` (1.3) times the camera's distance (capped at `fogFar`), with the day's band width, so night and sunset on a phone, where the camera stands twice as far back, are dark and never a flat fog colour. The day changes by `sunOf(day)` from `look.ts`: colour, direction (kept at least 20 degrees up, so shadows stay in their window), intensity, sky and a night factor.

Toy proportions, not a real machine's: a child reads each at a glance from the silhouette. Real details a child loves stay: moving hydraulic rams, tracks whose links run round, a ladder, mirrors, an exhaust that puffs, lights, the driver visible through the glass. Loads are visible: a mound in the bed or the bucket scaled by `amount`, sliding toward the tail as the bed rises; a mound in the mixer's hopper and two lamps on it for water and mixed.

The camera in easy mode looks north from the south at a fixed yaw, tilted about 50 degrees, so a tap and a direction always mean the same thing on the screen. It frames inside the free area (`free.ts`: the window less the HUD, below the bubble and the counters, above the portraits, left of the buttons, measured from the DOM on resize and HUD changes and never in a frame), and shifts the lens (`setViewOffset`) so what it looks at lands in the middle of that area. `easyGoal` (`frame.ts`, called by `Camera.tsx` with buffers it reuses, so a frame makes no arrays) frames the machine alone first, never drawn smaller than a tenth of the area's width for a 6 m truck and never showing less than `NARROWEST` (34 m) of ground across. The step's ring, with its four sides, joins it when `settle` finds a camera that holds both within `PULL_BACK_ENTER` (1.2) times the alone distance, and keeps it up to `PULL_BACK` (1.3): the two limits are a hysteresis, remembered in the buffers (`together`), so a machine driving near the limit does not make the camera pump in and out, and a ring that is more than 1 m from the last one, of another size or gone is a new ring that must enter below the lower limit again. So the machine is never drawn below about two thirds of its alone size (the sweep in `frame.test.ts` measures 0.644; with no limit it gave 0.398), and a ring that does not fit is left to the HUD's edge arrow, clamped to the free area by `onEdge` so it never lands on the bubble. The ring and the machine are framed together only up to `TOGETHER` (45 m) apart; beyond that it follows the machine. `settle` answers null when no distance up to its most fits and throws only for a number that is not finite, which no play makes: `easyGoal` falls back to the machine alone, so nothing the camera does in a frame throws and the screen is never blank. After every fit `pullIn` slides the middle until the machine's chest is inside 90% of the area, so the machine is in frame on the first frame after a hop however far the easing is behind; the easing time is the larger of the frame's real time and the sim time since the last frame (up to 3 s), so a fast-forward or a slow frame eases as if the time had passed. It pulls back for cranes (no limit for them), and looks at a `watch` step's place.

At a party the camera frames that chapter's points (`partyPoints`, fitted by `partyFit`: `PARTY_FILL` 0.95 of the free area, more than easy mode's 0.8, so the screen shows no more ground than it must, as that ground is kept clear of machines, and at least `NARROWEST_PARTY` 12 m wide, tilted `PARTY_TILT`: 60 degrees for the road, the house, the bridge and the playground, steeper than easy mode's 50, so the ground is not stretched far up the screen; and the animals at the show as focus points for the fade). The clock's tilt is 45 degrees, shallower than easy mode's, to raise the tower and the fireworks in the picture; its top still looks at the ground, with no sky or horizon. On the first frame of a party the camera cuts: it takes the party's tilt and lens shift at once instead of easing from the play view, which would sweep over the machines that just drove out. The road's frame follows the van to within `VAN_FOLLOW` (6 m) of where it stops. The house party frames Bunny from where she starts: her point is clamped to at most `BUNNY_AHEAD` (30 m) down the road from the door, and while she is more than `BUNNY_NEAR` (8 m) from it the storeys over the door fade out of the points (full at 24 m), so the camera starts wide and closes in as she runs home; while she is far she is drawn up to 2.4 times her size, down to her own at 6 m from the door (`walkInSize`), and her suitcase with her. Waving from her window she is `WAVE_SIZE` (2.4) times her size on a pink disc (`WaveHalo`, up to four, 1.5 m across, a plain hidden mesh that the upload compiles), so she reads white against the cream wall. The playground's frame is the three playthings plus `PLAY_SIDE` 1.1 m each side, 2.5 m in front and 1.5 behind, 3.5 m high: no wider than they need, so on a phone the animals are as large as the width allows. The fireworks are three rockets per sim `fireworks` event (every 1.5 s), launched 0, 0.5 and 1.0 s after it and rising 0.4 s, so a burst comes every half second: each a flash and about 60 small additive sparks (46 in the rocket's colour, 16 gold) that spread about 4 m, twinkle and fade within two seconds, on alternating sides of the hall inside `FIRE` (5.5 by 3 m, 9 to 13 m up) and under the sky band the camera shows (two points at `FIRE.high` + 1, `FIRE.spreadX` plus the sparks' reach to each side of the hall). The sparks are a particle shape of their own (`spark`, transparent, additive, no depth write) in the pool of 640. The party itself waits for the machines to drive out of its view (see Jobs).

Tall things in the way turn see-through (`fade.ts`, `useFade`): while the line from the camera to any focus point (the machine's middle, nose and tail, the ring's middle and four sides, a watched place, a party's landmark points) passes within a capsule of the thing, each of its materials is swapped for a blending copy with the same shader at opacity 0.25 and a white glow in proportion to the fade (`glowFor`, 0.5 at most), eased at 5 a second, so it reads as pale and not as a dark smudge; its shadow stays. Each solid mesh has a hidden depth-only twin child (instanced the way the mesh is, sharing its instance matrix and colour) that draws just before the see-through copy, so only the nearest surface shows: no inner surfaces, no double image. The copies and twins are prepared after every render of the faded thing (`prepareFade`, in an effect with no deps, so a repaint or a new driver gets them too) and the copies are registered with `spareLook`, so the upload compiles their programs before play and a fade adds none; a mesh that arrives later with a brand-new material gets its copy at once but compiles at its first fade. A mesh that has no copy yet stays solid. The twins add draw calls only while a thing is faded. The tower crane fades unless the child drives it outside a party, the truck crane fades the same way, and the water tower always. The clock party's two sky points are focus points, so a jib across the fireworks fades.

### Parts (`scene/parts.ts`)

The parts box every model uses (`scene/parts.ts`): one lit look, patched so that every vertex carries its finish (`aSurf`: roughness, metalness, glow and hazard stripes), and `GLASS` for cab glass and lamp covers. The lit look is three materials of one shader function, because three.js re-reads a material's program whenever consecutive objects drawn with it differ in being instanced or in carrying instance colours: `SOLID` for instanced shapes, `SOLID_TINTED` for instanced shapes with an instance colour (the lorry's logs), `SOLID_MESH` for plain meshes (`Static`, the beacon, loads, drivers, builds, the viewer). They share one program. The scenery has the same split (`PROP`, `PROP_MESH`). A part is a cached shape (`roundBox(w, h, d, r)` with at most 2 corner segments, `box`, `cylinder`, `sphere`, `shell`, `thickShell`, `arch`, `lathe`) with a finish: `enamel` (the machine's paint, as the vertex colour times the part's `tint`), `trim` and `plush` (fixed colours, glossy and matte), `rubber`, `steel`, `chrome`, `dark`, `hazard`, `lamp` (glows) or `glass`. `bake` merges a list of parts into one solid geometry and one glass geometry, and `Static` draws them as one mesh and one glass mesh, so a body is one or two draw calls. Bakes live in a module cache (`bakedParts(parts, paint)`: the same list and paint give the same geometries; `useBaked` and `holdBaked` count the mounted holders; `partsOf(key, make)` makes a stable list for a component that builds its parts from the numbers in its key, and throws when a key it already holds meets a different maker, since the second list would be ignored). `Static`, `Wheels`, `Treads`, `Rams` and `Beacon` bake through it, as do the mixer's drum, the trucks' bodies and the truck crane's beams and jacks. A sweep after each change disposes the bakes nobody holds except the newest idle one per list, and a bake made but never held (a render that was never committed) after two sweeps a second apart, so a remount of the play (a new game, back from the shop) re-bakes nothing and a repaint in the shop keeps only the newest paint's bake; a list passed afresh on every mount would leak one bake per mount, so callers pass module-level lists. Then `Wheels` (one instanced mesh per machine, fed `rollL`, `rollR` and `steer`), `Rams` (all of a machine's rams as one instanced mesh: a dark barrel of fixed length and a chrome rod sliding out of it, between moving points), `Treads` (instanced links running round a stadium path, read from a roll distance), `Beacon`, `mirror(at, side)` (a mirror's parts), `ExhaustPuff` and `Mound` (a load's heap). A model is a group per moving link, positioned from the state in `useFrame` through refs, never React state.

**A machine component is a pure function of `{ m: Machines[K]; paint: string; driver: DriverLook | { species: Species; hat: string } | null }`:** it never imports the terrain or the sim. What it holds on a hook or forks is drawn by `Things.tsx` from the thing's own position; falling stuff by `Effects.tsx` from `sim.streams`. `demoPose(m, t)` sweeps every joint through its range and sets loads, and `Viewer.tsx` (`?look=<machine>`) shows one machine on a turntable on it: **a screenshot per `?look=` is every machine's acceptance test.**

### Budget (an older iPad)

Measured by a `?stats` overlay of `gl.info.render.calls` and `triangles`, checked headless with all nine machines, 30 animals and a full town:

- At most 250 draw calls in the main pass and 400,000 triangles. Each machine at most 12 calls plus its driver; the machines' own counts are comments at the head of each machine file (digger 12, truck crane 12, mixer 11, dozer 9, dump truck 9, tower 8, forklift 8, lorry 8, roller 7), not constants. Measured by `?stats` with `?story=19&all` (a full town, all nine machines) after a 40 s settle: 203 calls and about 347,000 triangles in the main pass (87 percent of the triangle budget), 222 calls and 416,000 triangles with the shadows, and in the shadow pass 77 casting meshes with 141,000 triangles. The scenery's small things are low in triangles on purpose (a flower 13, a tuft 9, a reed 13, a fence section 36): that is the headroom. The far trees (about 16,000 triangles) and the bank stones (about 14,000) are the next place to save.
- An animal is at most 3 meshes, its parts merged with vertex colours and shared per species. Trees, plants' repeated parts and things use `InstancedMesh` per kind.
- Only two three.js lights: one directional sun and one hemisphere. Headlights, beacons and lit windows are emissive meshes or additive billboards, never lights. Particles: one global pool of 400 instances, emitting by `rate * dt` from every `Stream`, plus a tapered column mesh for a thick stream.
- `<Environment frames={1} resolution={64}>` with `Lightformer`s, mounted once in `Stage` outside the keyed screen; `scene.environmentIntensity` follows the day; non-machine materials keep `envMapIntensity` at 0.2 or less.
- The sun's shadow map is 1024 over about 48 m, following the camera's target, snapped to whole texels. Only machines, buildings, the near trees and the hut cast. Terrain, animals, plants, things, the fence, cones, barrels and the beacons do not cast. The canvas asks for `shadows="percentage"`, and `Sky` disposes its sun and hemisphere light on unmount, so a new game or a visit to the paint shop frees the shadow map.
- `dpr={[1, 1.25]}`, and drei's `PerformanceMonitor` drops to dpr 1 and 512 shadows below 40 fps.
- Every machine stays mounted from the start (a machine not yet come sits at `ENTRY`, hidden). Nothing is first seen mid-game: Stage's `Compiler` runs `gl.compileAsync` and then `uploadAll` (`preload.ts`: every hidden object shown, culling off, a mesh for each `spare()` geometry, one render into a zero-size scissor, everything put back) behind the title and again on every remount of the play (a new game, back from the shop), then `gl.info.reset()` so `?stats` does not count it. Also uploaded: a hidden copy of every mesh for each `spareLook` material (the fade's see-through copies, instanced the way the mesh is, because instancing is part of the program), and `giveColours(mesh)`, called in the ref callbacks of the particle pool, the thick-stream columns and the pond's rings, gives an instanced mesh its instance colour when it is made, so the instance-colour program is built in the upload and not on the first frame that sets a colour. In a full town (`?story=19&all&autoplay`) the geometries stay flat at 417 over 200 s of play and over shop round trips, nothing is left unsent, and the programs stay flat over the first 200 s. Every geometry a mesh swaps in during play is registered with `spare` or built at module load (the five mound shapes, the mixer's ten bulbs). `warmDrivers` (`creatures.ts`, called where `Animals` builds the crowd) makes every cab shape up front: the child's species and the friends', each in every hat colour and the golden hat, so `Driver` never bakes in play; `creature()` registers its body and legs with `spare`, so the upload sends every shape it makes.
- A material that is transparent and double-sided is drawn twice per render and bumps its `version` each time by default; the pond's ring and the marker's two transparent meshes set `forceSinglePass` so they draw once and do not look their program up every frame.

### Ground (`scene/Ground.tsx`)

- 36 chunks, each 33 by 33 vertices, sharing one Uint16 index buffer, each with a fixed bounding box (y from -2 to 8) set once. x and z never change.
- Position, normal and colour for vertex (ix, iz) always come from the `Terrain` arrays, never from the chunk's own buffer, so shared borders agree exactly. Normals are central differences of the height.
- A dirty queue sorted by distance from the camera's target rebuilds at most 4 chunks a frame (all at mount), comparing `terrain.version` with what it last drew. In place on the attributes.
- **Colour**: a soft point's colour is `LOOSE_COLOUR[kind]`; over it grass by `turf`, with the edge sharpened in the fragment shader against a world-space noise (`smoothstep` around 0.5), so grass ends in a crisp ragged line. A road-bed point goes from bare dirt to asphalt by `rolled`, sharpened the same way. `road`, `slab` and `riverbed` where a point has no loose stuff have their own colours. All on one patched `MeshStandardMaterial` (`onBeforeCompile`) with a fine tiling grain (a 256 px canvas noise at `uv = xz / 6`) and `envMapIntensity` about 0.15. Rock points get scattered instanced stones, shown while `kind` is rock and raised above the ground.
- Road centre dashes are instanced decals, shown where `rolled` passes 0.95.
- Beyond the 96 m terrain, a large flat apron in the grass colour with the river running on across it, and a ring of far hills, fogged, so no edge shows.
- Water: flat transparent planes (`depthWrite` false, drawn after the terrain) at `RIVER.waterY` and `pondY`, so the dug ground clips them by depth.

### Taps (`scene/pick.ts`)

No react-three-fiber events on scene objects: the canvas gets one `pointerdown`, one `pointermove` while down and one `pointerup`, tracking the first pointer. Pure functions: `pickRay(camera, ndcX, ndcY)`; `pickMachine(sim, ray)` tests each come machine but the active one against its `MACHINE_SPECS` body circles stood up to its `height`; `pickGround(terrain, ray)` marches the ray over the grid with a few bisection steps. The nearer hit wins; a ray into the sky is ignored. The ground point goes clamped to `setPoint`. Under 0.1 ms. HUD buttons stop their own events. `pick.test.ts` proves a ray at a heap's peak returns the peak.

## The HUD

Big, round, thumb-sized, sized by `min(vw, vh)` with limits, in the shared `Fredoka` font. White with an ink outline over cream cards, its own colours (safety yellow and asphalt).

- **Bubble**, top middle: the asker's face and the wish as pictures; under it the current step's `stepView` pictures and dots. It bumps when the step changes.
- **Top left**: stars and coins. Top right: pause, sound.
- **Bottom left**: the machines' portraits (those that have come), the active one raised, the wanted one pulsing with a small hand.
- **Bottom right**: the big action button with its picture and fill ring, the horn, and the microphone above them. The big button is not drawn when `actionFace` is null (between jobs, under a card, during a party, and in pro for the machines Space does nothing on); it is the component `BigButton`. The mic toggles on click (a press only shows it pressed, so a press never reaches the ground); it is hidden when `unsupported`, shows a small spinner when `starting`, glows when `on` and pulses harder while `hearing`, and shows a crossed-out mic and a note (`Mic blocked` or `Can't hear`) when `denied` or `broken`.
- **Heard**: beside the mic, for 2.5 s: what it understood as pictures, the words small.
- **Marker in the world**: a ring on the ground and a big bouncing arrow over the pointer. When the pointer is outside the free area (off the screen, or under the bubble or the buttons), an arrow on the free area's edge (`onEdge`, inset by half the arrow's width) points to it, so it never covers the bubble; the hint hand then stays hidden. The edge arrow and the hint hand show in easy mode only. The marker's transparent materials draw in one pass.
- **Hint hand**: taps on the ring, the button or the portrait after 12 idle seconds.
- **Cards never stop the job and never need a tap to go on**: a chapter's start is the top bubble growing for 3 s (who asks, the wish, bigger and bouncing, the middle of the screen kept clear). A chapter's end comes after its show (stars, Next!, going on by itself after 8 s). The End: golden hat, Next!, the same. A job's end is only stars flying up and a fanfare.
- **Pause card**: Keep going; Music; Sound; Easy or Pro, with the key legend in pro (`KEYS`, `LEVERS`: the active machine's rows first, then the common ones); Paint shop; Full screen (only where `canFullscreen`: the browser has `requestFullscreen`); More games (the directory); the Finnish words to try, in two short columns; the speech line for the parent (where the voice goes, what is wrong with the mic, or why there is no mic); the line "Part of the old save could not be read. A copy is kept." when `saveTrouble`; **Hold: new game** and, when `oldTown`, **Hold: old town back**. Both are `HoldButton`: a ring fills over 3 s while exactly one finger holds; a second finger, or any finger lifting, leaving or losing capture, empties it. Its other buttons act on `click`, so a scroll that starts on one does not press it. On a phone (under 760 px wide) the card is one column and scrolls.
- **Touch targets**: every button is at least 44 px (`--p` is clamped to 44 to 84 px, 48 to 84 px in portrait, where the portraits and the shelf are rows of five), and no two overlap, checked at 390 by 844 and 640 by 360.
- **Star flight**: a job's end draws the stars it earned, three, or four in pro (`Flight` draws `max(3, stars)` slots). The chapter and end cards show a number.
- **Title**: logo over the valley, machines working by themselves (the bot playing), one big Go!. The first time, the builder picker is on the same screen: six animals and six hats, one already picked, so one tap on Go! starts the game. **Paint shop**: the machine on a turntable, swatches (coins on the locked ones), and the builder again.

URL switches (read once; bad value shows on the page): `?job=<id>` (start at that story job, everything before stamped; nothing saved), `?story=<n>`, `?autoplay` (the bot plays), `?mode=pro`, `?fresh` (fresh save, unwritten), `?all` (every machine come, every paint open), `?say=<words>` (hear this at start), `?look=<machine>` (the viewer), `?stats` (the draw-call overlay). A visit with any switch leaves the game on `window.game`.

## Open work

- Whether the recognizer copes with a four-year-old in a noisy room is Sami's to try: no test here can measure it. If it does not: spoken Finnish replies, and a fuller understanding of sentences by a small model (needs a backend and a key, none exists for these games), are the next ideas.
- Spoken Finnish from the game (Pip reading the job aloud) is not built: the on-screen words stay English and pictures carry the meaning.
- On-device Finnish speech: switch it on when `SpeechRecognition.available` says so for `fi-FI`.

Left undone or decided against, each with its reason:

- **The route search has no per-tick cap.** A plan that stops after so many expansions and goes on next tick would need its search state outside the sim, which breaks cloning and determinism. Warm, the worst full search is about 2.5 ms in node (the p99 of a plan is about 4 ms, mostly cold), which is about 8 to 10 ms on an iPad by the usual factor of four; the first few hundred plans after load take up to 20 ms while the code warms up. If an iPad shows a hitch when a machine is first sent, a resumable plan kept as plain arrays on the machine is the next step.
- **Some garbage per tick remains**: the sim makes about 38 KB a tick in a full town with the tap bot (the animals' share is about 2 KB), mostly `stepMachine` (about 5), `ride`, `overlapDepth` and `groundY` (about 4 each), where doubles are boxed in calls to the `math.ts` helpers and `armPoints` and the per-machine step functions return small objects; `terrain.ts`'s `rollOver` and `forStrip` allocate too. `Math.hypot` is still used in `pickThing`, `ringSlot` and `setDownThing`, which run on events, not every tick. The render path's top allocator is three.js's `getParameters`, about 60 KB a frame, which nothing here can change.
- **Cab views are blocked for some machines in pro.** The dump truck's has the A-pillar and exhaust across the left third and the bed behind cannot be seen, so tipping from the cab is blind; the truck crane's shows the steering wheel and no boom or hook; the digger's is mostly boom and roof. The behind camera follows the machine's yaw, not the digger's swing, so after half a turn the arm points away from the camera. The behind and high views work for all of them.
- **Night is hard to read** from hallFloor to hallTop: the fog starts behind what the camera frames, so a phone shows the ground, but the road and the grass are close to the same navy, the ring is faint, the green roller and dozer are hard to pick out, and the ghost house is a pale-blue box that reads as a window. Raising the ambient light and an outline on machines and rings at night is the fix to try.
- **What only a real iPad can show**: whether sound unlocks (the capture listeners for `pointerup`, `touchend` and `click` have no test), the microphone on Safari and on Android (Android repeats and cuts results differently; the tracks are written for it from reports, not from a device), the hold button's lost pointer capture and the false case of `canFullscreen` on an iPhone, and speed (draw calls and triangles are counted, frame time on an iPad is not; the timings above are node's).
- **Every game in the repo has its own sound synth** (the `Synth` class, the bell, the retire and lead-in code), with many lines the same across `ruokapeli`, `pia-jump`, `rocket-factory` and this game. A shared module imported by all of them would remove it; this game only imports `mulberry32` from `chase`.
- **The speech matching has known edges.** The repeat rule keys on the part's name, so two different words for one part inside 1.5 s count as one; a separate later utterance that starts with the words of the session's last final text loses them ("kaiva", then "kaiva kippaa" as two utterances, loses the second "kaiva"); two clauses naming the same work ("kaivuri kaiva ja kippiauto kaiva") lose the second. A result list sent again from a lower index can make an interim track's command fire once more if it turns final after 1.5 s.
- **The sandpit job's bot time depends on where the machines start.** 70 s now: the scoop 30.6 s because the digger starts far away after the house party's clearing, the tip 10.7 s and the push 29 s. Its number follows how the machines are parked at the party before it, so a change to `clearParty`'s exits or to the parties' frames moves it; `sim.test.ts`'s bot-times test fails when it does, and the new number is the one to put in `BOT_SECONDS` after the cause is understood. The same holds for hole2, street and rocks.
- **Fading copies are made once per material.** `fade.ts` clones a material when a mesh is prepared, so a repaint or an emissive change while the thing is faded shows only after it is solid again; a mesh made after the upload with a brand-new material compiles its program at its first fade. No test reaches the React side of fading (`useFade`'s effects): it is proved by a script and screenshots, and the twins' extra passes are counted only while a thing is faded.
- **Animals avoid machines locally only.** `detour` takes the least turn that clears one step, with no memory and no search, so two machines side by side can form a pocket at an edge of which an animal waits until the stuck cheat walks her through (`STUCK_FOR` 1.5 s, then `THROUGH_FOR` 1.2 s). In a roomy pocket her wandering about inside counts as progress, so the cheat fires late: in the test's cup of seven machines she arrived at about 11.5 s of a 14 s party. Path-finding for animals, through the route grid, is the next step if it shows.
- **Late arrivals are tested for the starting crowd only.** An animal further than 56 m from her door (`RUN * ARRIVE_IN`) arrives after `ARRIVE_IN` and still before the party ends; the test covers every animal of the starting crowd at every house, not animals that have wandered.
- **The pull-back trade-off on small screens.** `PULL_BACK` 1.3 keeps the machine at about two thirds of its alone size at the price of the ring: when the ring and the machine are far apart on a small free area (640 by 360, 390 by 844) the ring is left to the HUD's edge arrow (849 of the sweep's samples, against 5271 framed together). A larger limit puts more rings in view and shrinks the machine (1.5 gave 0.56 of its alone size).
- **Bunny is small in the first second of the house party.** She starts about 27 m down the road from her door and the free area holds only that much road, so she is a small figure while she runs in (drawn up to 2.4 times her size while far), readable as a bunny with a case and not large. A closer start would need `moveIn` to put her nearer, which gives a shorter walk. The playground's frame is width-bound at 390: the strip of playthings sits in the upper half of the free area with grass below, since a bigger one would cut off the climber or the swing.
- **`PARTY_FRAMES` copies numbers from the scene.** `game` cannot import `scene`, so the ground each party camera shows is a table in `jobs.ts`, made from the camera's projection in `frame.test.ts`; when `scene/parties.ts`, the party tilt, `PARTY_FILL` or the story's timing change, the table has to be made again by hand (the house rows depend on where Bunny is when the party starts, which depends on the wait, so it takes more than one round), and the tests fail until it is.
- **The layout tests are chaotic.** Where the machines park after a party decides who is near whom for the next job, so a change anywhere in the sim's timing can show a 2.7 to 3.0 s overlap (a dumper in a lot, a forklift in a lot) that another seed of the same rule did not. If a layout test fails after a change elsewhere, look at who parks where first.
- **A crane parked on the road can pin a forklift.** A crane at z 27 held the forklift against it for 6 s in one tuning of the parties' clearing, so the forklift's routing round a parked crane is weak; `layout.test.ts`'s 2.5 s limit still holds.
- **The tap latch sees one press per tick.** A press while the button is already down, up and down between two ticks, is not seen; a quick tap is.
- **Fireworks, the wave halo and the walking-in size have no vitest for the React side** (`Fireworks`' scheduler, `WaveHalo`, the pose size in `Animals.tsx`): the numbers they use are tested, the look is proved by screenshots.
- **A `watch` step frames the watched place, not a machine more than 45 m away from it.** That is on purpose; the HUD's edge arrow is for the machine then.
- **The free area is one rectangle**, so it is cautious where only a corner holds a button (the gap in the middle of the bottom is not used).
- **The scenery trades looks for headroom**: flowers have flat six-sided heads and the fence is two-sided boards, so they read flat from the side. No paired close-ups against the fuller shapes were taken.
- **Shadow cost is counted, not timed**: 77 casters, 141,000 triangles; machine loads (`Mound`) and `Rams` cast, because they belong to the machines.
- **An unreadable save keeps one copy.** `digger-town-broken` is one slot: the current save's unreadable text replaces it at every load that finds one, and an old town's unreadable text is copied there only while the slot is empty. A parent who wants to rescue a save must do it before the next unreadable one replaces it.
- **`ActionFace` has `'none'`**, for a machine that has not come. It cannot be the active machine, so nothing shows it; the type and its icon stay.
- **A bake held late can be left behind.** When the sweep frees a bake nobody held, `holdBaked` registers the geometry its component renders again, so nothing throws. If another component baked the same list and paint in between, the late one holds that newer slot while it renders its own older geometry, which nothing then disposes: a bounded leak of one geometry, only after a commit more than a second late.
- **A truck crane's stand can fall back to wet ground.** `standFor` tries eight turns round a piece for dry ground inside the town and, when none is dry, uses the first, which can lie in the river; the crane then never gets in reach. No story or generated job has shown it.
