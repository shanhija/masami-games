# Masami games: instructions for Claude

Canonical project rules are the same as for Cursor: **`.cursor/rules/`** (plus root **`.cursorrules`** as a short Cursor pointer). **Read and follow those files** before substantive work; do not rely only on this summary.

**Load these paths** (principles; long examples stay in `docs/`):

- `.cursor/rules/maintain-canonical-rules.mdc`: keeping rules in sync; user corrections and redirects
- `.cursor/rules/coding-style.md`: languages, tooling, engineering defaults
- `.cursor/rules/plans.md`: where plan files live and how they evolve

Games for a five-year-old, one folder per game, all pages of the one Vite + React + TypeScript project at the repo root: the 3D ones on react-three-fiber, the 2D ones on the browser's canvas. The root `index.html` is the game directory: a tap on a card navigates to `<game>/index.html`, so only that game loads. Commands run at the root: `npm run dev` (port 3002), `npm run typecheck`, `npm test`, `npm run build`. `hosting/Dockerfile` builds the image that serves them (`README.md`, Hosting). The repo is public on GitHub: nothing of an employer's and no recording of the children goes in (`.cursor/rules/coding-style.md`, Public repo).

- **`chase/`**: the side-view cartoon police chase (see `chase/README.md`).
- **`foot-chase/`**: the two-player chase on foot (see `foot-chase/README.md`, brief in `foot-chase/design.md`).
- **`castle-builder/`**: the castle on a meadow island, seen from high up (see `castle-builder/README.md`, brief in `castle-builder/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/castle-builder.md`).
- **`pia-jump/`**: Pia Jump, a platformer that plays like Super Mario in true 2D on the canvas (see `pia-jump/README.md`, brief in `pia-jump/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/pia-jump.md`).
- **`cat-hat-builder/`**: Cat Hat Builder, drive a truck, make materials and build a house of themed rooms, side view on the canvas (see `cat-hat-builder/README.md`, brief in `cat-hat-builder/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/cat-hat-builder.md`).
- **`rocket-factory/`**: Rocket Factory, a kids' version of Factorio seen from straight above on the canvas: dig, place machine stickers, draw belts, make science, defend against the munchers, fill the rocket (see `rocket-factory/README.md`, brief in `rocket-factory/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/rocket-factory.md`).
- **`super-me/`**: Super Me, make your own person in a closet, then a platformer in Pia Jump's places where you build your way with planks, cubes, trampolines and ladders and find presents with new things to wear; it imports Pia Jump's physics constants, world art, camera, icons and synth (see `super-me/README.md`, brief in `super-me/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/super-me.md`).
- **`slime-guy/`**: Slime Guy, a 3D obstacle course over water on react-three-fiber: steer, jump and hide under the spinners, grow on slime balls, carry the lost baby slimes, bounce up the tower past the numbered circles and open the surprise box for new clothes (see `slime-guy/README.md`, brief in `slime-guy/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/slime-guy.md`).
- **`monster-lunch/`**: Monster Lunch, a course seen from above at a slant on the canvas: pick a burger or a croissant and its fillings, carry the plate round picking food off the ground past moving poles, water, colour gates and poop, then feed the dish from a hot air balloon to a monster on a rail track (see `monster-lunch/README.md`, the inventor's words in `monster-lunch/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/monster-lunch.md`).
- **`digger-town/`**: Digger Town, 3D on react-three-fiber for a four-year-old: nine toy machines that dig, push, tip, roll, mix and lift on a ground a child can really dig, a story of five chapters building a town for the animals and new jobs forever after, played by one finger and one big button, by speech in the player's language (the browser's recognizer and a small parser for Finnish and English, no LLM), or by mouse and keyboard (see `digger-town/README.md`, brief in `digger-town/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/digger-town.md`).
- **`silly-billy/`**: Silly Billy, 3D on react-three-fiber in Castle Builder's look: a room of avatars and things (a playground, a swimming pool, a forest); a child calls an avatar by name and says what to do, in Finnish or English by the pause card's setting, which every word shown, said and heard follows (the starting avatars are named in it); what is not known yet is understood and written as code by Opus through the Claude Code CLI from a dev-server plugin, tested in a sandbox and kept in `silly-billy/library/` for next time; avatars are made and dressed by voice too (see `silly-billy/README.md`, brief in `silly-billy/design.md`, decisions and open work in `plans/silly-billy.md`).
- **`magic-house/`**: Magic House, 3D on react-three-fiber in Silly Billy's look, for one to four children of about seven at one screen (key zones, touch pads, gamepads): a house of rooms nobody has seen before, each one screen with a theme, furniture and props that answer; the children work out what the room wants and do it together, then open presents to wear and dress up. Opus designs each new room as code through the Claude Code CLI from a dev-server plugin while they play the one before, a second call writes its look, bots in a page's worker prove it can be finished, and every kept room joins `magic-house/library/` (see `magic-house/README.md`, brief in `magic-house/design.md`, decisions and open work in `magic-house/plan.md`, which lies beside the brief).
- **`profiles/`**: not a game. The players of the gallery: it asks who is playing, makes and edits a player (an avatar built from parts, a name, the language by a flag, Start over for each game they have saved in, Remove player), and keeps every game's saves per player under `profile/<id>/<game>/<key>` in the browser (`profiles/src/profiles.ts`). It also holds what every game shares for the two languages (`lang.ts`, `voice.ts`) and the directory's list of games with their names in both (`directory.ts`). Silly Billy's library on the dev server is everyone's; only what its page keeps in the browser is per player.

A new game starts as a short brief at `<game>/design.md`, written by Sami in Finnish or English; the brief stays in the language it was written in, and every other file in the repo is English. Treat it as the spec, mirror an existing game's `index.html` and `src/` (a game has no package.json, Vite config or tsconfig of its own), name its folder in kebab-case after the game's English name, and invent the details the brief leaves open ("keksi lisää" is an invitation). **Every game plays in Finnish or English**, as the player's flag says: every word a child sees or hears is simple Finnish and simple English for a five-year-old, kept side by side as `{ en, fi }` in the game's `src/words.ts`, and picked by the `lang` its `main.tsx` gets (plans/finnish-and-english.md; a test in `profiles/` checks). The look is each game's own: share another game's camera, palette and shading only when the brief names it ("kuten chase") or Sami asks, and then by importing its modules, never copying them. **Every new game is added to the directory**: a card on the root `index.html` (its link and picture) and its folder, name, line and players in both languages in `profiles/src/directory.ts`, which `vite.config.ts` builds a page for. Its `main.tsx` calls `startPage('<folder>', lang)`. A game that keeps anything keeps it per player: its `main.tsx` gets the saves (and the language) from `enterGame('<folder>')`, its `Game` takes them, and its folder joins `SAVING_GAMES` in `profiles/src/profiles.ts`; no game touches `localStorage` itself (a test in `profiles/` checks).
